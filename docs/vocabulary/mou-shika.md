# <ruby>以外<rt>いがい</rt></ruby> vs. しか — <ruby>除外<rt>じょがい</rt></ruby>と<ruby>限定<rt>げんてい</rt></ruby>

## <ruby>彼<rt>かれ</rt></ruby><ruby>以外<rt>いがい</rt></ruby><ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>らない。 / <ruby>彼<rt>かれ</rt></ruby>しか<ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>らない。

---

## JLPT Level {#jlpt-level}

!!! abstract "JLPT Level — N4 Core / N3 Extended"
    Both patterns are mechanically N4: particle drop, simple attachment, and for しか, the mandatory negative verb. But the sociolinguistic calibration — when to use analytical <ruby>以外<rt>いがい</rt></ruby> versus emotional しか, how they overlap when both are paired with negatives, and how they fit into the broader "only" landscape with だけ and ばかり — pushes solidly into N3. This page maps the full exclusion/limitation terrain.

    | Tier | Pattern | Description | Example |
    |------|---------|-------------|---------|
    | N5 — Foundation | も (inclusive) | Also / even | <ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>も<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>く。 |
    | N5 — Foundation | だけ (neutral limit) | Only / just | これだけ。 |
    | **N4 — Core ← You are here** | **以外** | **Exclusion — "except / other than"** | **<ruby>水<rt>みず</rt></ruby><ruby>以外<rt>いがい</rt></ruby><ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>まない。** |
    | **N4 — Core ← You are here** | **しか + negative** | **Emotional limitation — "only"** | **<ruby>水<rt>みず</rt></ruby>しか<ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>めない。** |
    | N4 — Core | しかも | Conjunction — "moreover" (unrelated to しか) | しかも、<ruby>無料<rt>む料</rt></ruby>だ。 |
    | N3 — Extended | **以外 + negative** | **Objective "only" — "nothing other than"** | **<ruby>彼<rt>かれ</rt></ruby><ruby>以外<rt>いがい</rt></ruby><ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>らない。** |
    | N3 — Extended | **だけ vs. しか calibration** | **Neutral vs. emotional "only"** | **これだけある vs. これしかない。** |
    | N3 — Extended | **ばかり** | **Emphatic "only / just" (often complaint)** | **<ruby>寝<rt>ね</rt></ruby>てばかりいる。** |
    | N2 — Awareness | のみ | Formal written "only" | <ruby>会員<rt>かいいん</rt></ruby>のみ。 |

## Quick Summary {#quick-summary}

!!! tip "At a glance"
    - **<ruby>以外<rt>いがい</rt></ruby>** draws a **boundary**. It marks what sits outside the set of valid items. Everything else is fair game. It is **objective, analytical, and socially cool**. It works with **affirmative or negative** verbs.
    - **しか** draws a **wall**. It marks the single item that occupies the entire set. Nothing else exists or is permitted. It is **subjective, emotional, and often implies scarcity or disappointment**. It **requires a negative verb**.
    - Both drop the particle that would normally mark the noun: <ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>**は/が** → <ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby><ruby>以外<rt>いがい</rt></ruby> / <ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>しか.
    - When both are paired with negative verbs, English translates them identically as "only." But Japanese encodes a critical emotional difference: <ruby>以外<rt>いがい</rt></ruby> states the boundary of your knowledge; しか laments the poverty of your options.
    - **<ruby>以外<rt>いがい</rt></ruby>** can follow **verb phrases** (<ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>っている<ruby>以外<rt>いがい</rt></ruby>). **しか** cannot — it attaches only to nouns and quantifiers.
    - **しかない** has lexicalized into a set phrase meaning "have no choice but to," carrying necessity as well as limitation.

## Core Meaning {#core-meaning}

!!! note "Core Rule"
    Use <ruby>以外<rt>いがい</rt></ruby> when you want to exclude an item from a larger set and discuss what remains. Use しか when you want to express that a set contains exactly one item and that this limitation feels small, exclusive, or insufficient.

| <ruby>水<rt>みず</rt></ruby><ruby>以外<rt>いがい</rt></ruby><ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>まない。 | I don't drink anything other than water [objective exclusion]. |
|---|---|
| | *Context: Stating a dietary boundary. The speaker identifies water as the sole permitted item by excluding all others. The tone is matter-of-fact.* |

| <ruby>水<rt>みず</rt></ruby>しか<ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>めない。 | I can drink only water [emotional limitation]. |
|---|---|
| | *Context: Stating a physical restriction or scarcity. The speaker emphasizes that the available set contains exactly one item. The tone implies frustration, medical constraint, or hardship.* |

| <ruby>日曜日<rt>にちようび</rt></ruby><ruby>以外<rt>いがい</rt></ruby><ruby>働<rt>はたら</rt></ruby>く。 | I work except on Sundays [affirmative exclusion]. |
|---|---|
| | *Context: Describing a schedule. <ruby>以外<rt>いがい</rt></ruby> works with affirmative verbs to define the negative space (the exception).* |

| <ruby>日曜日<rt>にちようび</rt></ruby>しか<ruby>休<rt>やす</rt></ruby>みがない。 | I have only Sunday off [emotional scarcity]. |
|---|---|
| | *Context: Complaining about a brutal schedule. The grammar makes Sunday feel tiny and insufficient against the weight of the work week.* |

| <ruby>彼<rt>かれ</rt></ruby><ruby>以外<rt>いがい</rt></ruby><ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>らない。 | I don't know anyone other than him [objective "only"]. |
|---|---|
| | *Context: Answering a question about acquaintances. The speaker states the boundary of their knowledge without emotional coloring.* |

| <ruby>彼<rt>かれ</rt></ruby>しか<ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>らない。 | I know only him [emotional exclusivity]. |
|---|---|
| | *Context: Could be romantic ("he is my entire world") or could express isolation ("I have no other contacts"). The grammar packs emotional weight that <ruby>以外<rt>いがい</rt></ruby> cannot carry.* |

## The Exclusion Logic {#the-exclusion-logic}

Both grammar points manipulate sets, but they do so from opposite directions.

**<ruby>以外<rt>いがい</rt></ruby> — The Boundary Marker**


```

Set: All beverages
Excluded: Alcohol
Valid remainder: Everything that is not alcohol
Statement: お酒以外飲む。 = "I drink things other than alcohol."

```

**しか — The Sole Occupant**


```

Set: All beverages
Valid member: Water only
Invalid remainder: Everything else is negated
Statement: 水しか飲まない。 = "I drink nothing but water."

```

!!! note "The negative overlap zone"
    When <ruby>以外<rt>いがい</rt></ruby> is paired with a negative verb, the two patterns converge in English:
    - 水以外飲まない = "I don't drink anything except water" = "I only drink water."
    - 水しか飲まない = "I drink nothing but water" = "I only drink water."

    But in Japanese, the first is a **boundary statement** (I have drawn a line at water), while the second is a **quantity statement** (the amount of things I drink equals one). The emotional valence differs even when the logical outcome is identical.

## Deep Dive: <ruby>以外<rt>いがい</rt></ruby> {#deep-dive-igai}

### What It Attaches To

<ruby>以外<rt>いがい</rt></ruby> attaches directly to nouns with the particle dropped, or to entire verb phrases in plain form.

| Attachment | Example | Meaning |
|------------|---------|---------|
| Noun (particle dropped) | <ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby><ruby>以外<rt>いがい</rt></ruby> | other than me |
| Noun (particle dropped) | <ruby>日本<rt>にほん</rt></ruby><ruby>以外<rt>いがい</rt></ruby> | outside Japan / other than Japan |
| Noun (particle dropped) | これ<ruby>以外<rt>いがい</rt></ruby> | other than this |
| Verb phrase (plain) | <ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>ている<ruby>以外<rt>いがい</rt></ruby> | other than knowing |
| Verb phrase (plain) | <ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>く<ruby>以外<rt>いがい</rt></ruby> | other than going |
| Verb phrase (plain) | ある<ruby>以外<rt>いがい</rt></ruby> | other than existing |

### Predicate Freedom

Unlike しか, <ruby>以外<rt>いがい</rt></ruby> has **no verb restriction**. It works with affirmative, negative, and stative predicates.

| <ruby>日本語<rt>にほんご</rt></ruby><ruby>以外<rt>いがい</rt></ruby>も<ruby>勉強<rt>べんきょう</rt></ruby>する。 | I also study things other than Japanese [affirmative]. |
|---|---|
| | *Context: The speaker studies Japanese plus other subjects. <ruby>以外<rt>いがい</rt></ruby> defines what is NOT the sole focus.* |

| これ<ruby>以外<rt>いがい</rt></ruby><ruby>必要<rt>ひつよう</rt></ruby>ない。 | I don't need anything other than this [negative]. |
|---|---|
| | *Context: Objective inventory. The speaker identifies the boundary of necessity.* |

| <ruby>彼<rt>かれ</rt></ruby><ruby>以外<rt>いがい</rt></ruby><ruby>誰<rt>だれ</rt></ruby>も<ruby>来<rt>こ</rt></ruby>なかった。 | No one came other than him [negative with 誰も]. |
|---|---|
| | *Context: Headcount. The exclusion is factual.* |

### <ruby>以外<rt>いがい</rt></ruby> + の (Attributive)

<ruby>以外<rt>いがい</rt></ruby> can modify a following noun with の.

| <ruby>以外<rt>いがい</rt></ruby>の<ruby>方法<rt>ほうほう</rt></ruby> | a method other than this |
|---|---|
| <ruby>以外<rt>いがい</rt></ruby>の<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby> | people other than [the specified person] |
| <ruby>以外<rt>いがい</rt></ruby>の<ruby>選択肢<rt>せんたくし</rt></ruby> | options other than [the specified option] |

!!! note "以外に as adverb: 'unexpectedly'"
    <ruby>以外<rt>いがい</rt></ruby> can also become an adverb with に, meaning "unexpectedly" or "more than expected." This is a separate N3 usage.
    
    | <ruby>彼<rt>かれ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>以外<rt>いがい</rt></ruby>に<ruby>優<rt>やさ</rt></ruby>しい。 | He is unexpectedly kind. |
    |---|---|
    | | *Context: This 以外に is etymologically related but functionally distinct from the exclusion marker. Learners should treat it as a separate vocabulary item.* |

## Deep Dive: しか {#deep-dive-shika}

### What It Attaches To

しか attaches to nouns and quantifiers with the particle dropped. It cannot attach to verb phrases.

| Attachment | Example | Meaning |
|------------|---------|---------|
| Noun | <ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>しか | only me |
| Noun | これしか | only this |
| Noun | <ruby>彼<rt>かれ</rt></ruby>しか | only him |
| Quantifier | <ruby>一人<rt>ひとり</rt></ruby>しか | only one person |
| Quantifier | <ruby>一度<rt>いちど</rt></ruby>しか | only one time |
| Quantifier | <ruby>少<rt>すこ</rt></ruby>ししか | only a little |
| Quantifier | 100<ruby>円<rt>えん</rt></ruby>しか | only 100 yen |

### The Mandatory Negative

**しか requires a negative predicate.** This is absolute.

| Correct | これしかない。 | There is only this. |
|---------|---------------|---------------------|
| Correct | <ruby>彼<rt>かれ</rt></ruby>しか<ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>らない。 | I know only him. |
| Correct | <ruby>一度<rt>いちど</rt></ruby>しか<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>ったことがない。 | I have been only once. |
| **Incorrect** | **これしかある。** | Ungrammatical. |
| **Incorrect** | **彼しか知っている。** | Ungrammatical. |

!!! note "Why negative?"
    The logic is double-negative: "Only X [exists], and [everything else does] not." The negative verb negates the remainder of the set. これしかない literally means "Only this exists, [other things] do not exist."

### Emotional Nuance

しか is never emotionally neutral. It always carries one or more of these shades:

| Shade | Example | Context |
|-------|---------|---------|
| **Scarcity** | 100<ruby>円<rt>えん</rt></ruby>しかない。 | I only have 100 yen (and it's not enough). |
| **Exclusivity** | <ruby>彼<rt>かれ</rt></ruby>しか<ruby>信<rt>しん</rt></ruby>じられない。 | I can believe only him (he is my sole anchor). |
| **Disappointment** | <ruby>三人<rt>さんにん</rt></ruby>しか<ruby>来<rt>こ</rt></ruby>なかった。 | Only three came (I expected more). |
| **Necessity** | <ruby>謝<rt>あやま</rt></ruby>るしかない。 | I have no choice but to apologize. |
| **Habitual limitation** | <ruby>日本語<rt>にほんご</rt></ruby>しか<ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>せない。 | I can speak only Japanese (and this is a constraint). |

!!! note "しかない as necessity"
    When しかない follows a plain verb form (or a noun + しか), it becomes a set phrase meaning "have no choice but to" or "there is nothing to do but."
    
    | <ruby>謝<rt>あやま</rt></ruby>るしかない。 | I have no choice but to apologize. |
    |---|---|
    | | *Context: Not merely "I only apologize" but "apologizing is the sole remaining option." The grammar encodes inevitability.* |

## Head-to-Head Comparison {#head-to-head-comparison}

| Dimension | <ruby>以外<rt>いがい</rt></ruby> | しか |
|-----------|------|------|
| **Core logic** | Exclusion from a set | Sole occupant of a set |
| **English gloss** | except / other than / besides | only / nothing but |
| **Particle before** | Dropped (<ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby><ruby>以外<rt>いがい</rt></ruby>) | Dropped (<ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>しか) |
| **Verb requirement** | None — works with any verb | **Must be negative** |
| **Affirmative OK?** | Yes (<ruby>日曜日<rt>にちようび</rt></ruby><ruby>以外<rt>いがい</rt></ruby><ruby>働<rt>はたら</rt></ruby>く) | No — ungrammatical |
| **Follows verbs?** | Yes (<ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>ている<ruby>以外<rt>いがい</rt></ruby>) | No — nouns/quantifiers only |
| **Emotional tone** | Objective, analytical, cool | Subjective, emotional, warm |
| **Implication** | "This is the boundary" | "This is all there is" |
| **Scarcity nuance** | Absent | Present |
| **Exclusivity nuance** | Neutral | Strong |
| **Formal register** | Fully acceptable | Casual to standard; formal writing prefers のみ |

### The Critical Overlap: Both + Negative = "Only"

| | <ruby>以外<rt>いがい</rt></ruby> + negative | しか + negative |
|---|---|---|
| **Example** | これ<ruby>以外<rt>いがい</rt></ruby><ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>らない。 | これしか<ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>らない。 |
| **Literal** | I don't know things other than this. | I know nothing but this. |
| **Translation** | I only know this. | I only know this. |
| **Nuance** | Factual boundary of knowledge. | Emotional weight of limitation. |
| **Appropriate when** | Answering a survey, reporting facts. | Expressing loneliness, romance, frustration. |
| **Social risk** | Sounds clinical if used for feelings. | Sounds dramatic if used for inventory. |

!!! example "The same sentence, different temperatures"
    **Doctor to patient (dietary boundary):**
    > お<ruby>酒<rt>さけ</rt></ruby><ruby>以外<rt>いがい</rt></ruby><ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>んでも<ruby>大丈夫<rt>だいじょうぶ</rt></ruby>です。
    *"It's okay to drink things other than alcohol."* — Clinical, inclusive. The doctor excludes alcohol from the permitted set.

    **Patient to friend (complaining about restriction):**
    > <ruby>水<rt>みず</rt></ruby>しか<ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>めないよ。
    *"I can only drink water."* — The grammar encodes the frustration of a limited menu imposed by health constraints.

    **Person reading a menu (objective observation):**
    > <ruby>水<rt>みず</rt></ruby><ruby>以外<rt>いがい</rt></ruby>ない。
    *"There's nothing other than water."* — Factual inventory. The speaker notes the boundary of available options.

    **Person on desert island (scarcity):**
    > <ruby>水<rt>みず</rt></ruby>しかない。
    *"There's only water."* — Scarcity, limitation, resignation. The grammar makes the single option feel insufficient.

## In Real Life {#in-real-life}

!!! example "In Real Life — Objective Boundary vs. Emotional Limitation"
    **Situation:** Two colleagues are discussing a project budget. タカシ is the accountant; ユミ is the designer. They look at the remaining funds.

    > タカシ: <ruby>デザイン費<rt>でざいんひ</rt></ruby><ruby>以外<rt>いがい</rt></ruby>はまだ<ruby>余裕<rt>よゆう</rt></ruby>があるよ。
    *"Other than the design fee, we still have room."*

    > ユミ: え、<ruby>デザイン費<rt>でざいんひ</rt></ruby>しか<ruby>残<rt>のこ</rt></ruby>ってないの？
    *"Huh, only the design fee is left?"*

    **Why 以外 fits Takashi:** He is drawing a boundary. The design fee is one category; other categories are fine. His tone is analytical.

    **Why しка fits Yumi:** She hears that the design fee is the sole remaining item and panics. Her grammar encodes the emotional shock of scarcity. She does not say デザイン費以外残ってないの — that would be a factual question. しか makes it an exclamation of alarm.

!!! example "In Real Life — Schedule Exclusion vs. Scarcity"
    **Situation:** ケン is a freelancer explaining his availability to a client.

    > <ruby>月曜日<rt>げつようび</rt></ruby><ruby>以外<rt>いがい</rt></ruby>ならいつでも<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>けます。
    *"Any day except Monday works for me."*

    **Why 以外 fits:** Ken is offering a wide set of options with one exclusion. The tone is accommodating and professional.

    **If Ken were desperate:**
    > <ruby>月曜日<rt>げつようび</rt></ruby>しか<ruby>空<rt>あ</rt></ruby>いてません。
    *"Only Monday is open."* — This would imply his schedule is packed and he is constrained, not generous.

!!! example "In Real Life — Romantic Exclusivity"
    **Situation:** Two friends are gossiping about a mutual acquaintance who never dates.

    > <ruby>彼女<rt>かのじょ</rt></ruby>、<ruby>彼<rt>かれ</rt></ruby><ruby>以外<rt>いがい</rt></ruby><ruby>男<rt>おとこ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby><ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>らないんじゃない？
    *"Don't you think she doesn't know any men other than him?"* — Observational, slightly clinical.

    **If the same speaker were confessing:**
    > <ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></rubybk>、<ruby>君<rt>きみ</rt></ruby>しか<ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>らない。
    *"I know only you."* — Romantic, exclusive, emotionally charged.

    **The 以外 version would sound like:**
    > <ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>、<ruby>君<rt>きみ</rt></ruby><ruby>以外<rt>いがい</rt></ruby><ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>らない。
    *"I don't know anyone other than you."* — Grammatically correct, but it sounds like a census report of the speaker's social circle. しか makes it a love declaration.

## Grammar Analysis {#grammar-analysis}

### Function in Context

- <ruby>水<rt>みず</rt></ruby><ruby>以外<rt>いがい</rt></ruby><ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>まない: 水 is the excluded item. <ruby>以外<rt>いがい</rt></ruby> marks the boundary. <ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>まない negates everything beyond the boundary. The grammar is: [Excluded item] + <ruby>以外<rt>いがい</rt></ruby> + [Negative predicate covering the remainder].
- <ruby>水<rt>みず</rt></ruby>しか<ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>めない: 水 is the sole valid item. しか marks the limit. <ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>めない negates the existence of other options. The grammar is: [Sole item] + しか + [Negative predicate negating the rest].
- <ruby>彼<rt>かれ</rt></ruby><ruby>以外<rt>いがい</rt></ruby><ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>らない: 彼 is the boundary of knowledge. The speaker does not claim to know only him emotionally; they simply report that their knowledge does not extend past him.
- <ruby>彼<rt>かれ</rt></ruby>しか<ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>らない: 彼 is the entire set of known people. The speaker implies that this set feels small, whether romantically or socially.

### Construction {#construction}

#### <ruby>以外<rt>いがい</rt></ruby>

| Preceding Element | Pattern | Example | Notes |
|-------------------|---------|---------|-------|
| Noun (particle dropped) | Noun + <ruby>以外<rt>いがい</rt></ruby> | <ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby><ruby>以外<rt>いがい</rt></ruby> | Standard |
| Noun + の + Noun | Noun + <ruby>以外<rt>いがい</rt></ruby> + の + Noun | <ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby><ruby>以外<rt>いがい</rt></ruby>の<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby> | Attributive |
| Verb (plain) | Verb + <ruby>以外<rt>いがい</rt></ruby> | <ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>く<ruby>以外<rt>いがい</rt></ruby> | Verb phrase exclusion |
| Adjective (plain) | Adj + <ruby>以外<rt>いがい</rt></ruby> | <ruby>難<rt>むずか</rt></ruby>しい<ruby>以外<rt>いがい</rt></ruby> | Rare but possible |
| <ruby>以外<rt>いがい</rt></ruby> + particle | <ruby>以外<rt>いがい</rt></ruby> + は / が / も | <ruby>以外<rt>いがい</rt></ruby>は / <ruby>以外<rt>いがい</rt></ruby>も | Post-position particles |

#### しか

| Preceding Element | Pattern | Example | Notes |
|-------------------|---------|---------|-------|
| Noun (particle dropped) | Noun + しか | <ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>しか | Standard |
| Quantifier | Number + しか | <ruby>一人<rt>ひとり</rt></ruby>しか | Implies smallness |
| Quantifier | Amount + しか | <ruby>少<rt>すこ</rt></ruby>ししか | Implies insufficiency |
| しか + negative verb | しか + V-neg | しかない | Mandatory negative |
| Plain verb + しか | V-plain + しか | <ruby>謝<rt>あやま</rt></ruby>るしか | Set phrase for necessity |

!!! warning "Do not keep particles before しか or 以外"
    !!! failure "Incorrect"
        <ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>**が**しか<ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>らない。
        <ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>**は**<ruby>以外<rt>いがい</rt></ruby><ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>かない。
    !!! success "Correct"
        <ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>しか<ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>らない。
        <ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby><ruby>以外<rt>いがい</rt></ruby><ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>かない。

    ~Both grammar points demand particle drop. Keeping particles is a direct transfer error from English "only me" where "me" retains its object feel.~

!!! warning "Do not use しか with affirmative verbs"
    !!! failure "Incorrect"
        これしかある。
    !!! success "Correct"
        これしかない。

    ~The affirmative verb fails to negate the remainder of the set, breaking the grammatical contract of しか.~

## Japanese Mindset {#japanese-mindset}

The <ruby>以外<rt>いがい</rt></ruby>/しか split reveals how Japanese grammar encodes **objective social boundaries versus subjective emotional containers**.

- **<ruby>以外<rt>いがい</rt></ruby> as social rule-making.** When a sign says <ruby>関係者<rt>かんけいしゃ</rt></ruby><ruby>以外<rt>いがい</rt></ruby><ruby>立入禁止<rt>たちいりきんし</rt></ruby> ("Entry prohibited except for authorized personnel"), it is drawing a clean legal boundary. The grammar is the language of institutions, schedules, and categories.
- **しか as emotional accounting.** When someone says 100<ruby>円<rt>えん</rt></ruby>しかない, they are not just reporting their wallet contents — they are expressing that the amount is insufficient for their needs. The grammar does the complaining.
- **The negative requirement of しか as cultural preference.** By forcing the speaker to use a negative verb, Japanese ensures that "only" statements are framed as **absence** rather than **presence**. Saying "I have only 100 yen" requires saying "I do not have more than 100 yen." The grammar makes the speaker acknowledge the missing remainder.
- **Exclusivity as intimacy.** <ruby>君<rt>きみ</rt></ruby>しか<ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>らない does not merely mean "I know only you" — it means "You occupy the entire space of my knowledge, and that space is therefore complete." The limitation becomes a romantic virtue. <ruby>以外<rt>いがい</rt></ruby> cannot perform this emotional alchemy.

## Nuance Comparison {#nuance-comparison}

| Grammar Point | Core Meaning | Emotional Register | Verb Requirement | Typical Context | JLPT |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| <ruby>以外<rt>いがい</rt></ruby> | Except / other than | Neutral, objective | Any verb | Schedules, rules, categories | N4 |
| しか + negative | Only / nothing but | Emotional, limited | **Must be negative** | Scarcity, complaint, romance | N4 |
| だけ | Only / just | Neutral, limiting | Any verb | Simple restriction, inventory | N5 |
| ばかり | Only / just / continually | Complaining, excessive | Any verb (often + いる) | Habits, complaints | N3 |
| のみ | Only | Formal, written | Any verb | Signs, legal text, academic | N2 |
| <ruby>以外<rt>いがい</rt></ruby> + negative | Nothing other than / only | Objective "only" | Negative | Factual restriction | N3 |

**<ruby>以外<rt>いがい</rt></ruby> vs. だけ:**
- これだけ<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べる = "I eat only this much" (neutral, measuring).
- これ<ruby>以外<rt>いがい</rt></ruby><ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べない = "I don't eat things other than this" (boundary).
- これしか<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べない = "I eat nothing but this" (emotional limitation).

**しか vs. ばかり:**
- <ruby>寝<rt>ね</rt></ruby>てばかりいる = "does nothing but sleep" (complaint about excessive habit).
- <ruby>寝<rt>ね</rt></ruby>てしかいない = Ungrammatical/unnatural. しか does not attach to verb stems in this way; ばかり does.

**<ruby>以外<rt>いがい</rt></ruby> + negative vs. しか:**
- <ruby>彼<rt>かれ</rt></ruby><ruby>以外<rt>いがい</rt></ruby><ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>らない = "I don't know anyone other than him" (factual).
- <ruby>彼<rt>かれ</rt></ruby>しか<ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>らない = "I know only him" (emotional).
- In a police interview, use <ruby>以外<rt>いがい</rt></ruby>. In a love letter, use しか.

## Register and Formality {#register-and-formality}

=== ":material-chat-outline: Casual"
    <ruby>金<rt>かね</rt></ruby>100<ruby>円<rt>えん</rt></ruby>しかねえよ。

    *"I only got 100 yen, man."*

    **Shift:** ねえ is casual negative of ない. よ is assertive. The しか amplifies the broke-college-student vibe.

=== ":material-chat-outline: Casual (boundary)"
    <ruby>月曜<rt>げつよう</rt></ruby><ruby>以外<rt>いがい</rt></ruby><ruby>都合<rt>つごう</rt></ruby>つくよ。

    *"Any day except Monday works for me."*

    **Shift:** <ruby>都合<rt>つごう</rt></ruby>つく is casual for <ruby>都合<rt>つごう</rt></ruby>がつく. <ruby>以外<rt>いがい</rt></ruby> drops the particle and keeps the rhythm light.

=== ":material-tie: Basic Polite"
    <ruby>水<rt>みず</rt></ruby><ruby>以外<rt>いがい</rt></ruby>は<ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>んでおりません。

    *"I do not drink anything other than water."*

    **Shift:** <ruby>以外<rt>いがい</rt></ruby>は keeps the topic marker for clarity. <ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>んでおります is polite progressive. The tone is a calm medical report.

=== ":material-tie: Basic Polite (limitation)"
    <ruby>水<rt>みず</rt></ruby>しか<ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>めません。

    *"I can drink only water."*

    **Shift:** <ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>めません is polite potential-negative. しか carries the limitation even through polite grammar.

=== ":material-briefcase-outline: Extra Polite (written)"
    <ruby>関係者<rt>かんけいしゃ</rt></ruby><ruby>以外<rt>いがい</rt></ruby><ruby>立入禁止<rt>たちいりきんし</rt></ruby>。

    *"Entry prohibited except for authorized personnel."*

    **Shift:** Classic sign grammar. <ruby>以外<rt>いがい</rt></ruby> defines the legal boundary. No particles, no verbs — just the exclusion marker and the prohibition.

=== ":material-briefcase-outline: Extra Polite (formal alternative)"
    <ruby>会員<rt>かいいん</rt></ruby>のみご<ruby>利用<rt>りよう</rt></ruby>いただけます。

    *"Available for members only."*

    **Shift:** のみ replaces しか in formal writing. しか would sound too casual for a luxury service or legal notice.

## Common Mistakes {#common-mistakes}

### 1. Using しか with affirmative verbs

!!! failure "Polarity error"
    これしかある。

    ~しか requires a negative verb to negate the remainder of the set. The affirmative verb leaves the grammatical contract unfulfilled.~

!!! success "Correct"
    ...
    これしかない。

### 2. Keeping particles before しか or 以外

!!! failure "Particle retention error"
    <ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>**が**しか<ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>らない。
    <ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>**は**<ruby>以外<rt>いがい</rt></ruby><ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>かない。

    ~Both grammar points demand particle drop. The noun must sit directly against the grammar marker.~

!!! success "Correct"
    <ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>しか<ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>らない。
    <ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby><ruby>以外<rt>いがい</rt></ruby><ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>かない。

### 3. Confusing しかも and しか

!!! failure "Vocabulary error"
    しかも<ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>らない。

    ~しかも means "moreover / furthermore." It is a conjunction, not a limitation marker. It has no grammatical relationship to しか.~

!!! success "Correct"
    しか<ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>らない。 (I only know.)
    しかも、<ruby>彼<rt>かれ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>らない。 (Moreover, he doesn't know.)

### 4. Using 以外 for emotional "only" when しか is appropriate

!!! failure "Register mismatch"
    <ruby>君<rt>きみ</rt></ruby><ruby>以外<rt>いがい</rt></ruby><ruby>愛<rt>あい</rt></ruby>せない。 (in a love letter)

    ~Grammatically correct but emotionally cold. It sounds like "I am incapable of loving outside your category" rather than "You are my only one."~

!!! success "Correct"
    <ruby>君<rt>きみ</rt></ruby>しか<ruby>愛<rt>あい</rt></ruby>せない。

### 5. Using しか for objective inventory when だけ or 以外 is appropriate

!!! failure "Register mismatch"
    <ruby>倉庫<rt>そうこ</rt></ruby>には<ruby>椅子<rt>いす</rt></ruby>が<ruby>三<rt>みっ</rt></ruby>つしかありません。

    ~Grammatically correct, but in a warehouse inventory report, しか injects emotion where none belongs. <ruby>椅子<rt>いす</rt></ruby>は<ruby>三<rt>みっ</rt></ruby>つだけあります or <ruby>椅子<rt>いす</rt></ruby>は<ruby>三<rt>みっ</rt></ruby>つ<ruby>以外<rt>いがい</rt></ruby>ありません is more appropriate.~

!!! success "Correct (inventory)"
    <ruby>椅子<rt>いす</rt></ruby>は<ruby>三<rt>みっ</rt></ruby>つだけあります。

### 6. Using 以外 with verb phrases incorrectly

!!! failure "Attachment error"
    <ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>き**ます**<ruby>以外<rt>いがい</rt></ruby滑、<ruby>選択肢<rt>せんたくし</rt></ruby>がない。

    ~<ruby>以外<rt>いがい</rt></ruby> attaches to plain verb forms, not polite forms. The ます form is too dressed-up to sit directly against <ruby>以外<rt>いがい</rt></ruby>.~

!!! success "Correct"
    <ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>く<ruby>以外<rt>いがい</rt></ruby>、<ruby>選択肢<rt>せんたくし</rt></ruby>がない。

## Hidden Points {#hidden-points}

- **Quantifier + しか + negative implies the quantity is small.** <ruby>五人<rt>ごにん</rt></ruby>しか<ruby>来<rt>こ</rt></ruby>なかった means "Only five came" with the implication that this is fewer than expected. If exactly five were expected, the speaker might say <ruby>五人<rt>ごにん</rt></ruby>だけ<ruby>来<rt>き</rt></ruby>た (neutral) or <ruby>五人<rt>ごにん</rt></ruby><ruby>来<rt>き</rt></ruby>た (simple report).
- **<ruby>以外<rt>いがい</rt></ruby> can be used with も to create "also besides."** <ruby>日本語<rt>にほんご</rt></ruby><ruby>以外<rt>いがい</rt></ruby>も<ruby>勉強<rt>べんきょう</rt></ruby>する = "I study things besides Japanese too." The も attaches to <ruby>以外<rt>いがい</rt></ruby>, not the noun.
- **<ruby>以外<rt>いがい</rt></ruby> can be fronted for emphasis:** <ruby>以外<rt>いがい</rt></ruby>の<ruby>者<rt>もの</rt></ruby>は<ruby>入<rt>はい</rt></ruby>るな = "Those other than [authorized] shall not enter." This is formal, almost military or legal register.
- **しかない as necessity is stronger than なければならない.** <ruby>謝<rt>あやま</rt></ruby>るしかない = "There is no alternative but to apologize." <ruby>謝<rt>あやま</rt></ruby>らなければならない = "I must apologize." The first conveys desperation; the second conveys obligation.
- **The "only" overlap zone is a minefield for translators.** English "only" is emotionally neutral. Japanese forces the speaker to choose between objective boundary (<ruby>以外<rt>いがい</rt></ruby>), emotional limitation (しか), neutral restriction (だけ), and formal restriction (のみ). A good translator must read the emotional temperature before choosing.
- **しか can attach to adverbs in limited contexts:** <ruby>少<rt>すこ</rt></ruby>ししか, ちょっとしか. These emphasize the tininess of the amount.
- **以外に (adverb) and <ruby>以外<rt>いがい</rt></ruby> (exclusion) are etymologically related but functionally distinct.** Do not try to derive the adverbial meaning from the exclusion logic. Treat 以外に as a separate vocabulary item meaning "unexpectedly."
- **In very casual speech, しか can be followed by slurred negatives:** これしかねえ (for ない), <ruby>彼<rt>かれ</rt></ruby>しか<ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>らねえ. This is standard masculine casual speech, not slang.
- **The particle は can appear after <ruby>以外<rt>いがい</rt></ruby> for topic clarity:** <ruby>水<rt>みず</rt></ruby><ruby>以外<rt>いがい</rt></ruby>**は**<ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>まない. This is acceptable and common. But は cannot appear between the noun and <ruby>以外<rt>いがい</rt></ruby>: ~~水は以外~~ is ungrammatical.
- **だけ and しか can coexist for emphasis:** これだけしかない = "There is only just this." The double limitation sounds emphatic and slightly desperate.

## Learner Stage {#learner-stage}

| Stage | What It Looks Like |
|-------|-------------------|
| **Now — N4** | Attach <ruby>以外<rt>いがい</rt></ruby> and しか correctly with particle drop; use しか only with negative verbs; produce basic "only" and "except" sentences; avoid affirmative しか |
| **Developing — N4→N3** | Calibrate emotional temperature: use <ruby>以外<rt>いがい</rt></ruby> for boundaries and facts, しか for scarcity and feelings; deploy <ruby>以外<rt>いがい</rt></ruby> with verb phrases; recognize しかない as necessity; distinguish しかも from しか |
| **Automatic — N3→N2** | The choice between <ruby>以外<rt>いがい</rt></ruby> and しか becomes intuitive. You hear <ruby>彼<rt>かれ</rt></ruby>しか<ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>らない and register romance or isolation. You hear <ruby>彼<rt>かれ</rt></ruby><ruby>以外<rt>いがい</rt></ruby><ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>らない and register factual report. You never use しか in a business inventory. |
| **Mastery** | You control the full limitation spectrum: だけ for neutral restriction, <ruby>以外<rt>いがい</rt></ruby> for objective exclusion, しか for emotional limitation, ばかり for habitual complaint, and のみ for formal register. You know when <ruby>以外<rt>いがい</rt></ruby> + negative is the sober alternative to しか + negative, and you never confuse しかも with the limitation marker. |

### Commonly Confused With

- しかも — Conjunction "moreover" (unrelated to しか)

- 〜にする vs. 〜になる — Transitivity (not directly related but appears in learner error space)

- Particle drop (N5) — Required for both patterns

- Quantifiers (N5) — Required for <ruby>少<rt>すこ</rt></ruby>ししか, <ruby>一人<rt>ひとり</rt></ruby>しか, etc.
