## Quick Summary {#quick-summary}

!!! tip "At a glance"
- In そういう, どういう, こういう, and ああいう, the word **いう is not the verb "to say."** It has grammaticalized into a **classificatory device** meaning "the kind called / the so-called / that which is referred to as." そういう人ひと = "a person of that type" — literally "a person [of the kind] called that."
- **こういう** = this kind of (near speaker, concrete). **そういう** = that kind of (near listener or just mentioned). **ああいう** = that kind of (distant, remembered, or hypothetical). **どういう** = what kind of (question, often seeking explanation).
- **どういう** is not neutral description. It asks "what do you mean by that category?" and can carry **confrontational or skeptical charge**. For neutral "what attributes?" use **どんな**.
- **こんな / そんな / あんな / どんな** are the casual, often emotional siblings. そんな is frequently dismissive: そんなことない = "No such thing."
- **そのような / このような** are the formal, written equivalents. A business email uses そのような状況じょうきょう; a friend says そういう状況じょうきょう.
- **という** is the broader quotative system from which そういう grew. It nominalizes definitions: 日本語にほんごという言葉ことば = "the language called Japanese."
- **っていう** is the casual spoken form. In rapid speech, そういう becomes そうゆう or even そっつう. This is standard, not sloppy.

## Core Meaning {#core-meaning}

!!! note "Core Rule"
Use こういう / そういう / ああいう / どういう when you want to classify a noun by **type, category, or discursive reference** rather than by simple physical pointing. The いう adds a layer of "the kind known as / the sort referred to as," turning a demonstrative into a classifier.

| そういう人ひと。 | That kind of person [the type we both know / just discussed]. |
| --- | --- |
|  | *Context: Gossiping. The speaker does not point at a specific body; they invoke a category. いう turns the demonstrative into a type-label.* |

| どういう意味いみ？ | What kind of meaning is that? / What do you mean by that? [explanatory challenge]. |
| --- | --- |
|  | *Context: The listener said something ambiguous. どういう demands clarification of the category, not just description of attributes.* |

| こういう時とき、どうする？ | What do you do at a time like this? [classificatory reference]. |
| --- | --- |
|  | *Context: Facing a novel situation. こういう時とき classifies the moment as a type: "times of this sort."* |

| ああいう人ひとになりたい。 | I want to become that kind of person [distant ideal]. |
| --- | --- |
|  | *Context: Pointing at a role model across the room, or recalling someone from memory. ああいう carries aspirational distance.* |

## Deep Dive: いう — From "Speak" to "Classify" {#deep-dive-iu}

For English speakers, this is the most important insight on this page: **いう in そういう is not "say."** It has traveled so far from its lexical origin that translating it literally produces nonsense. Understanding this grammaticalization is the key to unlocking dozens of N3–N4 patterns.

### The Three Lives of いう

| Layer | Function | Example | English Gloss |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Lexical** | To speak, to utter | 彼かれはそう言いった。 | He said so. |
| **Quotative** | To name, to call, to define | 日本語にほんごという言語げんご | the language called Japanese |
| **Classificatory** | To categorize by type | そういう人ひと | that kind of person |

### How いう Became a Classifier

Japanese demonstratives (こそあど) originally pointed at physical things: この本ほん = "this book." But when speakers wanted to talk about **abstract types** — kinds of people, sorts of situations, classes of meaning — simple pointing was insufficient.

The solution was to embed the demonstrative inside a **quotative frame**: そういう = そう + いう = "that [way] + called." The logic is: "a person **of the type called** that."

This mirrors how English uses **"so-called"** or **"what kind of"**, but Japanese has integrated it so deeply that そういう functions as a single lexical unit.

!!! note "Why this matters for learners"
An English speaker sees そういう人ひと and wants to translate it as "a person who says that" or "a person that is said." This is wrong. The いう has ceased to be about speech acts and has become about **cognitive categorization**. It is closer to "such a" or "that sort of."

### いう as Social Distance Device

Adding いう to a demonstrative creates **discursive distance**. Compare:

| その人ひと | That person [simple pointing]. |
| --- | --- |
| そういう人ひと | That kind of person [categorical judgment]. |

そういう人ひと is not merely pointing — it is **placing the referent in a box**. The box may be positive (そういう人ひとになりたい), negative (そういう人ひとは嫌きらい), or analytical (そういう人ひとが成功せいこうする). But the grammar always adds a layer of **typification**.

### Formality Spectrum of いう

| Form | Register | Example | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| という | Formal, written | 日本語にほんごという言語げんご | Standard quotative |
| そういう | Neutral, spoken/written | そういう人ひと | Standard classificatory |
| っていう | Casual spoken | そうっていうこと？ | Glottal stop insertion |
| てゆう | Very casual, manga, dialect | そうてゆうこと？ | Phonetic spelling of casual speech |
| つう / っつう | Slang, rough | そうつうこと？ | Extreme contraction |

!!! note "The って phenomenon"
In casual speech, という often becomes って. This is not lazy — it is the standard spoken form. そういう can become そうってゆう or simply そうゆう. When you see てゆう in manga, it is representing this natural phonetic reduction.

## The こそあど + いう Family {#the-kosoado-iu-family}

Japanese demonstratives operate on a **three-way spatial axis** extended into a **four-way discursive axis**. The いう attachment turns spatial pointing into categorical classification.

|  | Near Speaker | Near Listener / Just Mentioned | Distant / Hypothetical | Question |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Simple demonstrative** | この | その | あの | どの |
| **Casual type** | こんな | そんな | あんな | どんな |
| **Classificatory いう** | **こういう** | ** arsenic そういう** | **ああいう** | **どういう** |
| **Formal type** | このような | そのような | あそのような | どのような |

### こういう — This Kind of (Concrete, Present)

| こういう時とき、困こまるよね。 | It's tough at times like this, isn't it? |
| --- | --- |
|  | *Context: The speaker is experiencing the situation right now. こういう anchors the type to the immediate present.* |

| こういうのが欲ほしかった。 | This is exactly the kind of thing I wanted. |
| --- | --- |
|  | *Context: Finding the perfect item. の nominalizes the type. こういうの = "a thing of this sort."* |

### そういう — That Kind of (Referential, Discourse-Linked)

| そういうわけじゃないけど。 | It's not that kind of reason, but… / It's not like that, but… |
| --- | --- |
|  | *Context: The most common そういう sentence in anime and drama. そういうわけ = "that kind of reasoning / the explanation called that." The speaker rejects the category the listener has imposed.* |

| そういうところかわいいね。 | That side of you is cute / I like that about you. |
| --- | --- |
|  | *Context: そういうところ = "that kind of place/aspect." The speaker is not pointing at a physical location but at a **characterological trait** — the type of behavior the person just displayed.* |

| でもそういうこと言いうと好すきになっちゃうよ。 | But if you say that kind of thing, I'll end up falling for you. |
| --- | --- |
|  | *Context: そういうこと = "that kind of thing [you just said]." The いう classifies the utterance into a romantic-danger category.* |

### ああいう — That Kind of (Distant, Ideal, Remembered)

| ああいう人ひとになりたい。 | I want to become that kind of person. |
| --- | --- |
|  | *Context: Looking at a role model, or recalling someone admirable. ああいう carries distance — the type is far from the speaker's current self.* |

| ああいう所ところに行いきたい。 | I want to go to a place like that. |
| --- | --- |
|  | *Context: Seeing a photo of a beautiful beach. ああいう invokes the distant, idealized type.* |

### どういう — What Kind of (Explanatory, Often Confrontational)

| どういう人ひとがタイプなの？ | What kind of person is your type? |
| --- | --- |
|  | *Context: Asking for the listener's romantic taxonomy. どういう seeks the **definition** of the type, not just surface attributes.* |

| どういうことだ！ | What the hell do you mean by that! / What is the meaning of this! |
| --- | --- |
|  | *Context: Anger. どういう turns a question into a demand for categorical explanation. It is stronger than 何なにだ because it challenges the listener's entire framing.* |

| どういう意味いみ？ | What do you mean by that? / What kind of meaning is that? |
| --- | --- |
|  | *Context: Seeking clarification. どういう asks for the **category of meaning**; どんな意味いみ asks for **descriptive attributes** of the meaning.* |

## The Classification Landscape: Similar Patterns {#the-classification-landscape}

Beyond the core こう/そう/ああ/どう + いう family, Japanese has a full ecosystem of type-marking devices. Understanding how they layer is essential for N3 calibration.

### こんな / そんな / あんな / どんな — Casual & Emotional

These are **contracted forms** of このような / そのような, but they have developed independent emotional lives. They conjugate like i-adjectives (こんなに, そんなに) and carry stronger speaker attitude.

| どんな人ひと？ | What kind of person? [neutral-casual description]. |
| --- | --- |
|  | *Context: Asking about appearance, personality, job. どんな is softer and more open than どういう.* |

| そんなことない！ | No such thing! / That's not true! [dismissal]. |
| --- | --- |
|  | *Context: The most common そんな sentence. そんなこと = "a thing of that sort." The grammar rejects the category entirely.* |

| こんなの初はじめて。 | This is the first time [I've experienced] something like this. |
| --- | --- |
|  | *Context: Surprise. の nominalizes the situation type. こんなの = "a thing of this sort."* |

!!! note "そんな as dismissive weapon"
そんな is inherently more emotional than そういう. It can belittle: そんな仕事しごと、辞やめちゃえ = "Quit that kind of job [which I judge as worthless]." そういう仕事しごと would be neutral description; そんな仕事しごと is judgmental.

### このような / そのような / あのような / どのような — Formal & Written

These are the **na-adjective + ような** combinations. They appear in business, academic, and literary contexts where そういう would sound too casual.

| そのような状況じょうきょうでは、どうすればいいですか。 | In a situation like that, what should one do? |
| --- | --- |
|  | *Context: Business meeting or formal writing. そのような elevates the register.* |

| どのようなお仕事しごとをされていますか。 | What kind of work do you do? [ultra-polite]. |
| --- | --- |
|  | *Context: High-status interview. どのような is the polite twin of どんな.* |

### という — The Quotative Nominalizer

The parent pattern from which そういう grew. という attaches to nouns to create definitions, titles, and named categories.

| 日本語にほんごという言葉ことばは難むずかしい。 | The language called Japanese is difficult. |
| --- | --- |
|  | *Context: Defining the subject. という = "called / known as."* |

| 彼かれという人間にんげん | The person [known as] him / A man like him |
| --- | --- |
|  | *Context: Can be neutral definition or emphatic characterization. という adds discursive weight.* |

| そういうことです。 | That is what it means / That is the kind of thing it is. |
| --- | --- |
|  | *Context: Explanatory closing. そういう is simply そう + いう, where いう retains more of its "say" sense: "It says that."* |

### っていう / てゆう — Casual Spoken Layer

In conversation, という collapses into って. This affects そういう too.

| そうっていうこと？ | Is that what you mean? [casual]. |
| --- | --- |
|  | *Context: Confirming understanding. The っていう is the casual quotative.* |

| なんかさ、こうっていう感かんじ？ | Like, it's this kind of feeling? [very casual]. |
| --- | --- |
|  | *Context: Struggling to articulate a vibe. The doubled っていう shows the speaker groping for the right category.* |

```
*"I told you, that's not true."*

```

```

**Shift:** そういうこと classifies the utterance. <ruby>言<rt>い</rt></ruby>うと is conditional. なっちゃう is casual てしまう. Standard young-adult romantic register.

```

```
*"What kind of person is your type?"*

**Shift:** どういう with のですか explanatory question. Neutral, polite, curious. The いう remains classificatory.

```

```
*"What kind of person do you prefer?"*
```

## In Real Life {#in-real-life}

!!! example "In Real Life — Rejecting a Category"
**Situation:** ハル is being teased by a friend who assumes they are dating someone. Haru tries to deflect without confirming or denying.

```
> そういうわけじゃないけど…

*"It's not that kind of reason, but…"*

**Why そういう fits:** Haru is not saying "that's wrong." They are saying "the category of reasoning you have applied does not fit." そういうわけ = "the explanation of that sort." The rejection is categorical, not factual.

**What そのわけじゃない would change:** That would mean "that reason is not [the reason]," which is a simple denial. そういう adds the nuance of "your whole framing is off."

**The trailing けど:** The sentence trails off because Haru does not want to explain further. This is classic Japanese deflection — reject the category, then go silent.

```

!!! example "In Real Life — Characterological Praise"
**Situation:** タケシ has just done something modest and thoughtful — offering his umbrella to a stranger. His partner smiles.

```
> そういうところかわいいね。

*"That side of you is cute / I like that about you."*

**Why そういうところ fits:** ところ means "place," but here it means "aspect / side / facet." そういうところ = "that kind of place [in your character]." The speaker is not praising the single act but the **type of behavior** it represents.

**What そのところ would change:** Unnatural. そのところ would mean "that place," implying a physical location. そういう turns it into a characterological category.

**The emotional weight:** This is a common romantic phrase. It says "I love not just what you did, but the kind of person who does that."

```

!!! example "In Real Life — Romantic Taxonomy"
**Situation:** Two friends are people-watching at a café. One asks about the other's dating preferences.

```
> どういう<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>がタイプなの？

*"What kind of person is your type?"*

**Why どういう fits:** The speaker wants the listener's **definition** of attraction — the categorical system they use. どんな<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>がタイプ？ would ask for surface attributes (tall, funny, kind). どういう asks for the deeper type: "What do you mean by 'your type'? What category are we talking about?"

**What 1790 どんな would change:** どんな<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>がタイプ？ is perfectly natural but slightly more superficial. It asks "describe the person." どういう asks "define the category."

```

!!! example "In Real Life — Romantic Danger"
**Situation:** ユイ is being complimented by someone she is trying not to fall for. The compliment is precise and devastating.

```
> でもそういうこと<ruby>言<rt>い</rt></ruby>うと<ruby>好<rt>す</rt></ruby>きになっちゃうよ。

*"But if you say that kind of thing, I'll end up falling for you."*

**Why そういうこと fits:** The speaker classifies the utterance into a dangerous category: "things of that sort [which you just said]." It is not the specific words but the **type** of statement that threatens her emotional defenses.

**What そのこと would change:** "That thing" — too literal, too small. そういうこと makes the utterance represent a whole class of romantic provocations.

**The 言うと conditional:** "If you [continue to] say [things of that type]…" The conditional frames the category as a pattern, not a one-time event.

```

## Grammar Analysis {#grammar-analysis}

### Function in Context

* そういうわけじゃない: そう modifies いう. いう classifies わけ. じゃない negates the category. The structure is: [demonstrative-adverb + classifier] + [noun] + [negative copula]. The entire phrase rejects the listener's imposed typification.
* そういうところかわいい: そういう modifies ところ. かわいい predicates. The grammar treats a character trait as a "place" (ところ) of a certain "type" (そういう).
* どういう人ひとがタイプ: どういう modifies 人ひと. が marks the subject of the question. タイプ is the predicate noun. The grammar asks for the listener's taxonomic definition.
* そういうこと言いうと: そういう modifies こと. 言いうと is the conditional "if [one] says." The grammar warns that utterances of this class trigger a result.

### Construction {#construction}

#### The Core Pattern: Demonstrative-Adverb + いう + Noun

| Form | Pattern | Example | Register |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Standard | こう/そう/ああ/どう + いう + Noun | そういう人ひと | Neutral |
| Casual contracted | こんな/そんな/あんな/どんな + Noun | そんな人ひと | Casual |
| Formal | この/その/あの/どの + ような + Noun | そのような人ひと | Formal |
| Quotative | Noun + という + Noun | 天才てんさいという人ひと | Definitional |
| Casual quotative | Noun + っていう + Noun | 天才てんさいっていう人ひと | Spoken |

#### どういう vs. どんな: The Critical N3 Distinction

|  | どういう | どんな |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Core ask** | "What category / what do you mean by" | "What attributes / describe" |
| **Implication** | Seeks explanation of type | Seeks description of instance |
| **Emotional register** | Can be confrontational | Neutral, open |
| **Appropriate when** | Challenging framing, defining systems | Gathering surface information |
| **Example** | どういう意味いみ？ (What do you mean?) | どんな意味いみ？ (What kind of meaning?) |
| **Example** | どういう人ひと？ (What's your type? / Define it.) | どんな人ひと？ (What's he like? / Describe him.) |

!!! note "The overlap zone"
In casual speech, native speakers often use どういう and どんな interchangeably for simple questions. But in charged contexts — arguments, interviews, romantic probing — the distinction matters. どういう is the grammar of "explain your categories to me."

## Japanese Mindset {#japanese-mindset}

The いう classification system reveals how Japanese speakers **think in types rather than tokens**.

* **Categorical thinking.** When a Japanese speaker says そういう人ひと, they are not describing an individual but invoking a **socially recognized type**. The grammar assumes that types exist and that speakers share knowledge of them.
* **Deflection through categorization.** そういうわけじゃない is a classic Japanese defense because it does not deny facts — it denies **framing**. The speaker says "your category is wrong" rather than "your fact is wrong." This is less confrontational.
* **Romantic taxonomy.** どういう人ひとがタイプ？ assumes that attraction operates through **types**. The question is not "who do you like?" but "what is your system of classification?" This reflects a cultural comfort with defining abstract categories.
* **Humble distancing with ああいう.** Using ああいう for role models creates aspirational distance. It says "that kind [over there, above me]". The grammar encodes hierarchy.
* **Emotional coloring through contraction.** そんな carries more speaker attitude than そういう because the contraction forces the speaker's breath and body into the word. It is physically harder to say そんな dismissively without feeling it.

**そういう vs. その:** その人ひと = "that person [I am pointing at]." そういう人ひと = "that kind of person [we are discussing]." The いう adds a whole layer of categorical abstraction.

**そういう vs. そんな:** そういう is analytical. そんな is emotional. A therapist says そういう気持ちきもち = "that kind of feeling [which you described]." A friend says そんな気持ちきもちわかる = "I get that kind of feeling [empathetically]."

| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| N5 — Foundation | この / その / あの / どの | Simple demonstratives | その人ひと |
| N5 — Foundation | こんな / そんな / あんな / どんな | Casual type descriptors | どんな人ひと？ |
| **N4 — Core ← You are here** | **こういう / そういう / ああいう / どういう** | **Classificatory "kind of"** | **そういう人ひと。** |
| N4 — Core | **という** | **Quotative nominalizer** | **日本語にほんごという言語げんご。** |
| N3 — Extended | **そのような / このような** | **Formal written equivalents** | **そのような状況じょうきょう。** |

|  | *Context: Rejecting the listener's framing without direct contradiction. The けど trails off into silence.* |

|  | *Context: Praising a trait, not an act. そういう turns the behavior into a type.* |

|  | *Context: Seeking the listener's romantic classification system.* |

|  | *Context: Classifying the utterance as a dangerous type. The conditional frames it as a pattern.* |

| --- | --- |
|  | *Context: Facing a novel crisis. こういう時とき classifies the moment.* |

|  | *Context: Aspirational reference to a role model. ああいう carries distance and respect.* |

| --- | --- |

## Common Mistakes {#common-mistakes}

### 1. Translating いう as "say" in そういう

!!! failure "Literal translation error"
"That says person" or "a person who says that."

```
~いう in this pattern is not the lexical verb "to say." It is a grammaticalized classifier. Translating it literally produces nonsense and prevents comprehension of dozens of N3 patterns.~

```

!!! success "Correct understanding"
そういう人ひと = "that kind of person" / "a person of that sort."

### 2. Using どういう when どんな is appropriate

!!! failure "Register mismatch"
カフェで店員てんいんに「どういうコーヒーがありますか。」

```
~どういう demands categorical explanation ("what do you mean by types of coffee?"). The barista is not defining a taxonomy; they are listing attributes. どんなコーヒーがありますか is natural.~

```

!!! success "Correct"
どんなコーヒーがありますか。

!!! success "Correct (if challenging a menu category)"
どういうコーヒーですか。 (What do you mean by that coffee? — said when pointing at an unclear menu item.)

### 3. Confusing その and そういう

!!! failure "Deixis error"
Topology error: pointing at an object requires spatial deixis, not abstract categorization.

```
~If the speaker is pointing at a physical book, その<ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt></ruby> is correct. そういう implies a type of book, not a specific object. Using そういう while pointing is a category error.~

```

!!! success "Correct"
その本ほんを指ささないでください。 (Please don't point at that book.)
そういう本ほんが好すきです。 (I like that kind of book.)

### 4. Using そんな in formal writing

!!! failure "Register error"
そんな状況じょうきょうでは、対応たいおうが必要ひつようです。

```
~そんな is casual and can sound dismissive even when unintended. Formal documents require そのような.~

```

!!! success "Correct"
そのような状況じょうきょうでは、対応たいおうが必要ひつようです。

### 5. Keeping だ/です before いう in classification

!!! failure "Copula stacking error"
そうだいう人ひと。

```
~The demonstrative adverb そう connects directly to いう. No copula intervenes.~

```

!!! success "Correct"
精度そういう人ひと。

### 6. Assuming ああいう and そういう are interchangeable

!!! failure "Deixis error"
隣となりに座すわっている人ひとについて「ああいう人ひと」

```
~ああいう is for distant or remembered referents. For someone sitting right next to the listener, そういう is correct. Using ああいう would imply the person is far away or hypothetical.~

```

!!! success "Correct"
そういう人ひと。 (That kind of person [near you / just mentioned].)

## Hidden Points {#hidden-points}

* **そういうわけじゃない is the grammar of Japanese deflection.** It is used constantly in romance, politics, and comedy to reject a premise without denying facts. The categorical rejection is softer than factual denial because it attacks framing, not truth.
* **どういう can be a weapon.** どういうつもり？ = "What do you mean by that [hostile intent]?" どういうことだ！ = "What is the meaning of this [outrage]?" The grammar turns questions into accusations by challenging the listener's categorical system.
* **そういうところは、まさにキュートな特徴です。** Recognizing specific aspects of character is a cornerstone of conversational praise. Saying it to a Japanese person immediately signals a keen, personal observation.
* **こんな/そんな/あんな/どんな are historically contractions of このような/etc.** But they have developed independent emotional lives. そんなに cannot be replaced by そのように: そんなに高たかい = "so [surprisingly] expensive"; そのように高たかい = "expensive in that manner [formal]."
* **っていう in young speech has become a filler.** Some teenagers insert っていう between every clause: なんかさ、っていうか、あのさ… This is not the classificatory いう but a discourse marker of hesitation. It is controversial and often criticized by older speakers.
* **いう in という can define proper nouns.** 富士山ふじさんという山やま = "the mountain called Mt. Fuji." This is redundant (富士山ふじさん is already a name) but grammatically natural. It adds solemnity or didactic tone.
* **The いう classifier system does not work with all demonstratives.** こう/そう/ああ/どう are the only adverbial demonstratives that take いう. ここ/そこ/あそこ/どこ (place) and これ/それ/あれ/どれ (thing) do not form *ここいう or *これいう.
* **そういうの is a universal nominalizer.** When the speaker cannot find the noun, they say そういうの = "that kind of thing." It is the Japanese equivalent of "that thing, you know, the thing."
* **Manga spelling てゆう is phonetically accurate.** In rapid speech, そういう often becomes そうゆう because the い drops. Manga writers spell this as てゆう to mark casual register. It is not a different grammar — it is the same grammar in phonetic dress.
* **Business Japanese avoids いう classification when possible.** Instead of そういう状況じょうきょう, a memo writes 当該とうがい状況じょうきょう or その事案じあん. The classificatory いう feels too conversational for legal and corporate prose.

## Learner Stage {#learner-stage}

| Stage | What It Looks Like |
| --- | --- |
| **Now — N4** | Use こう/そう/ああ/どう + いう correctly with nouns; understand that いう is not "say" in this pattern; distinguish そういう (type) from その (instance); produce basic classification sentences |
| **Developing — N4→N3** | Calibrate どういう vs. どんな; use こんな/そんな/あんな/どんな for emotional coloring; recognize という as quotative nominalizer; avoid そんな in formal writing; deploy そのような in business contexts |
| **Automatic — N3→N2** | The choice between そういう and そんな becomes intuitive. You hear そういうわけじゃない and immediately parse it as deflection. You choose どのような for job interviews without deliberation. |
| **Mastery** | You control the full classification spectrum: こう/そう/ああ/どう + いう for neutral classification, こんな/そんな/あんな/どんな for emotional casual speech, この/その/あの/どの + ような for formal prose, という for definitions, っていう for spoken fluency, and いわゆる for meta-commentary. You never translate いう literally in these patterns. |

### Commonly Confused With

* その / この / あの — Simple demonstratives (pointing, not classifying)
* そうだ — Conjecture / hearsay (unrelated to そういう)
* そう — Manner adverb "in that way" (そう思おもう)
* どう — "How" as manner (どう思おもう?) vs. どういう "what kind"
* って — Casual topic marker (unrelated to っていう quotative, though homophonous)
