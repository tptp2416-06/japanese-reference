# なんて — "Something Like / The Very Idea Of / As If"

## 彼なんて信じない。

---

## JLPT Level {#jlpt-level}

!!! abstract "JLPT Level — N4 Core / N3 Extended"
    The basic dismissive-exemplification pattern (Noun + なんて) is solid N4 — a daily feature of casual speech. But the full system — the etymological contraction from など＋は, the three-way functional split (listing, dismissal, exclamation), the emotional calibration against なんか and など, and the quotative track from なんと言う → なんて — pushes into N3 sociolinguistic territory. This page maps the complete なんて landscape up to N3.

    | Tier | Pattern | Description | Example |
    |------|---------|-------------|---------|
    | N5 — Foundation | など | "Etc. / and so on" — neutral listing | 映画など |
    | N5 — Foundation | と言う / って | Quotative "that…" / "called…" | 彼と言う人 |
    | **N4 — Core ← You are here** | **なんて (dismissive)** | **"Something like / the likes of" — belittling** | **彼なんて。** |
    | **N4 — Core ← You are here** | **なんて (exclamatory)** | **"How…! / To think that…!" — emotional quotative** | **来るなんて。** |
    | N3 — Extended | **なんか** | **Casual dismissive (slightly softer than なんて)** | **彼なんか。** |
    | N3 — Extended | **なんて言う / なんてこと** | **"What do you mean" / "What the…" — confrontational** | **なんてことだ！** |
    | N3 — Extended | **～なんてのは / なんてものは** | **"The likes of which…" — emphatic topic** | **彼なんてのは。** |

## Quick Summary {#quick-summary}

!!! tip "At a glance"
    - **なんて** is a **contracted fusion** of など (exemplification marker) + は (topic/contrast particle). The path: など＋は → なんては → なんて. What began as "things like X, [as for them]" collapsed into a single emotional morpheme that means **"something like X"** with heavy speaker attitude.
    - **Function 1 — Dismissive exemplification:** Noun + なんて. Belittles the noun, treats it as unworthy, surprising, or trivial. 彼なんて信じない = "Someone like him / the very idea of him — I don't believe [it/him]."
    - **Function 2 — Exclamatory quotative:** Clause + なんて. Expresses shock, disbelief, or amazement at an entire proposition. 来るなんて知らなかった = "I had no idea [that you] would come — the very idea!"
    - **Function 3 — "What the…" confrontational:** なんてことだ！ / なんて言ってるの？. This track comes from なんと (what) + quotative って, a separate etymology that merged phonetically with the など-derived なんて.
    - **Emotional rule:** なんて is never neutral. Even when listing, it carries a shrug. It is the grammar of **emotional distance** — either looking down (dismissive) or looking up in shock (exclamatory).
    - **Register restriction:** なんて is **casual**. In formal writing, replace with など, そのような, or だなんて. Using bare なんて in a business email is a register crash.
    - **なんか** is the even softer, more casual sibling. Where なんて can wound, なんか often just mumbles.

## Core Meaning {#core-meaning}

!!! note "Core Rule"
    Use なんて when you want to frame a noun, person, or proposition as **small, surprising, or emotionally charged** — either because you dismiss it as trivial or because you are stunned that it exists at all. The grammar wraps the referent in your emotional reaction.

| 彼なんて信じない。 | Someone like him / The very idea of him — I don't believe it [dismissive]. |
|---|---|
| | *Context: The speaker considers the person untrustworthy. なんて shrinks him into a type not worth serious consideration.* |

| 映画なんて滅多に見ない。 | Things like movies / A movie or whatever — I hardly ever watch them [dismissive listing]. |
|---|---|
| | *Context: The speaker is not a cinephile. なんて turns "movies" into a category they casually brush off.* |

| 来るなんて聞いてないよ。 | I never heard that you were coming — the very idea! [exclamatory]. |
|---|---|
| | *Context: Surprise at an unexpected arrival. なんて wraps the entire clause 来る in the speaker's shock.* |

| なんてことだ！ | What the hell! / What is the meaning of this! [confrontational]. |
|---|---|
| | *Context: Outrage. This なんて comes from the なんと (what) + quotative track, but has merged phonetically with the dismissive なんて.* |

## The Etymological Path: など＋は → なんて {#the-etymological-path}

Understanding where なんて came from explains why it feels so emotionally loaded. It is not a single word — it is a **fossilized contraction** of two grammar points that have melted together over centuries of casual speech.

### Step 1: など — The Exemplification Marker

| 映画などを見る。 | I watch things like movies / movies and the like. |
|---|---|
| | *Context: など marks the noun as an example from a larger set. Neutral, slightly formal.* |

など is an **enumerative particle**. It says "X, among other similar things." It does not belittle — it simply opens a category.

### Step 2: など＋は — Topic + Contrast

| 映画などは滅多に見ない。 | Things like movies, [as for them,] I hardly watch. |
|---|---|
| | *Context: The は adds topic focus and contrast. "Movies (and things like them) — as opposed to other things — I don't watch."* |

Adding は turns the example into a **topic for judgment**. The speaker is not just listing movies; they are holding them up for evaluation.

### Step 3: Phonetic Erosion — などは → なんては

In rapid speech, などは undergoes standard Japanese phonetic reduction:
- など (nado) + は (wa) → なんぞは → なんては (nantewa)

The ど softens to ん, and the は glides into ては. This is the same process that turned といえば into ってば.

### Step 4: Final Contraction — なんては → なんて

| 映画なんて見ない。 | I don't watch things like movies / Movies? No way. |
|---|---|
| | *Context: The は has vanished. What remains is a single emotional particle: なんて. It carries the full weight of exemplification + topic + dismissive attitude in one syllable.* |

!!! note "Why this matters for learners"
    Because なんて is a contraction, it retains the **topic-like behavior** of its parent は. It isolates the noun for comment. It does not merely modify the noun — it **holds the noun up to the light and squints at it**.

## The Three Functional Tracks of なんて {#the-three-functional-tracks}

なんて in modern Japanese operates along three distinct tracks. Two come from the など＋は lineage. One comes from なんと＋って. They have merged in pronunciation but remain separate in grammar.

### Track 1: Dismissive Exemplification (など-derived)

Noun + なんて. The speaker treats the noun as trivial, unworthy, or surprising.

| 彼なんて知らない。 | I don't know someone like him / The likes of him? No idea [dismissal]. |
|---|---|
| | *Context: Dismissing a person's importance. なんて wraps the name in a sneer.* |

| お金なんてあるわけない。 | Money? As if I have any [dismissive + negative]. |
|---|---|
| | *Context: The speaker is broke. なんて turns "money" into a laughable concept.* |

| 英語なんて簡単だよ。 | English? That's easy [dismissive boast]. |
|---|---|
| | *Context: Can be genuine confidence or arrogant dismissal. The なんて makes the subject feel small.* |

| 彼女なんて好きじゃない。 | Someone like her? I don't like her [emotional rejection]. |
|---|---|
| | *Context: Romantic rejection. なんて adds cruelty by shrinking the person into a type.* |

!!! note "The 'as if' nuance"
    When なんて attaches to nouns in negative contexts, it often carries an **"as if"** flavor. 彼なんて信じない = "As if I would believe him." The grammar turns disbelief into scorn.

### Track 2: Exclamatory Quotative (など-derived, clause-final)

Clause (plain form) + なんて. The speaker is shocked, amazed, or disbelieving that the clause is true.

| 来るなんて知らなかった。 | I had no idea you were coming — the very idea! [surprise]. |
|---|---|
| | *Context: Unexpected guest. なんて wraps the entire proposition 来る in the speaker's astonishment.* |

| 合格したなんて信じられない。 | I can't believe you passed — imagine that! [amazement]. |
|---|---|
| | *Context: The speaker is stunned by good news. なんて makes the achievement feel almost mythical.* |

| こんな所に住むなんて、考えられない。 | Living in a place like this — I can't even imagine it [shock]. |
|---|---|
| | *Context: Visiting a terrible apartment. なんて frames the entire lifestyle as shocking.* |

| 君が泣くなんて、初めて見た。 | Seeing you cry — that's the first time [emotional impact]. |
|---|---|
| | *Context: The speaker is moved or surprised by an uncharacteristic display. なんて marks the event as extraordinary.* |

!!! note "Exclamatory なんて vs. quotative と"
    Both can report speech or thought. と is neutral: 来ると聞いた = "I heard that [you] would come." なんて is emotional: 来るなんて聞いてない = "I never heard [anything about] you coming — the nerve!" The grammar colors the reception, not just the transmission.

### Track 3: Confrontational "What" (なんと-derived)

なんてこと / なんて言う / なんてもの. This track comes from なんと (what) + って (quotative), but has merged with the dismissive なんて in sound.

| なんてことだ！ | What the hell! / What is the meaning of this! [outrage]. |
|---|---|
| | *Context: Discovering a disaster. なんてこと = "what thing" — the grammar demands categorical explanation of the horror.* |

| なんて言ってるの？ | What are you saying? / What do you mean? [disbelief]. |
|---|---|
| | *Context: The listener said something absurd. なんて asks for the words themselves, wrapped in shock.* |

| なんて失礼なことを言うんだ。 | How dare you say something so rude! [condemnation]. |
|---|---|
| | *Context: The なんて modifies the degree of rudeness. It is not "what rudeness" but "the very idea of such rudeness."* |

| なんてものを送ってきたんだ。 | What kind of thing have you sent me! [complaint]. |
|---|---|
| | *Context: Receiving a defective product. なんてもの = "what a thing" — the grammar treats the object as a category of offense.* |

!!! note "The merged phonetics, separate grammars"
    A native speaker hears 彼なんて and なんてことだ as the same word. But historically, the first is など-derived (exemplification + topic), the second なんと-derived (interrogative + quotative). For learners, the practical difference is: **noun-final なんて is dismissive; clause-final なんて is exclamatory; standalone なんて is confrontational.**

## The Emotional Spectrum: など vs. なんか vs. なんて {#the-emotional-spectrum}

These three form a gradient of emotional intensity. Choosing the wrong one is a social misstep.

| Particle | Core Meaning | Emotional Temperature | Social Risk | Example |
|----------|-----------|----------------------|-------------|---------|
| など | "Things like / and so on" | Neutral, slightly formal | Safe everywhere | 映画など |
| なんか | "Something like / kind of" | Casual, vague, slightly dismissive | Casual only; can sound sloppy | 映画なんか |
| なんて | "Something like / the very idea of" | Strongly emotional (dismissive or shocked) | Casual only; can wound or sound dramatic | 映画なんて |

| 映画など見ない。 | I don't watch things like movies [neutral statement]. |
|---|---|
| | *Context: A calm report of preferences. など is unmarked.* |

| 映画なんか見ない。 | I don't watch movies or whatever [casual shrug]. |
|---|---|
| | *Context: A lazy dismissal. なんか is softer than なんて — it shrugs rather than sneers.* |

| 映画なんて見ない。 | Movies? I don't watch that stuff [strong dismissal]. |
|---|---|
| | *Context: The speaker actively looks down on cinema. なんて makes the category feel beneath them.* |

!!! note "なんか as hesitation filler"
    なんか also functions as a vague filler: なんか、変だよね = "Kinda, it's weird, huh?" This is unrelated to the dismissive なんか. Context disambiguates.

## Register and Formality {#register-and-formality}

=== ":material-chat-outline: Casual (dismissive)"
    彼なんて信じない。

    *"Someone like him? No way I believe him."*

    **Shift:** Bare noun + なんて. Strong dismissal. Standard peer-to-peer emotional speech.

=== ":material-chat-outline: Casual (exclamatory)"
    来るなんて聞いてないよ。

    *"You coming? I never heard that!"*

    **Shift:** Clause + なんて. Surprise at the proposition. The よ adds assertive complaint.

=== ":material-chat-outline: Casual (softened dismissal)"
    映画なんか滅多に見ない。

    *"Movies or whatever — I hardly watch them."*

    **Shift:** なんか replaces なんて for a gentler, more mumbled dismissal. Less confrontational.

=== ":material-tie: Basic Polite"
    そのようなことは考えられません。

    *"I cannot imagine something like that."*

    **Shift:** そのような replaces なんて. 考えられません is polite negative potential. The emotion is drained into formality.

=== ":material-tie: Basic Polite (quotative)"
    来るとは聞いておりませんでした。

    *"I had not heard that [you] would come."*

    **Shift:** とは is the formal quotative. おりませんでした is humble past negative. The exclamatory charge of なんて is entirely absent.

=== ":material-briefcase-outline: Extra Polite (quotative)"
    合格されたとは、まったく存じ上げておりませんでした。

    *"I had absolutely no knowledge that you had passed."*

    **Shift:** Full keigo. された is honorific passive. 存じ上げておりません is humble. なんて would be unthinkable at this register.

=== ":material-briefcase-outline: Written / Formal (exemplification)"
    映画などを鑑賞することは稀である。

    *"Opportunities to view films and the like are rare."*

    **Shift:** など is the formal exemplification marker. 鑑賞する elevates "watching" to "viewing." 稀である is literary.

## Grammar Analysis {#grammar-analysis}

### Function in Context

- 彼なんて信じない: 彼 is the noun. なんて is the dismissive particle. 信じない is the negative predicate. The grammar isolates 彼 as an unworthy type and then negates belief.
- 来るなんて知らなかった: 来る is the plain verb clause. なんて is the exclamatory particle. 知らなかった is the negative past predicate. The grammar wraps the entire clause in shock before stating ignorance.
- なんてことだ: なんて modifies こと. だ is the copula. The grammar demands categorical identification of an outrageous event.
- 彼なんてのは: なんて + のは turns the dismissive phrase into a topic. "The likes of him — [as for that category]…" This is slightly more emphatic than bare 彼なんて.

### Construction {#construction}

#### Dismissive Exemplification (Noun + なんて)

| Preceding Element | Pattern | Example | Notes |
|-------------------|---------|---------|-------|
| Noun (person) | Noun + なんて | 彼なんて | Strong dismissal |
| Noun (thing) | Noun + なんて | お金なんて | Trivialization |
| Noun (activity) | Noun + なんて | 英語なんて | Category belittling |
| Noun + なんて + のは | Noun + なんて + のは | 彼なんてのは | Emphatic topic |
| Noun + なんて + もの | Noun + なんて + もの | 彼なんてもの | "The likes of which" |

#### Exclamatory Quotative (Clause + なんて)

| Preceding Form | Pattern | Example | Notes |
|----------------|---------|---------|-------|
| Plain verb | Verb + なんて | 来るなんて | Surprise at action |
| Past verb | Past + なんて | 来たなんて | Surprise at completed action |
| Adjective (i) | Adj + なんて | 高いなんて | Surprise at quality |
| Adjective (na) | Stem + な + なんて | 静かなんて | Surprise at state |
| Noun + だ | Noun + なんて | 先生なんて | Surprise at identity |

#### Confrontational (なんて + Noun / Verb)

| Pattern | Example | Notes |
|---------|---------|-------|
| なんて + こと | なんてことだ | "What thing!" — outrage |
| なんて + 言う | なんて言うの | "What are you saying!" — disbelief |
| なんて + もの | なんてものだ | "What a thing!" — complaint |
| なんて + 失礼な | なんて失礼な | "How rude!" — condemnation |

!!! warning "Do not use なんて in formal writing"
    !!! failure "Register crash"
        彼なんて信じられません。
    !!! success "Correct (formal)"
        彼のような人物を信じることはできません。

    ~なんて is strictly casual. In business, academic, or polite social contexts, it sounds immature or aggressive.~

!!! warning "Do not confuse clause-final and noun-final なんて"
    !!! failure "Ambiguity"
        Interpreting 来るなんて as "someone like 'coming.'"
    !!! success "Correct understanding"
        来るなんて = "the very idea of [someone] coming" (exclamatory).
        彼なんて = "someone like him" (dismissive).

    ~The same phonetic shape does two different grammatical jobs. Context (noun vs. clause before なんて) resolves it instantly for native speakers.~

## Japanese Mindset {#japanese-mindset}

The なんて grammar reveals a cultural comfort with **emotional categorization** — wrapping people, things, and events in your subjective reaction and presenting that reaction as grammar.

- **Dismissive なんて as social hierarchy.** Calling someone 彼なんて in their absence is a power move. It places them in a box labeled "unworthy of serious consideration." Japanese workplace bullying sometimes uses this grammar to marginalize colleagues without direct confrontation.
- **Exclamatory なんて as emotional release.** Japanese social norms discourage direct emotional expression. なんて provides a grammatical valve: 来るなんて知らなかった wraps shock in syntax so the speaker does not have to shout.
- **The "as if" deflection.** 彼なんて信じない does not say "I have evaluated him and found him dishonest." It says "The very category of 'him' is incompatible with my belief." This is indirect criticism encoded in grammar.
- **なんて and feminine speech.** Exclamatory なんて is stereotypically associated with young feminine speech: 可愛いなんて！ = "How cute!" But dismissive なんて crosses gender lines — anyone can sneer.
- **The contraction as intimacy marker.** Because なんて is a collapsed casual form, using it signals "this conversation is relaxed enough for phonetic erosion." It is a register flag as much as a semantic tool.

## Nuance Comparison {#nuance-comparison}

| Grammar Point | Core Meaning | Emotional Register | Formality | Typical Context |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| など | And so on / things like | Neutral, inclusive | Formal-neutral | Lists, examples |
| なんか | Something like / kind of | Casual, vague, sloppy | Casual | Mumbled dismissal, filler |
| なんて (dismissive) | Something like / the likes of | Strong dismissal, scorn | Casual | Rejection, belittling |
| なんて (exclamatory) | The very idea that… | Shock, amazement, disbelief | Casual | Surprise at events |
| なんて (confrontational) | What the… | Outrage, demand | Casual | Anger, complaint |
| なんて言う | What [do you] say | Confrontational question | Casual | Challenging speech |
| そのような | That kind of | Neutral, formal | Formal | Business, writing |
| だなんて | That… [exclamatory in formal] | Surprise (formal-acceptable) | Semi-formal | Written exclamation |
| とは | Quoted topic (formal) | Neutral | Formal | Formal quotative |

**なんて vs. なんか:** Both dismiss. なんて is sharper. なんか is lazier. A teenager says 数学なんか嫌い (I hate math or whatever) — bored. An angry ex says 彼なんて忘れた (Someone like him? I've forgotten him) — cutting.

**なんて vs. など:** など is the librarian's "et cetera." なんて is the teenager's "whatever." They share exemplification DNA but have diverged into opposite social temperatures.

**Clause + なんて vs. と:** 来ると聞いた = "I heard [someone] would come" (neutral report). 来るなんて聞いてない = "I never heard [anything about] coming" (shocked complaint). The quotative particle carries the emotional difference.

## Common Mistakes {#common-mistakes}

### 1. Using なんて in formal writing

!!! failure "Register crash"
    当社なんて考えられません。

    ~なんて is strictly casual. In business, use そのような or 当社としては.~

!!! success "Correct"
    そのようなことは考えられません。

### 2. Confusing なんて with なんと

!!! failure "Vocabulary error"
    なんと言っているの？ (when meaning "What the hell are you saying?")

    ~なんと言っているの is grammatically correct but means "What words are you speaking?" — a literal request for vocabulary. The confrontational "What are you saying?!" is なんて言っているの？~

!!! success "Correct"
    なんて言っているの？ (What are you saying?! — confrontational)
    なんと言っていますか。 (What are you saying? — literal, polite)

### 3. Using なんて when など is socially required

!!! failure "Register mismatch"
    論文には、映画なんて参考資料を使わないでください。

    ~In academic writing, など is the standard exemplification marker. なんて trivializes the source material and insults the reader's seriousness.~

!!! success "Correct"
    論文には、映画などの参考資料を使わないでください。

### 4. Misinterpreting clause-final なんて as dismissive

!!! failure "Pragmatic error"
    Interpreting 来るなんて as "someone like 'coming.'"

    ~When なんて follows a clause (来る), it is exclamatory, not dismissive. It means "the very idea of coming!" not "something like coming."~

!!! success "Correct understanding"
    来るなんて = "the very idea of [you] coming!"
    彼なんて = "someone like him"

### 5. Using なんて with positive politeness

!!! failure "Social error"
    先生なんて、優しいですね。

    ~Even when praising, なんて can sound disrespectful because it trivializes the category. "A teacher? How kind." The grammar shrinks the profession. Use 先生は優しいですね instead.~

!!! success "Correct"
    先生は優しいですね。

### 6. Confusing なんて with なんでも

!!! failure "Vocabulary error"
    なんでもない vs. なんてもない

    ~なんでもない = "It's nothing." なんてもない is not a standard phrase. The なんて particle does not stack with も this way.~

!!! success "Correct"
    なんでもない。 (It's nothing.)
    彼なんて気にしない。 (I don't care about someone like him.)

## Hidden Points {#hidden-points}

- **だなんて is the formal-acceptable exclamation.** In written Japanese, inserting だ before なんて makes the exclamation slightly more acceptable: 来るだなんて知らなかった. This is still casual in tone but grammatically closer to standard written grammar.
- **なんて can soften compliments into backhanded praise.** 可愛いなんて！ can mean "How cute!" (genuine) or "Cute? As if." (sarcastic) depending on intonation. The grammar is ambiguous; the voice decides.
- **The なんてのは construction is a bully's grammar.** あいつなんてのは、すぐ辞めるだろう = "The likes of him will quit soon." The のは adds deliberateness and cruelty.
- **なんて and なんか can stack for emphasis:** 彼なんかなんて信じない is redundant but emphatic — "Someone like him or whatever, I totally don't believe." This is very casual, almost childish.
- **Exclamatory なんて often triggers past-tense reporting.** 来るなんて聞いてない = "I never heard [of] coming." The なんて makes the hearing feel like a past event that should have happened but didn't.
- **なんて can nominalize adjectives for exclamation:** 高いなんて！ = "How expensive!" This is more emotional than 高い！ alone. The なんて turns the adjective into a shocking category.
- **Manga uses なんて heavily for emotional punctuation.** Because manga lacks intonation, なんて provides visual emotional cues. A speech bubble ending in なんて！ signals shock without needing facial close-ups.
- **The など → なんて contraction is still alive.** Speakers occasionally say などは in very careful speech, but なんては and なんて have completely taken over casual registers. The original form survives only in formal writing and conservative speech.
- **なんてことない means "It's nothing" in a specific sense:** なんてことない贈り物 = "A gift that is nothing special." Here なんてことない is an adjectival phrase meaning "trivial / unremarkable." It is related to なんて but functions as a fixed modifier.
- **Young speakers use なんて to mark irony:** 天才なんて呼ばれると困る = "Being called a genius or whatever is embarrassing." The なんて marks the label as externally imposed and slightly ridiculous.

## Learner Stage {#learner-stage}

| Stage | What It Looks Like |
|-------|-------------------|
| **Now — N4** | Use なんて for basic dismissal (彼なんて) and basic exclamation (来るなんて). Recognize that なんて is casual only. Avoid it in formal contexts. Distinguish noun-final (dismissive) from clause-final (exclamatory). |
| **Developing — N4→N3** | Calibrate なんて against なんか and など. Use なんか for softer dismissal. Deploy なんてことだ for confrontational contexts. Recognize the など＋は etymology. Understand that なんて is doing emotional work, not just grammatical work. |
| **Automatic — N3→N2** | なんて arrives as an emotional particle before conscious analysis. You hear 彼なんて and register dismissal instantly. You hear 来るなんて and register shock. You choose など for writing without deliberation. |
| **Mastery** | You control the full exemplification spectrum: など for formal lists, なんか for casual shrugs, なんて for emotional dismissal and exclamation, そのような for business prose, and だなんて for written exclamation. You never confuse the dismissive and exclamatory tracks, and you know when なんて wounds versus when it marvels. |

## See Also {#see-also}

### Same Family

- など — Neutral exemplification
- なんか — Casual soft dismissive / filler
- だなんて — Formal-admissible exclamation
- なんてことだ — Confrontational "what the hell"
- なんて言う — Confrontational "what are you saying"

### Commonly Confused With

- なんと — "What" (interrogative, formal)
- なんでも — "Anything" / "whatever"
- なんてもない — Not a standard phrase (do not confuse with なんでもない)
- なんか (filler) — "Kinda / like" (unrelated to dismissive なんか in some uses)
- って (quotative) — Neutral quotative, less emotional than なんて

### Builds From

- Particle など (N5) — Exemplification foundation
- Particle は (N5) — Topic / contrast
- Quotative と / って (N5) — Reported speech foundation
- Plain verb forms (N5) — Required for clause + なんて
- Casual register awareness (N4) — Required for appropriate deployment
