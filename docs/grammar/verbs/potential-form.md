# 可能形 — Potential Form

## 日本語が話せるようになった。

---

## JLPT Level {#jlpt-level}

!!! abstract "JLPT Level — N4 Core / N3 Extended"
    Potential formation itself is solid N4 — the mechanical shift of godan verbs to the e-row and the られる attachment for ichidan verbs. But the true mastery lies in N3 territory: the が/を particle calibration, the ら抜き spoken disambiguation, the spontaneous/potential split with sensory verbs (聞こえる vs 聞ける, 見える vs 見られる), and the social decision of when to use compact potential form versus periphrastic 〜ことができる.

    | Tier | Pattern | Description | Example |
    |------|---------|-------------|---------|
    | N5 — Foundation | Plain verb forms | Active execution | 日本語を話す。 |
    | N5 — Foundation | 〜たい | Desire to do | 日本語を話したい。 |
    | N4 — Foundation | 〜ている | Progressive / experiential | 日本語を話している。 |
    | **N4 — Core ← You are here** | **〜られる / 〜れる** | **Inherent ability / possibility** | **日本語が話せる。** |
    | N4 — Core | 〜ことができる | Formal periphrastic potential | 日本語を話すことができる。 |
    | N3 — Extended | **が vs. を** | **Object marking shift in potential** | **日本語が話せる。** |
    | N3 — Extended | **ら抜き言葉** | **Spoken disambiguation of られる** | **食べれる。** |
    | N3 — Extended | **聞こえる / 見える** | **Spontaneous sensory capability** | **音が聞こえる。** |
    | N3 — Extended | **〜ようになる** | **Process of acquiring ability** | **話せるようになった。** |
    | N2 — Awareness | 〜得る (うる/える) | Literary/formal potential | あり得る。 |
    | N2 — Awareness | 〜かねる | Negative potential (difficult to do) | 信じかねる。 |

## Quick Summary {#quick-summary}

!!! tip "At a glance"
    - Potential form transforms an action verb into a **state of capability**. 話す (to speak) becomes 話せる (can speak). The verb is no longer about doing — it is about **being able to do**.
    - **Godan verbs** undergo a stem vowel shift: the final う-sound moves to the え-row, and る is added. 書く → 書ける, 読む → 読める, 泳ぐ → 泳げる.
    - **Ichidan verbs** attach られる to the stem. 食べる → 食べられる, 見る → 見られる. In casual speech, the ら drops: 食べれる, 見れる.
    - **する** becomes できる. **来る** becomes 来られる (こられる) or 来れる in casual speech.
    - The object of a potential verb is marked by **が**, not を. 日本語**が**話せる. This is because potential verbs are grammatically **stative/intransitive** — they describe a property, not an action.
    - **聞こえる** and **見える** are **not** potential forms. They are spontaneous verbs describing natural sensory perception. Their potential counterparts (聞ける, 見られる) describe learned/technical ability or possibility.
    - Potential form is **natural and spoken**. 〜ことができる is **formal and written**. Using the latter in casual conversation sounds stiff or foreign.

## Core Meaning {#core-meaning}

!!! note "Core Rule"
    Use the potential form when you want to express that the subject **possesses the inherent or acquired capacity** to perform an action, or that an action is **objectively possible** given circumstances. The potential form does not describe an action taking place — it describes a **state of ability**.

| 日本語が話せる。 | I can speak Japanese [acquired ability]. |
|---|---|
| | *Context: The speaker has learned Japanese. The が marks the language as the thing that "can be spoken."* |

| この部屋から海が見える。 | The ocean is visible from this room [natural sensory perception]. |
|---|---|
| | *Context: 見える is spontaneous, not potential. The view is simply there; no effort is required.* |

| この部屋から海が見られる。 | The ocean can be seen from this room [technical possibility]. |
|---|---|
| | *Context: 見られる is potential. It implies that the room's position or design permits viewing, or that the viewer has the visual capacity.* |

| 明日なら行ける。 | If it's tomorrow, I can go [situational possibility]. |
|---|---|
| | *Context: 行ける is the potential of 行く. The ability here is not physical fitness but schedule availability.* |

| 納豆が食べられるようになった。 | I have become able to eat natto [acquired tolerance]. |
|---|---|
| | *Context: 食べられる is potential. The ようになった frames the change from inability to ability — a common and natural collocation.* |

## The Godan Transformation {#the-godan-transformation}

Godan potential formation is one of the most dramatic morphological changes in Japanese. The final syllable of the dictionary form shifts to its e-row equivalent, and る is added. This is not a suffix — it is a **stem mutation**.

| Dictionary | Final Syllable | E-Row Shift | Potential | Meaning |
|------------|---------------|-------------|-----------|---------|
| 書く | く | け | 書ける | can write |
| 話す | す | せ | 話せる | can speak |
| 待つ | つ | て | 待てる | can wait |
| 死ぬ | ぬ | ね | 死ねる | can die |
| 読む | む | め | 読める | can read |
| 遊ぶ | ぶ | べ | 遊べる | can play |
| 泳ぐ | ぐ | げ | 泳げる | can swim |
| 急ぐ | ぐ | げ | 急げる | can hurry |
| 走る | る | れ | 走れる | can run |
| 帰る | る | れ | 帰れる | can return |
| 入る | る | れ | 入れる | can enter |
| 買う | う | え | 買える | can buy |
| 打つ | つ | て | 打てる | can hit |
| 持つ | つ | て | 持てる | can hold |
| 売る | る | れ | 売れる | can sell |
| 取る | る | れ | 取れる | can take |
| 飛ぶ | ぶ | べ | 飛べる | can fly |
| 知る | る | れ | 知れる | can know |
| 眠る | る | れ | 眠れる | can sleep |

!!! note "The godan る-ending verbs"
    走る, 帰る, 入る, 売る, 取る, 知る, 眠る are godan verbs despite ending in る. They follow the godan pattern (e-row shift), not the ichidan pattern (stem + られる). 走られる would be passive/honorific, not potential.

!!! warning "入れる is a homonym trap"
    入る (hairu, godan, to enter) → 入れる (haireru, potential, can enter). But 入れる (ireru, ichidan, to put in) is a completely different verb meaning "to insert/put in." In writing they are identical; in speech the pitch accent differs. Context is the only guide.

!!! warning "売れる is also an independent intransitive verb"
    売れる (ureru) as potential means "can sell (something)." But 売れる is also an intransitive verb meaning "to sell well / to be in demand." この本は売れる can mean either "I can sell this book" or "This book sells well." Context and particles disambiguate.

## The Ichidan Dilemma {#the-ichidan-dilemma}

Ichidan verbs form potential by attaching られる to the stem. This creates the infamous **三役のられる** (three-function られる) ambiguity: passive, honorific, and potential all share the same shape.

| Dictionary | Stem | Standard Potential | Casual Potential (ら抜き) |
|------------|------|-------------------|-------------------------|
| 食べる | 食べ | 食べられる | 食べれる |
| 見る | 見 | 見られる | 見れる |
| 起きる | 起き | 起きられる | 起きれる |
| 寝る | 寝 | 寝られる | 寝れる |
| 借りる | 借り | 借りられる | 借りれる |
| いる (居る) | い | いられる | いれる |

!!! note "ら抜き is not slang — it is standard spoken Japanese"
    Dropping the ら in ichidan potential (食べれる, 見れる, 来れる) is nearly universal in casual speech. It is not lazy or incorrect in that register — it is the natural form. However, it is **not accepted in formal writing, academic prose, or business documents.** In those contexts, always use the full られる.

!!! note "Disambiguation strategy"
    Native speakers rely on context and particles to distinguish the three られる functions:
    - **Potential:** Object marked by が (or を in casual speech). Subject is the capable entity. No に-marked agent.
    - **Passive:** Agent marked by に. Subject is the recipient of action.
    - **Honorific:** Subject is a high-status person performing the action. No agent.

    In speech, potential られる often drops the ら, instantly resolving the ambiguity.

### Irregular Verbs

| Dictionary | Standard Potential | Casual Potential | Notes |
|------------|-------------------|------------------|-------|
| する | できる | — | できる is a standalone verb, not a suffix pattern. |
| 来る | 来られる (こられる) | 来れる (これる) | 来れる is extremely common in speech to avoid ambiguity with honorific 来られる. |

!!! warning "Do not say できられる"
    する → できる. できる is already a potential/state verb. Adding られる (できられる) is ungrammatical/unnatural. If you need "will be able to," use できるようになる.

## The Particle Shift {#the-particle-shift}

Potential verbs are grammatically **stative and intransitive**. They describe a property or state, not an action directed at an object. Therefore, the object of the original verb is re-marked with **が**, not を.

| Active | Potential | Particle Logic |
|--------|-----------|---------------|
| 日本語**を**話す | 日本語**が**話せる | The language is now the subject of the ability-state. |
| ピアノ**を**弾く | ピアノ**が**弾ける | The piano is the thing that "can be played." |
| 漢字**を**読む | 漢字**が**読める | The characters possess the property of being readable by the subject. |

!!! note "Why が and not を?"
    In active sentences, を marks the direct object — the thing being acted upon. In potential sentences, the verb is no longer transitive. 話せる does not mean "to speak" (an action); it means "can speak" (a state). The thing that "can be spoken" is grammatically the subject of that state, hence が.

!!! note "Casual を-usage"
    In modern casual speech, many native speakers say 日本語**を**話せる or ピアノ**を**弾ける. This is increasingly accepted conversationally, especially among younger speakers. However, in writing, formal speech, and JLPT testing, **が** remains the standard. Learners should master が first, then recognize casual を as register variation.

!!! warning "Do not use both を and が for the same object"
    !!! failure "Incorrect"
        日本語をが話せる。
    !!! success "Correct"
        日本語が話せる。

## Sensory Verbs: 聞こえる・見える vs. 聞ける・見られる {#sensory-verbs}

This is the most consequential N3 distinction in the potential landscape. Japanese separates **natural sensory perception** (spontaneous) from **learned or technical ability** (potential).

| Spontaneous (自然発生) | Potential (可能形) | Core Difference |
|------------------------|-------------------|-----------------|
| **聞こえる** | **聞ける** | |
| Can hear (no effort) | Can listen / is audible (ability or tech) | 聞こえる = your ears work. 聞ける = you have access or skill. |
| **見える** | **見られる / 見れる** | |
| Can see / is visible (no effort) | Can watch / can see (ability or arrangement) | 見える = your eyes work / line of sight exists. 見られる = you have the means or permission. |

| 隣の部屋の音が聞こえる。 | I can hear the noise from the next room [spontaneous, natural hearing]. |
|---|---|
| | *Context: Your ears pick up the sound automatically. No conscious effort. 聞こえる is the only natural choice here.* |

| このラジオは海外でも聞ける。 | This radio can be heard even overseas [potential, technical possibility]. |
|---|---|
| | *Context: The broadcast reaches overseas. This is about signal range and technical capability, not your ears. 聞こえる would sound odd — it would imply the radio noise naturally reaches your ears without equipment.* |

| 山が見える。 | The mountains are visible [spontaneous, natural sight]. |
|---|---|
| | *Context: You look up and the mountains are simply there. No effort, no obstruction. 見える is the natural verb for scenery.* |

| この映画は家で見られる。 | This movie can be watched at home [potential, situational ability]. |
|---|---|
| | *Context: Streaming availability. The movie is the thing that "can be viewed." This is a technical/possibility statement, not a description of your eyesight.* |

| 眼鏡をかければ、黒板が見える。 | If I wear glasses, I can see the blackboard [spontaneous with condition]. |
|---|---|
| | *Context: Even with the conditional, 見える remains spontaneous. The glasses restore natural vision; they do not create a learned ability.* |

!!! note "The English trap"
    English uses "can hear/see" for both. Japanese learners consistently overuse 聞ける and 見られる for natural sensory events because "can" maps to potential in their minds. The rule: if no effort, skill, or technology is involved, use 聞こえる / 見える.

!!! note "見える as "seems/looks"**
    見える can also mean "to seem" or "to look (a certain way)" when followed by an adjective: 元気に見える (looks healthy). This is a separate usage but worth noting as an N3 extension.

## In Real Life {#in-real-life}

!!! example "In Real Life — Acquired Ability"
    **Situation:** タケシ has been studying Japanese for three years. He is at a izakaya with a colleague who only speaks English. A server approaches, and Takeshi switches effortlessly.

    &gt; 日本語で注文できるよ。

    *"I can order in Japanese."*

    **Why potential fits:** Takeshi is not saying "I will order" (注文する). He is reassuring his colleague that the *capacity* exists. The potential form frames the action as a solved problem, not a future event.

    **What 注文することができます would change:** Grammatically correct, but socially stiff. It would sound like a language textbook example or a formal announcement. In a casual izakaya, できる or 〜せる is warm and confident.

    **The particle:** 日本語で (method) + 注文できる. The object (food/drinks) is omitted because it is obvious in context. If included: 料理が注文できる.

!!! example "In Real Life — Situational Possibility"
    **Situation:** ユイ is checking train schedules on her phone. Her friend suggests meeting at a café across town in twenty minutes.

    &gt; 二十分じゃ無理。間に合わないよ。

    &gt; 電車なら行けるかも。

    *"Twenty minutes is impossible. We won't make it."*
    *"If we take the train, maybe we can get there."*

    **Why 行ける fits:** 行く would commit to going. 行ける evaluates the possibility. The potential form turns the verb into a logistical assessment. The かも softens it into speculation.

    **What 行くことができます would change:** Completely unnatural here. It would sound like a robot calculating transit feasibility.

!!! example "In Real Life — Sensory Spontaneous vs. Potential"
    **Situation:** ケン is at a concert with his sister. The speakers are massive. He leans over during a quiet between songs.

    &gt; 前の方だと、ドラムの音がよく聞こえるね。

    *"From the front, you can really hear the drums, huh."*

    **Why 聞こえる fits:** The drums are audible because of the speaker system and their position. No effort or skill is required — the sound reaches their ears spontaneously. 聞ける would imply they have some technical means or trained listening skill, which is wrong for this context.

    **If Ken were a sound engineer:** この機材なら、細かい音まで聞ける。 (With this equipment, we can hear even subtle sounds.) Here 聞ける is correct because it describes technical capability.

## Grammar Analysis {#grammar-analysis}

### Function in Context

- 日本語が話せる: 日本語 is the subject (が) of the stative verb 話せる. The speaker is implied as the holder of the ability. The verb is intransitive — it does not take a を-marked object.
- この映画は家で見られる: この映画 is the subject (は). 家で is the location of possibility. 見られる is the potential of 見る. The sentence describes the movie's viewability, not an act of watching.
- 納豆が食べられるようになった: 納豆が食べられる is the potential clause. ようになった marks the transition from inability to ability. This is the standard Japanese way to say "I learned to / got used to."

## Japanese Mindset {#japanese-mindset}

Japanese potential form reveals a cultural preference for **stating capacity over declaring intent**. Saying 行ける ("I can go") is often more socially comfortable than 行く ("I will go") because it leaves the final decision open while affirming capability.

- **Ability as social lubricant.** 分かる (I understand) is a commitment. 分かっている (I am in a state of understanding) is a claim of knowledge. But 分かる lacks a natural potential form — you cannot easily say 分けれる. This grammatical gap reflects that "understanding" in Japanese is treated as a binary state, not a scalable ability.
- **Potential as humble avoidance.** Offering 手伝えますか ("Can I help?") uses potential to make the offer indirect. It asks about the speaker's capability rather than imposing their will. This is softer than 手伝いましょうか ("Shall I help?").
- **が as emotional centering.** The particle shift from を to が reorients the sentence. In 日本語が話せる, the language is grammatically spotlighted. This mirrors a Japanese tendency to let the object or situation take center stage rather than the ego-driven "I can do X."
- **The ら抜き as egalitarian speech.** Dropping the ら in casual potential (食べれる) is not just phonological laziness — it is a register signal. It says "this conversation is relaxed enough that we don't need the full formal armor of られる." The truncation itself encodes social intimacy.

## Nuance Comparison {#nuance-comparison}

| Grammar Point | Core Meaning | Subject Stance | Object Marker | Register | JLPT |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 〜られる / 〜れる | Can do / is possible | Capable / possible | が (standard), を (casual) | Neutral to casual | N4 |
| 〜ことができる | Can do / is possible (formal) | Capable / possible | を (standard) | Formal, written | N4 |
| 聞こえる / 見える | Natural sensory perception | Perceptive | が | Neutral | N3 |
| 聞ける / 見られる | Learned/technical ability | Capable | が | Neutral | N3 |
| 〜ようになる | Come to be able to | Developing | が | Neutral | N3 |
| 〜てみる | Try doing | Experimental | を | Neutral | N4 |
| 〜たい | Want to do | Desirous | を | Neutral | N5 |
| 〜られる (passive) | Is done to | Affected | が (subject), に (agent) | Neutral | N4 |
| 〜られる (honorific) | (Honorable person) does | Elevated | が (subject) | Formal | N4 |

**Potential vs. 〜ことができる:** Both express ability. 話せる is one word, natural, spoken. 話すことができる is three words, formal, written. A business email uses ご確認いただけますか (humble), but a casual text uses 見れる？ (can you see it?). Using 〜ことができる in a friendly chat sounds robotic.

**Potential vs. 〜ようになる:** 泳げる = I can swim (state). 泳げるようになった = I became able to swim (change). The latter is essential for marking acquisition milestones.

**聞こえる vs. 聞ける:** 隣の音が聞こえる = I hear the neighbor's noise (unavoidable). 隣の音が聞ける = I am capable of hearing / have access to hearing the neighbor's noise (odd unless referring to recording equipment). For natural senses, always prefer 聞こえる / 見える.

**Potential vs. passive られる:** 食べられる (potential) = can eat. 食べられる (passive) = is eaten. Context and particles disambiguate. If there is a に-marked agent, it is passive. If the subject is a high-status person with no agent, it is honorific. Otherwise, potential.

## Alternatives {#alternatives}

### 〜ことができる (formal periphrastic)

The standard alternative for formal contexts. It uses the dictionary form + ことが + できる.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Example** | 日本語を話すことができる。 |
| **English** | "I am able to speak Japanese." |
| **Intent** | Formal, explicit, safe for writing and presentations. |
| **vs. potential form** | Longer, more objective, less personal. Appropriate for resumes, academic writing, and polite self-introductions. |

!!! tip "When to use this instead"
    Use 〜ことができる in business documents, academic papers, formal speeches, or when you want to sound deliberately measured and objective.

### 〜ようになる (process of acquisition)

Marks the transition from inability to ability.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Example** | 漢字が読めるようになった。 |
| **English** | "I have become able to read kanji." |
| **Intent** | Celebrates development and effort. |
| **vs. potential form** | 読める is a state. 読めるようになった is a narrative of growth. Use the latter for milestones. |

### 〜てみる (experimental try)

Not ability, but worth contrasting.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Example** | 食べてみる。 |
| **English** | "I'll try eating it." |
| **Intent** | Tests the waters without commitment. |
| **vs. potential form** | 食べられる asks "can I?" 食べてみる asks "what if I do?" |

## Register and Formality {#register-and-formality}

=== ":material-chat-outline: Casual"
    納豆、食べれるようになった。

    *"I can eat natto now."*

    **Shift:** ら抜き (食べれる), topic-comment structure with comma, omission of subject. Warm, personal, spoken.

=== ":material-chat-outline: Casual (sensory)"
    ここから富士山が見える？

    *"Can you see Mt. Fuji from here?"*

    **Shift:** 見える (spontaneous) for natural sight. The question is soft and observational.

=== ":material-tie: Basic Polite"
    日本語が話せます。

    *"I can speak Japanese."*

    **Shift:** Standard potential with polite ます. が marking. The baseline for self-introductions.

=== ":material-tie: Basic Polite (formal alternative)"
    日本語を話すことができます。

    *"I am able to speak Japanese."*

    **Shift:** 〜ことができます. Slightly stiffer but appropriate for job interviews and formal introductions.

=== ":material-briefcase-outline: Extra Polite"
    ご確認いただけますか。

    *"Could you please confirm?"*

    **Shift:** This is not potential form — it is humble request. But it lives in the same semantic neighborhood as "can/could." Included here to show how Japanese replaces bare potential with humble grammar in upward communication.

=== ":material-briefcase-outline: Extra Polite (written)"
    本書はオンラインでもお読みいただけます。

    *"This book can also be read online."*

    **Shift:** お読みいただけます combines humble prefix (お) + potential of いただく (to receive). This is service-industry standard for "can be used/read/accessed."

## Examples {#examples}

| 漢字が読める。 | I can read kanji [acquired ability]. |
|---|---|
| | *Context: A learner announcing a milestone. The が marks kanji as the subject of readability.* |

| この部屋から海が見える。 | The ocean is visible from this room [spontaneous sight]. |
|---|---|
| | *Context: Real estate showing. 見える describes natural line of sight, not capability.* |

| 明日なら行ける。 | If it's tomorrow, I can go [situational possibility]. |
|---|---|
| | *Context: Schedule check. The potential form evaluates logistical feasibility.* |

| 納豆が食べられるようになった。 | I've become able to eat natto [acquired tolerance]. |
|---|---|
| | *Context: Common expression for overcoming food aversions. ようになった is the standard acquisition frame.* |

| このアプリは海外でも使える。 | This app can be used even overseas [technical possibility]. |
|---|---|
| | *Context: Product description. 使える is the potential of 使う, describing the app's functional range.* |

| ピアノが弾ける。 | I can play the piano [learned skill]. |
|---|---|
| | *Context: Skill statement. 弾ける is the potential of 弾く. The が marks the instrument as the subject of playability.* |

| 電車なら間に合えるかも。 | If we take the train, we might be able to make it [situational]. |
|---|---|
| | *Context: 間に合える is the potential of 間に合う. The かも softens the possibility into speculation.* |

| 眼鏡をかければ、文字が読める。 | If I wear glasses, I can read the letters [conditional potential]. |
|---|---|
| | *Context: 読める is potential. The conditional ば frame links the ability to a prerequisite.* |

| 子供の頃は泳げなかった。 | I couldn't swim when I was a child [past negative potential]. |
|---|---|
| | *Context: Past inability. 泳げなかった is natural and compact. 泳ぐことができなかった would be overly formal for a childhood memory.* |

| この音楽はどこでも聞ける。 | This music can be listened to anywhere [technical potential]. |
|---|---|
| | *Context: Streaming service. 聞ける (not 聞こえる) because it describes platform availability, not auditory physiology.* |

## Common Mistakes {#common-mistakes}

### 1. Using passive a-row instead of potential e-row for godan

!!! failure "Stem error"
    書かれる (when meaning "can write")

    ~書かれる is the passive/honorific form of 書く ("is written"). The potential requires the e-row: 書ける.~

!!! success "Correct"
    書ける (can write)

### 2. Confusing potential られる with passive/honorific

!!! failure "Ambiguity error"
    先生が食べられた。

    ~Without context, this is parsed as passive: "The teacher was eaten." If you mean potential ("The teacher could eat"), the object should be marked by が, and the context should make the teacher's capability clear.~

!!! success "Correct (potential)"
    先生は納豆が食べられた。 (The teacher could eat natto.)

!!! success "Correct (passive)"
    先生が子供に食べられた。 (The teacher was eaten by a child. — nonsense but grammatically clear.)

### 3. Using を in formal/written contexts

!!! failure "Register error"
    日本語を話せます。

    ~Casual を is acceptable in speech, but in writing and formal contexts, が is standard. Using を in a business email or JLPT answer sheet is marked wrong.~

!!! success "Correct"
    日本語が話せます。

### 4. Using potential with stative verbs

!!! failure "State verb error"
    あるられる / いられる (meaning "can exist")

    ~ある and いる are stative verbs of existence. They do not take potential form in natural Japanese. For "can be," use あり得る (advanced) or rephrase with 可能性がある.~

!!! success "Correct"
    ここにいる。 (I am here.)
    ここにいられる。 (I can stay here — acceptable but rare; usually implies permission rather than existential ability.)

!!! failure "State verb error"
    分かれる (meaning "can understand")

    ~分かる already implies comprehension. Its potential form is not used. For "can understand," simply use 分かる or 理解できる (understand intellectually).~

!!! success "Correct"
    分かる。 (I understand.)
    理解できる。 (I can comprehend / I am capable of understanding.)

### 5. Overusing 〜ことができる in casual speech

!!! failure "Register mismatch"
    友達に「寿司を食べることができるよ」

    ~Grammatically perfect, socially robotic. Friends say 寿司食べれる or 寿司食べられるの.~

!!! success "Correct"
    寿司、食べれるよ。

### 6. Using 聞ける/見られる for natural sensory events

!!! failure "Sensory category error"
    隣の部屋の音が聞ける。

    ~This implies you have the technical means or trained skill to hear the neighbor. If the sound simply reaches your ears, 聞こえる is correct.~

!!! success "Correct"
    隣の部屋の音が聞こえる。

## Hidden Points {#hidden-points}

- **Potential verbs are technically adjectives in semantic behavior.** They describe states, not actions. This is why they take が, resist を, and can be modified by adverbs of degree: よく話せる (can speak well), 少し読める (can read a little).
- **The ら抜き phenomenon is accelerating.** Younger speakers increasingly use 食べれない even in semi-formal contexts where 食べられない was once mandatory. The boundary is shifting, but formal writing remains conservative.
- **Some godan potential forms are identical to independent verbs.** 売れる (potential of 売る) = "can sell"; 売れる (intransitive) = "sells well." 切れる (potential of 切る) = "can cut"; 切れる (intransitive) = "breaks / runs out." Context and transitivity disambiguate.
- **Negative potential + ようになった is a common recovery narrative.** 泳げなくて、練習して、泳げるようになった. The arc from inability to ability is a classic Japanese self-improvement story structure.
- **Potential form can express permission indirectly.** ここで写真が撮れる does not just mean "I have the skill to take photos here" — it often means "Photos are permitted here." The grammar blurs ability, possibility, and permission.
- **The absence of potential for 分かる is philosophically loaded.** Japanese treats understanding as an all-or-nothing state rather than a scalable skill. You do not "partially understand" with 分かる — you either 分かる or you do not. For gradations, use 理解できる or 分かりかけている.
- **できる is the most versatile potential verb.** It serves as the potential of する, but it also functions independently: この部屋でできる (can be done in this room), 彼ならできる (if it's him, he can do it), できない (impossible / can't).
- **Potential + だけ is a humble brag.** 日本語が話せるだけで、就職できた. "Just because I could speak Japanese, I got the job." The だけ downplays the achievement while the potential form states the capability.
- **The particle を in casual potential is a generational marker.** Older speakers often correct を-usage; younger speakers use it naturally. In 20 years, を may become fully standard in potential sentences, but が will remain the conservative standard.
- **見える and 聞こえる can express social perception.** 彼の努力が見える = "His effort is visible (to me)." This is metaphorical sensory perception — you "see" his effort with your mind's eye. The spontaneous form extends beyond literal senses.

## Learner Stage {#learner-stage}

| Stage | What It Looks Like |
|-------|-------------------|
| **Now — N4** | Form godan potential correctly (e-row shift); form ichidan potential with られる; use できる for する; use が for the object; produce basic self-introductions (〜が話せます) |
| **Developing — N4→N3** | Deploy ら抜き naturally in casual speech; distinguish 聞こえる/見える from 聞ける/見られる; use 〜ようになった for acquisition narratives; avoid を in formal writing |
| **Automatic — N3→N2** | Potential forms arrive as state-descriptions, not conjugated verbs. You hear 行ける and immediately parse it as "possible" rather than "go + potential suffix." The が/を choice is intuitive. |
| **Mastery** | You control the full ability spectrum: compact potential (話せる) for casual speech, 〜ことができる for formal writing, 〜ようになる for growth narratives, 聞こえる/見える for natural senses, and ら抜き as a precise register signal. You never confuse godan potential with passive, and you know which stative verbs resist potentialization. |
