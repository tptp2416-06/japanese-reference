# <ruby>可能形<rt>かのうけい</rt></ruby> — Potential Form

## <ruby>日本語<rt>にほんご</rt></ruby>が<ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>せるようになった。

---

## JLPT Level {#jlpt-level}

!!! abstract "JLPT Level — N4 Core / N3 Extended"
    Potential formation itself is solid N4 — the mechanical shift of godan verbs to the e-row and the られる attachment for ichidan verbs. But the true mastery lies in N3 territory: the が/を particle calibration, the ら抜き spoken disambiguation, the spontaneous/potential split with sensory verbs (<ruby>聞こえる<rt>きこえる</rt></ruby> vs <ruby>聞ける<rt>きける</rt></ruby>, <ruby>見える<rt>みえる</rt></ruby> vs <ruby>見られる<rt>みられる</rt></ruby>), and the social decision of when to use compact potential form versus periphrastic 〜ことができる.

    | Tier | Pattern | Description | Example |
    |------|---------|-------------|---------|
    | N5 — Foundation | Plain verb forms | Active execution | <ruby>日本語<rt>にほんご</rt></ruby>を<ruby>話す<rt>はなす</rt></ruby>。 |
    | N5 — Foundation | 〜たい | Desire to do | <ruby>日本語<rt>にほんご</rt></ruby>を<ruby>話したい<rt>はなしたい</rt></ruby>。 |
    | N4 — Foundation | 〜ている | Progressive / experiential | <ruby>日本語<rt>にほんご</rt></ruby>を<ruby>話している<rt>はなしている</rt></ruby>。 |
    | **N4 — Core ← You are here** | **〜られる / 〜れる** | **Inherent ability / possibility** | **<ruby>日本語<rt>にほんご</rt></ruby>が<ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>せる。** |
    | N4 — Core | 〜ことができる | Formal periphrastic potential | <ruby>日本語<rt>にほんご</rt></ruby>を<ruby>話す<rt>はなす</rt></ruby>ことができる。 |
    | N3 — Extended | **が vs. を** | **Object marking shift in potential** | **<ruby>日本語<rt>にほんご</rt></ruby>が<ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>せる。** |
    | N3 — Extended | **ら抜き言葉** | **Spoken disambiguation of られる** | **<ruby>食べれる<rt>たべれる</rt></ruby>。** |
    | N3 — Extended | **聞こえる / 見える** | **Spontaneous sensory capability** | **<ruby>音<rt>おと</rt></ruby>が<ruby>聞こえる<rt>きこえる</rt></ruby>。** |
    | N3 — Extended | **〜ようになる** | **Process of acquiring ability** | **<ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>せるようになった。** |
    | N2 — Awareness | 〜得る (うる/える) | Literary/formal potential | あり<ruby>得る<rt>える</rt></ruby>。 |

## Quick Summary {#quick-summary}

!!! tip "At a glance"
    - Potential form transforms an action verb into a **state of capability**. <ruby>話す<rt>はなす</rt></ruby> (to speak) becomes <ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>せる (can speak). The verb is no longer about doing — it is about **being able to do**.
    - **Godan verbs** undergo a stem vowel shift: the final う-sound moves to the え-row, and る is added. <ruby>書く<rt>かく</rt></ruby> → <ruby>書<rt>か</rt></ruby>ける, <ruby>読む<rt>よむ</rt></ruby> → <ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>める, <ruby>泳ぐ<rt>およぐ</rt></ruby> → <ruby>泳<rt>およ</rt></ruby>げる.
    - **Ichidan verbs** attach られる to the stem. <ruby>食べる<rt>たべる</rt></ruby> → <ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べられる, <ruby>見る<rt>みる</rt></ruby> → <ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>られる. In casual speech, the ら drops: <ruby>食べれる<rt>たべれる</rt></ruby>, <ruby>見れる<rt>みれる</rt></ruby>.
    - **する** becomes できる. **来る** becomes <ruby>来られる<rt>こられる</rt></ruby> or <ruby>来れる<rt>これる</rt></ruby> in casual speech.
    - The object of a potential verb is marked by **が**, not を. <ruby>日本語<rt>にほんご</rt></ruby>**が**<ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>せる. This is because potential verbs are grammatically **stative/intransitive** — they describe a property, not an action.
    - **聞こえる** and **見える** are **not** potential forms. They are spontaneous verbs describing natural sensory perception. Their potential counterparts (<ruby>聞ける<rt>きける</rt></ruby>, <ruby>見られる<rt>みられる</rt></ruby>) describe learned/technical ability or possibility.
    - Potential form is **natural and spoken**. 〜ことができる is **formal and written**. Using the latter in casual conversation sounds stiff or foreign.

## Core Meaning {#core-meaning}

!!! note "Core Rule"
    Use the potential form when you want to express that the subject **possesses the inherent or acquired capacity** to perform an action, or that an action is **objectively possible** given circumstances. The potential form does not describe an action taking place — it describes a **state of ability**.

| <ruby>日本語<rt>にほんご</rt></ruby>が<ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>せる。 | I can speak Japanese [acquired ability]. |
|---|---|
| | *Context: The speaker has learned Japanese. The が marks the language as the thing that "can be spoken."* |

| この<ruby>部屋<rt>へや</rt></ruby>から<ruby>海<rt>うみ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>見える<rt>みえる</rt></ruby>。 | The ocean is visible from this room [natural sensory perception]. |
|---|---|
| | *Context: 見える is spontaneous, not potential. The view is simply there; no effort is required.* |

| この<ruby>部屋<rt>へや</rt></ruby>から<ruby>海<rt>うみ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>見られる<rt>みられる</rt></ruby>。 | The ocean can be seen from this room [technical possibility]. |
|---|---|
| | *Context: 見られる is potential. It implies that the room's position or design permits viewing, or that the viewer has the visual capacity.* |

| <ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby>なら<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>ける。 | If it's tomorrow, I can go [situational possibility]. |
|---|---|
| | *Context: 行ける is the potential of 行く. The ability here is not physical fitness but schedule availability.* |

| <ruby>納豆<rt>なっとう</rt></ruby>が<ruby>食べられる<rt>たべられる</rt></ruby>ようになった。 | I have become able to eat natto [acquired tolerance]. |
|---|---|
| | *Context: 食べられる is potential. The ようになった frames the change from inability to ability — a common and natural collocation.* |

## The Godan Transformation {#the-godan-transformation}

Godan potential formation is one of the most dramatic morphological changes in Japanese. The final syllable of the dictionary form shifts to its e-row equivalent, and る is added. This is not a suffix — it is a **stem mutation**.

| Dictionary | Final Syllable | E-Row Shift | Potential | Meaning |
|------------|---------------|-------------|-----------|---------|
| <ruby>書く<rt>かく</rt></ruby> | く | け | <ruby>書ける<rt>かける</rt></ruby> | can write |
| <ruby>話す<rt>はなす</rt></ruby> | す | せ | <ruby>話せる<rt>はなせる</rt></ruby> | can speak |
| <ruby>待つ<rt>まつ</rt></ruby> | つ | て | <ruby>待てる<rt>まてる</rt></ruby> | can wait |
| <ruby>死ぬ<rt>しぬ</rt></ruby> | ぬ | ね | <ruby>死ねる<rt>しねる</rt></ruby> | can die |
| <ruby>読む<rt>よむ</rt></ruby> | む | め | <ruby>読める<rt>よめる</rt></ruby> | can read |
| <ruby>遊ぶ<rt>あそぶ</rt></ruby> | ぶ | べ | <ruby>遊べる<rt>あそべる</rt></ruby> | can play |
| <ruby>泳ぐ<rt>およぐ</rt></ruby> | ぐ | げ | <ruby>泳げる<rt>およげる</rt></ruby> | can swim |
| <ruby>急ぐ<rt>いそぐ</rt></ruby> | ぐ | げ | <ruby>急げる<rt>いそげる</rt></ruby> | can hurry |
| <ruby>走る<rt>はしる</rt></ruby> | る | れ | <ruby>走れる<rt>はしれる</rt></ruby> | can run |
| <ruby>帰る<rt>かえる</rt></ruby> | る | れ | <ruby>帰れる<rt>かえれる</rt></ruby> | can return |
| <ruby>入る<rt>はいる</rt></ruby> | る | れ | <ruby>入れる<rt>はいれる</rt></ruby> | can enter |
| <ruby>買う<rt>かう</rt></ruby> | う | え | <ruby>買える<rt>かえる</rt></ruby> | can buy |
| <ruby>打つ<rt>うつ</rt></ruby> | つ | て | <ruby>打てる<rt>うてる</rt></ruby> | can hit |
| <ruby>持つ<rt>もつ</rt></ruby> | つ | て | <ruby>持てる<rt>もてる</rt></ruby> | can hold |
| <ruby>売る<rt>うる</rt></ruby> | る | れ | <ruby>売れる<rt>うれる</rt></ruby> | can sell |
| <ruby>取る<rt>とる</rt></ruby> | る | れ | <ruby>取れる<rt>とれる</rt></ruby> | can take |
| <ruby>飛ぶ<rt>とぶ</rt></ruby> | ぶ | べ | <ruby>飛べる<rt>とべる</rt></ruby> | can fly |
| <ruby>知る<rt>しる</rt></ruby> | る | れ | <ruby>知れる<rt>しれる</rt></ruby> | can know |
| <ruby>眠る<rt>ねむる</rt></ruby> | る | れ | <ruby>眠れる<rt>ねむれる</rt></ruby> | can sleep |

!!! note "The godan る-ending verbs"
    <ruby>走る<rt>はしる</rt></ruby>, <ruby>帰る<rt>かえる</rt></ruby>, <ruby>入る<rt>はいる</rt></ruby>, <ruby>売る<rt>うる</rt></ruby>, <ruby>取る<rt>とる</rt></ruby>, <ruby>知る<rt>しる</rt></ruby>, <ruby>眠る<rt>ねむる</rt></ruby> are godan verbs despite ending in る. They follow the godan pattern (e-row shift), not the ichidan pattern (stem + られる). <ruby>走られる<rt>はしられる</rt></ruby> would be passive/honorific, not potential.

!!! warning "入れる is a homonym trap"
    <ruby>入る<rt>はいる</rt></ruby> (hairu, godan, to enter) → <ruby>入れる<rt>はいれる</rt></ruby> (haireru, potential, can enter). But <ruby>入れる<rt>いれる</rt></ruby> (ireru, ichidan, to put in) is a completely different verb meaning "to insert/put in." In writing they are identical; in speech the pitch accent differs. Context is the only guide.

!!! warning "売れる is also an independent intransitive verb"
    <ruby>売れる<rt>うれる</rt></ruby> (ureru) as potential means "can sell (something)." But <ruby>売れる<rt>うれる</rt></ruby> is also an intransitive verb meaning "to sell well / to be in demand." この<ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt></ruby>は<ruby>売れる<rt>うれる</rt></ruby> can mean either "I can sell this book" or "This book sells well." Context and particles disambiguate.

## The Ichidan Dilemma {#the-ichidan-dilemma}

Ichidan verbs form potential by attaching られる to the stem. This creates the infamous **三役のられる** (three-function られる) ambiguity: passive, honorific, and potential all share the same shape.

| Dictionary | Stem | Standard Potential | Casual Potential (ら抜き) |
|------------|------|-------------------|-------------------------|
| <ruby>食べる<rt>たべる</rt></ruby> | <ruby>食べ<rt>たべ</rt></ruby> | <ruby>食べられる<rt>たべられる</rt></ruby> | <ruby>食べれる<rt>たべれる</rt></ruby> |
| <ruby>見る<rt>みる</rt></ruby> | <ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby> | <ruby>見られる<rt>みられる</rt></ruby> | <ruby>見れる<rt>みれる</rt></ruby> |
| <ruby>起きる<rt>おきる</rt></ruby> | <ruby>起き<rt>おき</rt></ruby> | <ruby>起きられる<rt>おきられる</rt></ruby> | <ruby>起きれる<rt>おきれる</rt></ruby> |
| <ruby>寝る<rt>ねる</rt></ruby> | <ruby>寝<rt>ね</rt></ruby> | <ruby>寝られる<rt>ねられる</rt></ruby> | <ruby>寝れる<rt>ねれる</rt></ruby> |
| <ruby>借りる<rt>かりる</rt></ruby> | <ruby>借り<rt>かり</rt></ruby> | <ruby>借りられる<rt>かりられる</rt></ruby> | <ruby>借りれる<rt>かりれる</rt></ruby> |
| いる (<ruby>居る<rt>いる</rt></ruby>) | い | いられる | いれる |

!!! note "ら抜き is not slang — it is standard spoken Japanese"
    Dropping the ら in ichidan potential (<ruby>食べれる<rt>たべれる</rt></ruby>, <ruby>見れる<rt>みれる</rt></ruby>, <ruby>来れる<rt>これる</rt></ruby>) is nearly universal in casual speech. It is not lazy or incorrect in that register — it is the natural form. However, it is **not accepted in formal writing, academic prose, or business documents.** In those contexts, always use the full られる.

!!! note "Disambiguation strategy"
    Native speakers rely on context and particles to distinguish the three られる functions:
    - **Potential:** Object marked by が (or を in casual speech). Subject is the capable entity. No に-marked agent.
    - **Passive:** Agent marked by に. Subject is the recipient of action.
    - **Honorific:** Subject is a high-status person performing the action. No agent.

    In speech, potential られる often drops the ら, instantly resolving the ambiguity.

### Irregular Verbs

| Dictionary | Standard Potential | Casual Potential | Notes |
|------------|-------------------|------------------|-------|
| する | できる | — | できる is a standalone verb, not a suffix pattern. |
| <ruby>来る<rt>くる</rt></ruby> | <ruby>来られる<rt>こられる</rt></ruby> | <ruby>来れる<rt>これる</rt></ruby> | <ruby>来れる<rt>これる</rt></ruby> is extremely common in speech to avoid ambiguity with honorific <ruby>来られる<rt>こられる</rt></ruby>. |

!!! warning "Do not say できられる"
    する → できる. できる is already a potential/state verb. Adding られる (できられる) is ungrammatical/unnatural. If you need "will be able to," use できるようになる.

## The Particle Shift {#the-particle-shift}

Potential verbs are grammatically **stative and intransitive**. They describe a property or state, not an action directed at an object. Therefore, the object of the original verb is re-marked with **が**, not を.

| Active | Potential | Particle Logic |
|--------|-----------|---------------|
| <ruby>日本語<rt>にほんご</rt></ruby>**を**<ruby>話す<rt>はなす</rt></ruby> | <ruby>日本語<rt>にほんご</rt></ruby>**が**<ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>せる | The language is now the subject of the ability-state. |
| ピアノ**を**<ruby>弾く<rt>ひく</rt></ruby> | ピアノ**が**<ruby>弾ける<rt>ひける</rt></ruby> | The piano is the thing that "can be played." |
| <ruby>漢字<rt>かんじ</rt></ruby>**を**<ruby>読む<rt>よむ</rt></ruby> | <ruby>漢字<rt>かんじ</rt></ruby>**が**<ruby>読める<rt>よめる</rt></ruby> | The characters possess the property of being readable by the subject. |

!!! note "Why が and not を?"
    In active sentences, を marks the direct object — the thing being acted upon. In potential sentences, the verb is no longer transitive. <ruby>話せる<rt>はなせる</rt></ruby> does not mean "to speak" (an action); it means "can speak" (a state). The thing that "can be spoken" is grammatically the subject of that state, hence が.

!!! note "Casual を-usage"
    In modern casual speech, many native speakers say <ruby>日本語<rt>にほんご</rt></ruby>**を**<ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>せる or ピアノ**を**<ruby>弾ける<rt>ひける</rt></ruby>. This is increasingly accepted conversationally, especially among younger speakers. However, in writing, formal speech, and JLPT testing, **が** remains the standard. Learners should master が first, then recognize casual を as register variation.

!!! warning "Do not use both を and が for the same object"
    !!! failure "Incorrect"
        <ruby>日本語<rt>にほんご</rt></ruby>をが<ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>せる。
    !!! success "Correct"
        <ruby>日本語<rt>にほんご</rt></ruby>が<ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>せる。

## Sensory Verbs: 聞こえる・見える vs. 聞ける・見られる {#sensory-verbs}

This is the most consequential N3 distinction in the potential landscape. Japanese separates **natural sensory perception** (spontaneous) from **learned or technical ability** (potential).

| Spontaneous (自然発生) | Potential (可能形) | Core Difference |
|------------------------|-------------------|-----------------|
| **<ruby>聞こえる<rt>きこえる</rt></ruby>** | **<ruby>聞ける<rt>きける</rt></ruby>** | |
| Can hear (no effort) | Can listen / is audible (ability or tech) | <ruby>聞こえる<rt>きこえる</rt></ruby> = your ears work. <ruby>聞ける<rt>きける</rt></ruby> = you have access or skill. |
| **<ruby>見える<rt>みえる</rt></ruby>** | **<ruby>見られる<rt>みられる</rt></ruby> / <ruby>見れる<rt>みれる</rt></ruby>** | |
| Can see / is visible (no effort) | Can watch / can see (ability or arrangement) | <ruby>見える<rt>みえる</rt></ruby> = your eyes work / line of sight exists. <ruby>見られる<rt>みられる</rt></ruby> = you have the means or permission. |

| <ruby>隣<rt>となり</rt></ruby>の<ruby>部屋<rt>へや</rt></ruby>の<ruby>音<rt>おと</rt></ruby>が<ruby>聞こえる<rt>きこえる</rt></ruby>。 | I can hear the noise from the next room [spontaneous, natural hearing]. |
|---|---|
| | *Context: Your ears pick up the sound automatically. No conscious effort. <ruby>聞こえる<rt>きこえる</rt></ruby> is the only natural choice here.* |

| このラジオは<ruby>海外<rt>かいがい</rt></ruby>でも<ruby>聞ける<rt>きける</rt></ruby>。 | This radio can be heard even overseas [potential, technical possibility]. |
|---|---|
| | *Context: The broadcast reaches overseas. This is about signal range and technical capability, not your ears. <ruby>聞こえる<rt>きこえる</rt></ruby> would sound odd — it would imply the radio noise naturally reaches your ears without equipment.* |

| <ruby>山<rt>やま</rt></ruby>が<ruby>見える<rt>みえる</rt></ruby>。 | The mountains are visible [spontaneous, natural sight]. |
|---|---|
| | *Context: You look up and the mountains are simply there. No effort, no obstruction. <ruby>見える<rt>みえる</rt></ruby> is the natural verb for scenery.* |

| この<ruby>映画<rt>えいが</rt></ruby>は<ruby>家<rt>いえ</rt></ruby>で<ruby>見られる<rt>みられる</rt></ruby>。 | This movie can be watched at home [potential, situational ability]. |
|---|---|
| | *Context: Streaming availability. The movie is the thing that "can be viewed." This is a technical/possibility statement, not a description of your eyesight.* |

| <ruby>眼鏡<rt>めがね</rt></ruby>をかければ、<ruby>黒板<rt>こくばん</rt></ruby>が<ruby>見える<rt>みえる</rt></ruby>。 | If I wear glasses, I can see the blackboard [spontaneous with condition]. |
|---|---|
| | *Context: Even with the conditional, <ruby>見える<rt>みえる</rt></ruby> remains spontaneous. The glasses restore natural vision; they do not create a learned ability.* |

!!! note "The English trap"
    English uses "can hear/see" for both. Japanese learners consistently overuse <ruby>聞ける<rt>きける</rt></ruby> and <ruby>見られる<rt>みられる</rt></ruby> for natural sensory events because "can" maps to potential in their minds. The rule: if no effort, skill, or technology is involved, use <ruby>聞こえる<rt>きこえる</rt></ruby> / <ruby>見える<rt>みえる</rt></ruby>.

!!! note "見える as "seems/looks""
    <ruby>見える<rt>みえる</rt></ruby> can also mean "to seem" or "to look (a certain way)" when followed by an adjective: <ruby>元気<rt>げんき</rt></ruby>に<ruby>見える<rt>みえる</rt></ruby> (looks healthy). This is a separate usage but worth noting as an N3 extension.

## In Real Life {#in-real-life}

!!! example "In Real Life — Acquired Ability"
    **Situation:** タケシ has been studying Japanese for three years. He is at a izakaya with a colleague who only speaks English. A server approaches, and Takeshi switches effortlessly.

    > <ruby>日本語<rt>にほんご</rt></ruby>で<ruby>注文<rt>ちゅうもん</rt></ruby>できるよ。

    *"I can order in Japanese."*

    **Why potential fits:** Takeshi is not saying "I will order" (<ruby>注文<rt>ちゅうもん</rt></ruby>する). He is reassuring his colleague that the *capacity* exists. The potential form frames the action as a solved problem, not a future event.

    **What 注文することができます would change:** Grammatically correct, but socially stiff. It would sound like a language textbook example or a formal announcement. In a casual izakaya, できる or 〜せる is warm and confident.

    **The particle:** <ruby>日本語<rt>にほんご</rt></ruby>で (method) + <ruby>注文<rt>ちゅうもん</rt></ruby>できる. The object (food/drinks) is omitted because it is obvious in context. If included: <ruby>料理<rt>りょうり</rt></ruby>が<ruby>注文<rt>ちゅうもん</rt></ruby>できる.

!!! example "In Real Life — Situational Possibility"
    **Situation:** ユい is checking train schedules on her phone. Her friend suggests meeting at a café across town in twenty minutes.

    > <ruby>二十分<rt>にじゅっぷん</rt></ruby>じゃ<ruby>無理<rt>むり</rt></ruby>。<ruby>間に合わ<rt>まにあわ</rt></ruby>ないよ。

    > <ruby>電車<rt>でんしゃ</rt></ruby>なら<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>けるかも。

    *"Twenty minutes is impossible. We won't make it."*
    *"If we take the train, maybe we can get there."*

    **Why 行ける fits:** <ruby>行く<rt>いく</rt></ruby> would commit to going. <ruby>行ける<rt>いける</rt></ruby> evaluates the possibility. The potential form turns the verb into a logistical assessment. The かも softens it into speculation.

    **What 行くことができます would change:** Completely unnatural here. It would sound like a robot calculating transit feasibility.

!!! example "In Real Life — Sensory Spontaneous vs. Potential"
    **Situation:** ケン is at a concert with his sister. The speakers are massive. He leans over during a quiet between songs.

    > <ruby>前<rt>まえ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>方<rt>ほう</rt></ruby>だと、ドラムの<ruby>音<rt>おと</rt></ruby>がよく<ruby>聞こえる<rt>きこえる</rt></ruby>ね。

    *"From the front, you can really hear the drums, huh."*

    **Why 聞こえる fits:** The drums are audible because of the speaker system and their position. No effort or skill is required — the sound reaches their ears spontaneously. <ruby>聞ける<rt>きける</rt></ruby> would imply they have some technical means or trained listening skill, which is wrong for this context.

    **If Ken were a sound engineer:** この<ruby>機材<rt>きざい</rt></ruby>なら、<ruby>細かい<rt>こまかい</rt></ruby><ruby>音<rt>おと</rt></ruby>まで<ruby>聞ける<rt>きける</rt></ruby>。 (With this equipment, we can hear even subtle sounds.) Here <ruby>聞ける<rt>きける</rt></ruby> is correct because it describes technical capability.

## Grammar Analysis {#grammar-analysis}

### Function in Context

- <ruby>日本語<rt>にほんご</rt></ruby>が<ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>せる: <ruby>日本語<rt>にほんご</rt></ruby> is the subject (が) of the stative verb <ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>せる. The speaker is implied as the holder of the ability. The verb is intransitive — it does not take a を-marked object.
- この<ruby>映画<rt>えいが</rt></ruby>は<ruby>家<rt>いえ</rt></ruby>で<ruby>見られる<rt>みられる</rt></ruby>: この<ruby>映画<rt>えいが</rt></ruby> is the subject (は). <ruby>家<rt>いえ</rt></ruby>で is the location of possibility. <ruby>見られる<rt>みられる</rt></ruby> is the potential of <ruby>見る<rt>みる</rt></ruby>. The sentence describes the movie's viewability, not an act of watching.
- <ruby>納豆<rt>なっとう</rt></ruby>が<ruby>食べられる<rt>たべられる</rt></ruby>ようになった: <ruby>納豆<rt>なっとう</rt></ruby>が<ruby>食べられる<rt>たべられる</rt></ruby> is the potential clause. ようになった marks the transition from inability to ability. This is the standard Japanese way to say "I learned to / got used to."

### Construction {#construction}

## Japanese Mindset {#japanese-mindset}

Japanese potential form reveals a cultural preference for **stating capacity over declaring intent**. Saying <ruby>行ける<rt>いける</rt></ruby> ("I can go") is often more socially comfortable than <ruby>行く<rt>いく</rt></ruby> ("I will go") because it leaves the final decision open while affirming capability.

- **Ability as social lubricant.** <ruby>分かる<rt>わかる</rt></ruby> (I understand) is a commitment. <ruby>分かっている<rt>わかっている</rt></ruby> (I am in a state of understanding) is a claim of knowledge. But <ruby>分かる<rt>わかる</rt></ruby> lacks a natural potential form — you cannot easily say <ruby>分けれる<rt>わけれる</rt></ruby> (ungrammatical). This grammatical gap reflects that "understanding" in Japanese is treated as a binary state, not a scalable ability.
- **Potential as humble avoidance.** Offering <ruby>手伝えます<rt>てつだえます</rt></ruby>か ("Can I help?") uses potential to make the offer indirect. It asks about the speaker's capability rather than imposing their will. This is softer than <ruby>手伝いましょう<rt>てつだいましょう</rt></ruby>か ("Shall I help?").
- **が as emotional centering.** The particle shift from を to が reorients the sentence. In <ruby>日本語<rt>にほんご</rt></ruby>が<ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>せる, the language is grammatically spotlighted. This mirrors a Japanese tendency to let the object or situation take center stage rather than the ego-driven "I can do X."
- **The ら抜き as egalitarian speech.** Dropping the ら in casual potential (<ruby>食べれる<rt>たべれる</rt></ruby>) is not just phonological laziness — it is a register signal. It says "this conversation is relaxed enough that we don't need the full formal armor of られる." The truncation itself encodes social intimacy.

## Nuance Comparison {#nuance-comparison}

| Grammar Point | Core Meaning | Subject Stance | Object Marker | Register | JLPT |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 〜られる / 〜れる | Can do / is possible | Capable / possible | が (standard), を (casual) | Neutral to casual | N4 |
| 〜ことができる | Can do / is possible (formal) | Capable / possible | を (standard) | Formal, written | N4 |
| <ruby>聞こえる<rt>きこえる</rt></ruby> / <ruby>見える<rt>みえる</rt></ruby> | Natural sensory perception | Perceptive | が | Neutral | N3 |
| <ruby>聞ける<rt>きける</rt></ruby> / <ruby>見られる<rt>みられる</rt></ruby> | Learned/technical ability | Capable | が | Neutral | N3 |
| 〜ようになる | Come to be able to | Developing | が | Neutral | N3 |
| 〜てみる | Try doing | Experimental | を | Neutral | N4 |
| 〜たい | Want to do | Desirous | を | Neutral | N5 |
| 〜られる (passive) | Is done to | Affected | が (subject), に (agent) | Neutral | N4 |
| 〜られる (honorific) | (Honorable person) does | Elevated | が (subject) | Formal | N4 |

**Potential vs. 〜ことができる:** Both express ability. <ruby>話せる<rt>はなせる</rt></ruby> is one word, natural, spoken. <ruby>話す<rt>はなす</rt></ruby>ことができる is three words, formal, written. A business email uses ご<ruby>確認<rt>かくにん</rt></ruby>いただけますか (humble), but a casual text uses <ruby>見れる<rt>みれる</rt></ruby>？ (can you see it?). Using 〜ことができる in a friendly chat sounds robotic.

**Potential vs. 〜ようになる:** <ruby>泳げる<rt>およげる</rt></ruby> = I can swim (state). <ruby>泳げる<rt>およげる</rt></ruby>ようになった = I became able to swim (change). The latter is essential for marking acquisition milestones.

**<ruby>聞こえる<rt>きこえる</rt></ruby> vs. <ruby>聞ける<rt>きける</rt></ruby>:** <ruby>隣<rt>となり</rt></ruby>の<ruby>音<rt>おと</rt></ruby>が<ruby>聞こえる<rt>きこえる</rt></ruby> = I hear the neighbor's noise (unavoidable). <ruby>隣<rt>となり</rt></ruby>の<ruby>音<rt>おと</rt></ruby>が<ruby>聞ける<rt>きける</rt></ruby> = I am capable of hearing / have access to hearing the neighbor's noise (odd unless referring to recording equipment). For natural senses, always prefer <ruby>聞こえる<rt>きこえる</rt></ruby> / <ruby>見える<rt>みえる</rt></ruby>.

**Potential vs. passive られる:** <ruby>食べられる<rt>たべられる</rt></ruby> (potential) = can eat. <ruby>食べられる<rt>たべられる</rt></ruby> (passive) = is eaten. Context and particles disambiguate. If there is a に-marked agent, it is passive. If the subject is a high-status person with no agent, it is honorific. Otherwise, potential.

## Alternatives {#alternatives}

### 〜ことができる (formal periphrastic)

The standard alternative for formal contexts. It uses the dictionary form + ことが + できる.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Example** | <ruby>日本語<rt>にほんご</rt></ruby>を<ruby>話す<rt>はなす</rt></ruby>ことができる。 |
| **English** | "I am able to speak Japanese." |
| **Intent** | Formal, explicit, safe for writing and presentations. |
| **vs. potential form** | Longer, more objective, less personal. Appropriate for resumes, academic writing, and polite self-introductions. |

!!! tip "When to use this instead"
    Use 〜ことができる in business documents, academic papers, formal speeches, or when you want to sound deliberately measured and objective.

### 〜ようになる (process of acquisition)

Marks the transition from inability to ability.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Example** | <ruby>漢字<rt>かんじ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>読める<rt>よめる</rt></ruby>ようになった。 |
| **English** | "I have become able to read kanji." |
| **Intent** | Celebrates development and effort. |
| **vs. potential form** | <ruby>読める<rt>よめる</rt></ruby> is a state. <ruby>読める<rt>よめる</rt></ruby>ようになった is a narrative of growth. Use the latter for milestones. |

### 〜てみる (experimental try)

Not ability, but worth contrasting.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Example** | <ruby>食べてみる<rt>たべてみる</rt></ruby>。 |
| **English** | "I'll try eating it." |
| **Intent** | Tests the waters without commitment. |
| **vs. potential form** | <ruby>食べられる<rt>たべられる</rt></ruby> asks "can I?" <ruby>食べてみる<rt>たべてみる</rt></ruby> asks "what if I do?" |

## Register and Formality {#register-and-formality}

=== ":material-chat-outline: Casual"
    <ruby>納豆<rt>なっとう</rt></ruby>、<ruby>食べれる<rt>たべれる</rt></ruby>ようになった。

    *"I can eat natto now."*

    **Shift:** ら抜き (<ruby>食べれる<rt>たべれる</rt></ruby>), topic-comment structure with comma, omission of subject. Warm, personal, spoken.

=== ":material-chat-outline: Casual (sensory)"
    ここから<ruby>富士山<rt>ふじさん</rt></ruby>が<ruby>見える<rt>みえる</rt></ruby>？

    *"Can you see Mt. Fuji from here?"*

    **Shift:** <ruby>見える<rt>みえる</rt></ruby> (spontaneous) for natural sight. The question is soft and observational.

=== ":material-tie: Basic Polite"
    <ruby>日本語<rt>にほんご</rt></ruby>が<ruby>話せます<rt>はなせます</rt></ruby>。

    *"I can speak Japanese."*

    **Shift:** Standard potential with polite ます. が marking. The baseline for self-introductions.

=== ":material-tie: Basic Polite (formal alternative)"
    <ruby>日本語<rt>にほんご</rt></ruby>を<ruby>話す<rt>はなす</rt></ruby>ことができます。

    *"I am able to speak Japanese."*

    **Shift:** 〜ことができます. Slightly stiffer but appropriate for job interviews and formal introductions.

=== ":material-briefcase-outline: Extra Polite"
    ご<ruby>確認<rt>かくにん</rt></ruby>いただけますか。

    *"Could you please confirm?"*

    **Shift:** This is not potential form — it is humble request. But it lives in the same semantic neighborhood as "can/could." Included here to show how Japanese replaces bare potential with humble grammar in upward communication.

=== ":material-briefcase-outline: Extra Polite (written)"
    <ruby>本書<rt>ほんしょ</rt></ruby>はオンラインでもお<ruby>読み<rt>よみ</rt></ruby>いただけます。

    *"This book can also be read online."*

    **Shift:** お<ruby>読み<rt>よみ</rt></ruby>いただけます combines humble prefix (お) + potential of いただく (to receive). This is service-industry standard for "can be used/read/accessed."

## Common Mistakes {#common-mistakes}

### 1. Using passive a-row instead of potential e-row for godan

!!! failure "Stem error"
    <ruby>書かれる<rt>かかれる</rt></ruby> (when meaning "can write")

    ~<ruby>書かれる<rt>かかれる</rt></ruby> is the passive/honorific form of <ruby>書く<rt>かく</rt></ruby> ("is written"). The potential requires the e-row: <ruby>書ける<rt>かける</rt></ruby>.~

!!! success "Correct"
    <ruby>書ける<rt>かける</rt></ruby> (can write)

### 2. Confusing potential られる with passive/honorific

!!! failure "Ambiguity error"
    <ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>が<ruby>食べられた<rt>たべられた</rt></ruby>。

    ~Without context, this is parsed as passive: "The teacher was eaten." If you mean potential ("The teacher could eat"), the object should be marked by が, and the context should make the teacher's capability clear.~

!!! success "Correct (potential)"
    <ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>は<ruby>納豆<rt>なっとう</rt></ruby>が<ruby>食べられた<rt>たべられた</rt></ruby>。 (The teacher could eat natto.)

!!! success "Correct (passive)"
    <ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>が<ruby>子供<rt>こども</rt></ruby>に<ruby>食べられた<rt>たべられた</rt></ruby>。 (The teacher was eaten by a child. — nonsense but grammatically clear.)

### 3. Using を in formal/written contexts

!!! failure "Register error"
    <ruby>日本語<rt>にほんご</rt></ruby>を<ruby>話せます<rt>はなせます</rt></ruby>。

    ~Casual を is acceptable in speech, but in writing and formal contexts, が is standard. Using を in a business email or JLPT answer sheet is marked wrong.~

!!! success "Correct"
    <ruby>日本語<rt>にほんご</rt></ruby>が<ruby>話せます<rt>はなせます</rt></ruby>。

### 4. Using potential with stative verbs

!!! failure "State verb error"
    あるられる / いられる (meaning "can exist")

    ~ある and いる are stative verbs of existence. They do not take potential form in natural Japanese. For "can be," use あり<ruby>得る<rt>える</rt></ruby> (advanced) or rephrase with <ruby>可能性<rt>かのうせい</rt></ruby>がある.~

!!! success "Correct"
    ここにいる。 (I am here.)
    ここにいられる。 (I can stay here — acceptable but rare; usually implies permission rather than existential ability.)

!!! failure "State verb error"
    分かれる (meaning "can understand")

    ~<ruby>分かる<rt>わかる</rt></ruby> already implies comprehension. Its potential form is not used. For "can understand," simply use <ruby>分かる<rt>わかる</rt></ruby> or <ruby>理解<rt>りかい</rt></ruby>できる (understand intellectually).~

!!! success "Correct"
    <ruby>分かる<rt>わかる</rt></ruby>。 (I understand.)
    <ruby>理解<rt>りかい</rt></ruby>できる。 (I can comprehend / I am capable of understanding.)

### 5. Overusing 〜ことができる in casual speech

!!! failure "Register mismatch"
    <ruby>友達<rt>ともだち</rt></ruby>に「<ruby>寿司<rt>すし</rt></ruby>をすることがことができるよ」

    ~Grammatically perfect, socially robotic. Friends say <ruby>寿司<rt>すし</rt></ruby><ruby>食べれる<rt>たべれる</rt></ruby> or <ruby>寿司<rt>すし</rt></ruby><ruby>食べられる<rt>たべられる</rt></ruby>の.~

    <ruby>寿司<rt>すし</rt></ruby>、<ruby>食べれる<rt>たべれる</rt></ruby>よ。

## Hidden Points {#hidden-points}

- **Potential verbs are technically adjectives in semantic behavior.** They describe states, not actions. This is why they take が, resist を, and can be modified by adverbs of degree: よく<ruby>話せる<rt>はなせる</rt></ruby> (can speak well), <ruby>少し<rt>すこし</rt></ruby><ruby>読める<rt>よめる</rt></ruby> (can read a little).
- **The ら抜き phenomenon is accelerating.** Younger speakers increasingly use <ruby>食べれない<rt>たべれない</rt></ruby> even in semi-formal contexts where <ruby>食べられない<rt>たべられない</rt></ruby> once mandatory. The boundary is shifting, but formal writing remains conservative.
- **Some godan potential forms are identical to independent verbs.** <ruby>売れる<rt>うれる</rt></ruby> (potential of <ruby>売る<rt>うる</rt></ruby>) = "can sell"; <ruby>売れる<rt>うれる</rt></ruby> (intransitive) = "sells well." <ruby>切れる<rt>きれる</rt></ruby> (potential of <ruby>切る<rt>きる</rt></ruby>) = "can cut"; <ruby>切れる<rt>きれる</rt></ruby> (intransitive) = "breaks / runs out." Context and transitivity disambiguate.
- **Negative potential + ようになった is a common recovery narrative.** <ruby>泳げなくて<rt>およげなくて</rt></ruby>、<ruby>練習<rt>れんしゅう</rt></ruby>して、<ruby>泳げる<rt>およげる</rt></ruby>ようになった. The arc from inability to ability is a classic Japanese self-improvement story structure.
- **Potential form can express permission indirectly.** ここで<ruby>写真<rt>しゃしん</rt></ruby>が<ruby>撮れる<rt>とれる</rt></ruby> does not just mean "I have the skill to take photos here" — it often means "Photos are permitted here." The grammar blurs ability, possibility, and permission.
- **The absence of potential for <ruby>分かる<rt>わかる</rt></ruby> is philosophically loaded.** Japanese treats understanding as an all-or-nothing state rather than a scalable skill. You do not "partially understand" with <ruby>分かる<rt>わかる</rt></ruby> — you either <ruby>分かる<rt>わかる</rt></ruby> or you do not. For gradations, use <ruby>理解<rt>りかい</rt></ruby>できる or <ruby>分かり<rt>わかり</rt></ruby>かけている.
- **できる is the most versatile potential verb.** It serves as the potential of する, but it also functions independently: この<ruby>部屋<rt>へや</rt></ruby>でできる (can be done in this room), <ruby>彼<rt>かれ</rt></ruby>ならできる (if it's him, he can do it), できない (impossible / can't).
- **Potential + だけ is a humble brag.** <ruby>日本語<rt>にほんご</rt></ruby>が<ruby>話せる<rt>はなせる</rt></ruby>だけで、<ruby>就職<rt>しゅうしょく</rt></ruby>できた. "Just because I could speak Japanese, I got the job." The だけ downplays the achievement while the potential form states the capability.
- **The particle を in casual potential is a generational marker.** Older speakers often correct を-usage; younger speakers use it naturally. In 20 years, を may become fully standard in potential sentences, but が will remain the conservative standard.
- **<ruby>見える<rt>みえる</rt></ruby> and <ruby>聞こえる<rt>きこえる</rt></ruby> can express social perception.** <ruby>彼<rt>かれ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>努力<rt>どりょく</rt></ruby>が<ruby>見える<rt>みえる</rt></ruby> = "His effort is visible (to me)." This is metaphorical sensory perception — you "see" his effort with your mind's eye. The spontaneous form extends beyond literal senses.

## Learner Stage {#learner-stage}

| Stage | What It Looks Like |
|-------|-------------------|
| **Now — N4** | Form godan potential correctly (e-row shift); form ichidan potential with られる; use できる for する; use が for the object; produce basic self-introductions (〜が<ruby>話せます<rt>はなせます</rt></ruby>) |
| **Developing — N4→N3** | Deploy ら抜き naturally in casual speech; distinguish <ruby>聞こえる<rt>きこえる</rt></ruby>/<ruby>見える<rt>みえる</rt></ruby> from <ruby>聞ける<rt>きける</rt></ruby>/<ruby>見られる<rt>みられる</rt></ruby>; use 〜ようになった for acquisition narratives; avoid を in formal writing |
| **Automatic — N3→N2** | Potential forms arrive as state-descriptions, not conjugated verbs. You hear <ruby>行ける<rt>いける</rt></ruby> and immediately parse it as "possible" rather than "go + potential suffix." The が/を choice is intuitive. |
| **Mastery** | You control the full ability spectrum: compact potential (<ruby>話せる<rt>はなせる</rt></ruby>) for casual speech, 〜ことができる for formal writing, 〜ようになる for growth narratives, <ruby>聞こえる<rt>きこえる</rt></ruby>/<ruby>見える<rt>みえる</rt></ruby> for natural senses, and ら抜き as a precise register signal. You never confuse godan potential with passive, and you know which stative verbs resist potentialization. |
