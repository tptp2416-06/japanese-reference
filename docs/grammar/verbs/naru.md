# なる — The <ruby>自動詞<rt>じどうし</rt></ruby> Change Engine

## <ruby>日本語<rt>にほんご</rt></ruby>が<ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>せるようになった。

---

## JLPT Level {#jlpt-level}

!!! abstract "JLPT Level — N4 Core / N3 Extended"
    The basic change-of-state patterns (〜になる, 〜くなる) are solid N4. The true mastery of なる lies in N3: the <ruby>獲得<rt>かくとく</rt></ruby> frame (〜ようになる), the <ruby>決定<rt>けってい</rt></ruby>/<ruby>結果<rt>けっか</rt></ruby> frame (〜ことになる), standing arrangements (〜ことになっている), and the <ruby>否定<rt>ひてい</rt></ruby> transition (〜なくなる). These patterns reveal that なる is not merely "become" — it is Japanese's primary engine for encoding intransitive change, from physical transformation to social arrangement.

    | Tier | Pattern | Description | Example |
    |------|---------|-------------|---------|
    | N5 — Foundation | だ / です | Static state | <ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>だ。 |
    | N5 — Foundation | 〜ている | Ongoing state | <ruby>元気<rt>げんき</rt></ruby>している。 |
    | N4 — Foundation | **〜にする** | **Transitive change — "make it X"** | **<ruby>部屋<rt>へや</rt></ruby>を<ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>かにする。** |
    | **N4 — Core ← You are here** | **〜になる / 〜くなる** | **Intransitive change — "become X"** | **<ruby>部屋<rt>へや</rt></ruby>が<ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>かになる。** |
    | N4 — Core | **〜なくなる** | **Loss of state or ability** | **<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>えなくなる。** |
    | N3 — Extended | **〜ようになる** | **Reach a new ability or habit** | **<ruby>泳<rt>およ</rt></ruby>げるようになる。** |
    | N3 — Extended | **〜ことになる** | **Decision, arrangement, or natural result** | **<ruby>出張<rt>しゅっちょう</rt></ruby>することになる。** |
    | N3 — Extended | **〜ことになっている** | **Standing rule, custom, or scheduled arrangement** | **<ruby>毎朝<rt>まいあさ</rt></ruby><ruby>体操<rt>たいそう</rt></ruby>をすることになっている。** |
    | N3 — Extended | **〜ないようになる** | **Reach a state of not doing** | **<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>かないようになる。** |
    | N2 — Awareness | 〜てくる / 〜ていく | Directional aspect with change | <ruby>寒<rt>さむ</rt></ruby>くなってきた。 |
    | N2 — Awareness | 〜がちになる / 〜<ruby>気味<rt>ぎみ</rt></ruby>になる | Tendency development | <ruby>忘<rt>わす</rt></ruby>れがちになる。 |

## Quick Summary {#quick-summary}

!!! tip "At a glance"
    - なる is the **intransitive** verb of change. It means the subject transforms, reaches a state, or enters a condition **without an agent being grammatically present**. If you cause the change, use 〜にする instead.
    - **Nouns and na-adjectives** connect with **に**: <ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>になる, <ruby>元気<rt>げんき</rt></ruby>になる, <ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>かになる.
    - **I-adjectives** connect with **く**: <ruby>大<rt>おお</rt></ruby>きくなる, <ruby>寒<rt>さむ</rt></ruby>くなる, <ruby>難<rt>むずか</rt></ruby>しくなる.
    - **〜ようになる** attaches to the plain form of a verb and means "come to (be able to) do / reach a state of doing." It is the grammar of **acquired habits and unlocked abilities**. <ruby>泳<rt>およ</rt></ruby>げるようになる = "come to be able to swim."
    - **〜ことになる** attaches to the plain form and means "it has been decided that…" or "the natural result is that…" It is the grammar of **external decisions and inevitable outcomes**. <ruby>出張<rt>しゅっちょう</rt></ruby>することになる = "it was decided that I would go on a business trip."
    - **〜ことになっている** means "it is arranged / customary / scheduled that…" It describes standing rules, not one-time decisions.
    - **〜なくなる** attaches to the negative form and means "cease to be / lose the ability to." <ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べられなくなる = "become unable to eat."
    - **Time and seasons** use なる to mark arrival: 3<ruby>時<rt>じ</rt></ruby>になる, <ruby>春<rt>はる</rt></ruby>になる. This is not "become three o'clock" but "the state of 3:00 is reached."

## Core Meaning {#core-meaning}

!!! note "Core Rule"
    Use なる when you want to express that the subject undergoes a change of state, enters a new condition, or reaches a milestone — and you do not wish to name the person or force causing that change. The grammar treats the change as a natural, arranged, or self-occurring event.

| <ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>になる。 | To become a teacher [career change]. |
|---|---|
| | *Context: The subject enters the professional state of being a teacher. No agent is named. The change may be self-driven or externally decided, but grammatically it is intransitive.* |

| <ruby>部屋<rt>へや</rt></ruby>が<ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>かになる。 | The room becomes quiet [natural change]. |
|---|---|
| | *Context: The room quiets down. Perhaps people left, perhaps the music stopped. The grammar does not specify why — it only records the result.* |

| <ruby>日本語<rt>にほんご</rt></ruby>が<ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>せるようになった。 | I have come to be able to speak Japanese [acquired ability]. |
|---|---|
| | *Context: The culmination of study. 〜ようになる frames the change as a developmental milestone.* |

| <ruby>来週<rt>らいしゅう</rt></ruby>、<ruby>出張<rt>しゅっちょう</rt></ruby>することになった。 | It has been decided that I will go on a business trip next week [external decision]. |
|---|---|
| | *Context: The speaker did not necessarily choose this. The grammar frames it as a settled arrangement. The subject is often omitted because the decision is the true subject.* |

| <ruby>毎朝<rt>まいあさ</rt></ruby>、ジョギングすることになっている。 | It is arranged that I jog every morning [standing habit]. |
|---|---|
| | *Context: Not a one-time decision but a current rule or custom. The speaker may have decided it long ago, but grammatically it is presented as an ongoing arrangement.* |

## The Adjective-Noun Split: くなる vs になる {#the-adjective-noun-split}

Japanese divides its change-of-state grammar cleanly between i-adjectives and everything else. This split is absolute — there are no exceptions at N4–N3 level.

### I-Adjectives: く + なる

The い drops and is replaced by く, the adverbial form. The adjective is effectively saying "in an X manner, it becomes."

| Dictionary | Adverbial | + なる | Meaning |
|------------|-----------|--------|---------|
| <ruby>大<rt>おお</rt></ruby>きい | <ruby>大<rt>おお</rt></ruby>きく | <ruby>大<rt>おお</rt></ruby>きくなる | become big |
| <ruby>小<rt>ちい</rt></ruby>さい | <ruby>小<rt>ちい</rt></ruby>さく | <ruby>小<rt>ちい</rt></ruby>さくなる | become small |
| <ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>い | <ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>く | <ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>くなる | become high / expensive |
| <ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>い | <ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>く | <ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>くなる | become cheap |
| <ruby>寒<rt>さむ</rt></ruby>い | <ruby>寒<rt>さむ</rt></ruby>く | <ruby>寒<rt>さむ</rt></ruby>くなる | become cold |
| <ruby>暑<rt>あつ</rt></ruby>い | <ruby>暑<rt>あつ</rt></ruby>く | <ruby>暑<rt>あつ</rt></ruby>くなる | become hot |
| <ruby>難<rt>むずか</rt></ruby>しい | <ruby>難<rt>むずか</rt></ruby>しく | <ruby>難<rt>むずか</rt></ruby>しくなる | become difficult |
| <ruby>易<rt>やさ</rt></ruby>しい | <ruby>易<rt>やさ</rt></ruby>しく | <ruby>易<rt>やさ</rt></ruby>しくなる | become easy |
| <ruby>早<rt>はや</rt></ruby>い / <ruby>速<rt>はや</rt></ruby>い | <ruby>早<rt>はや</rt></ruby>く / <ruby>速<rt>はや</rt></ruby>く | <ruby>早<rt>はや</rt></ruby>く / <ruby>速<rt>はや</rt></ruby>くなる | become early / fast |
| <ruby>遅<rt>おそ</rt></ruby>い | <ruby>遅<rt>おそ</rt></ruby>く | <ruby>遅<rt>おそ</rt></ruby>くなる | become late / slow |
| <ruby>新<rt>あたら</rt></ruby>しい | <ruby>新<rt>あたら</rt></ruby>しく | <ruby>新<rt>あたら</rt></ruby>しくなる | become new |
| <ruby>古<rt>ふる</rt></ruby>い | <ruby>古<rt>ふる</rt></ruby>く | <ruby>古<rt>ふる</rt></ruby>くなる | become old |
| <ruby>強<rt>つよ</rt></ruby>い | <ruby>強<rt>つよ</rt></ruby>く | <ruby>強<rt>つよ</rt></ruby>くなる | become strong |
| <ruby>弱<rt>よわ</rt></ruby>い | <ruby>弱<rt>よわ</rt></ruby>く | <ruby>弱<rt>よわ</rt></ruby>くなる | become weak |
| <ruby>長<rt>なが</rt></ruby>い | <ruby>長<rt>なが</rt></ruby>く | <ruby>長<rt>なが</rt></ruby>くなる | become long |
| <ruby>短<rt>みじか</rt></ruby>い | <ruby>短<rt>みじか</rt></ruby>く | <ruby>短<rt>みじか</rt></ruby>くなる | become short |
| <ruby>多<rt>おお</rt></ruby>い | <ruby>多<rt>おお</rt></ruby>く | <ruby>多<rt>おお</rt></ruby>くなる | become numerous |
| <ruby>少<rt>すく</rt></ruby>い | <ruby>少<rt>すく</rt></ruby>く | <ruby>少<rt>すく</rt></ruby>くなる | become few |
| <ruby>良<rt>よ</rt></ruby>い | よく | よくなる | become good / improve |
| <ruby>悪<rt>わる</rt></ruby>い | <ruby>悪<rt>わる</rt></ruby>く | <ruby>悪<rt>わる</rt></ruby>くなる | become bad / worsen |

!!! warning "Do not use い + なる"
    !!! failure "Incorrect"
        <ruby>大<rt>おお</rt></ruby>きいなる。
    !!! success "Correct"
        <ruby>大<rt>おお</rt></ruby>きくなる。

    ~い is the attributive/predicative ending. It cannot attach directly to a verb. The adverbial form く is required.~

### Nouns and Na-Adjectives: に + なる

に is the target particle — it marks the destination state. The noun or na-adjective is the goal that the subject reaches.

| Noun / Na-Adj | + に + なる | Meaning |
|---------------|-------------|---------|
| <ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby> | <ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>になる | become a teacher |
| <ruby>大人<rt>おとな</rt></ruby> | <ruby>大人<rt>おとな</rt></ruby>になる | become an adult |
| <ruby>春<rt>はる</rt></ruby> | <ruby>春<rt>はる</rt></ruby>になる | become spring |
| 3<ruby>時<rt>じ</rt></ruby> | 3<ruby>時<rt>じ</rt></ruby>になる | reach 3:00 |
| <ruby>元気<rt>げんき</rt></ruby> | <ruby>元気<rt>げんき</rt></ruby>になる | become healthy / energetic |
| <ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>か | <ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>かになる | become quiet |
| <ruby>便利<rt>べんり</rt></ruby> | <ruby>便利<rt>べんり</rt></ruby>になる | become convenient |
| <ruby>簡単<rt>かんたん</rt></ruby> | <ruby>簡単<rt>かんたん</rt></ruby>になる | become simple |
| <ruby>好<rt>す</rt></ruby>き | <ruby>好<rt>す</rt></ruby>きになる | come to like |
| <ruby>嫌<rt>きら</rt></ruby>い | <ruby>嫌<rt>きら</rt></ruby>いになる | come to dislike |
| <ruby>上手<rt>じょうず</rt></ruby> | <ruby>上手<rt>じょうず</rt></ruby>になる | become skilled |
| <ruby>下手<rt>へた</rt></ruby> | <ruby>下手<rt>へた</rt></ruby>になる | become unskilled |
| <ruby>有名<rt>ゆうめい</rt></ruby> | <ruby>有名<rt>ゆうめい</rt></ruby>になる | become famous |
| <ruby>残念<rt>ざんねん</rt></ruby> | <ruby>残念<rt>ざんねん</rt></ruby>になる | become unfortunate / regrettable |

!!! note "<ruby>好<rt>す</rt></ruby>きになる vs <ruby>好<rt>す</rt></ruby>きだ"
    <ruby>好<rt>す</rt></ruby>きだ is a static state. <ruby>好<rt>す</rt></ruby>きになる is a transition into that state. Japanese requires なる to mark the change — you cannot say <ruby>好<rt>す</rt></ruby>きになった to mean "was liking" (that would be <ruby>好<rt>す</rt></ruby>きだった). The なる makes the emotional shift explicit.

!!! note "Time + になる"
    3<ruby>時<rt>じ</rt></ruby>になる and <ruby>春<rt>はる</rt></ruby>になる use なる not as "become" in the English sense but as "reach / arrive at." The clock does not transform into 3:00; the moment of 3:00 is attained. This is a collocation worth memorizing as a unit.

## 〜ようになる — The Acquisition Frame {#you-ni-naru}

This is the N3 grammar of **developmental change**. It attaches to the plain form of a verb (including potential, negative, and passive forms) and means "reach a state where…" or "come to be able to…"

| <ruby>泳<rt>およ</rt></ruby>げるようになる。 | Come to be able to swim [acquired ability]. |
|---|---|
| | *Context: The end point of swimming lessons. The potential form <ruby>泳<rt>およ</rt></ruby>げる is embedded inside the 〜ようになる frame.* |

| <ruby>早<rt>はや</rt></ruby>く<ruby>起<rt>お</rt></ruby>きるようになる。 | Come to get up early [acquired habit]. |
|---|---|
| | *Context: A former night owl who has reformed. No potential form needed — just the plain verb inside 〜ようになる marks the new habitual state.* |

| <ruby>納豆<rt>なっとう</rt></ruby>が<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べられるようになった。 | I have come to be able to eat natto [acquired tolerance]. |
|---|---|
| | *Context: Overcoming food aversion. The potential <ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べられる sits inside 〜ようになる, creating a double-layered change: from inability → ability → habitual state of that ability.* |

| <ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>かないようになる。 | Come to not go / stop going [acquired non-habit]. |
|---|---|
| | *Context: A negative verb inside 〜ようになる. The subject reaches a state of abstention. This is common for quitting habits or drifting apart from places.* |

!!! note "〜ようになる vs 〜ことになる"
    Both attach to plain-form verbs. 〜ようになる is about **ability and habit** — can the subject do it, and do they now do it regularly? 〜ことになる is about **decision and arrangement** — has it been settled that the action will occur?

    - <ruby>泳<rt>およ</rt></ruby>げるようになった = "I can now swim." (My body learned.)
    - <ruby>泳<rt>およ</rt></ruby>ぐことになった = "It was decided that I will swim." (My schedule changed.)

!!! note "〜ようになる implies process"
    Unlike 〜た (simple past) or 〜ている (current state), 〜ようになる carries the nuance of **effort, time, or natural development leading to the new state**. It is the grammar of milestones.

## 〜ことになる — The Decision & Result Frame {#koto-ni-naru}

This N3 pattern attaches to plain-form verbs and frames the action as a **settled outcome**. The subject is often omitted because the focus is on the arrangement itself.

### Two Core Meanings

| Meaning | Nuance | Example |
|---------|--------|---------|
| External decision | Someone else decided; the speaker accepts | <ruby>来月<rt>らいげつ</rt></ruby>、<ruby>転勤<rt>てんきん</rt></ruby>することになった。 |
| Natural result | Events converged to this outcome; no single decider | <ruby>遅刻<rt>ちこく</rt></ruby>することになった。 |

| <ruby>来月<rt>らいげつ</rt></ruby>、<ruby>転勤<rt>てんきん</rt></ruby>することになった。 | It has been decided that I will transfer next month [external decision]. |
|---|---|
| | *Context: The company decided. The speaker reports the arrangement. The passive-like framing removes the need to name the decision-maker.* |

| <ruby>一人<rt>ひとり</rt></ruby>で<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>くことになった。 | It ended up that I will go alone [natural result / self-decision]. |
|---|---|
| | *Context: Plans collapsed, friends cancelled, and the speaker is left going solo. The grammar frames this as a convergent outcome, not a deliberate choice.* |

| <ruby>今日<rt>きょう</rt></ruby>は<ruby>残業<rt>ざんぎょう</rt></ruby>することになった。 | It has turned out that I have to work overtime today [result]. |
|---|---|
| | *Context: The day unfolded this way. The speaker is not necessarily complaining, but the grammar acknowledges that the situation settled into this shape.* |

!!! note "〜ことになる vs 〜ことにする"
    〜ことにする means "I decide that…" — the subject is the decider. 〜ことになる means "it is decided / it turns out that…" — the decision is external, collective, or simply presented as a fait accompli.

    - <ruby>出張<rt>しゅっちょう</rt></ruby>することにした = "I decided to go on a business trip."
    - <ruby>出張<rt>しゅっちょう</rt></ruby>することになった = "It was decided that I would go on a business trip." (Boss decided, or circumstances dictated.)

## 〜ことになっている — The Standing Arrangement {#koto-ni-natte-iru}

This N3 pattern describes **ongoing rules, customs, or scheduled arrangements**. It is not about a one-time decision but about a current system.

| <ruby>毎朝<rt>まいあさ</rt></ruby>、<ruby>体操<rt>たいそう</rt></ruby>をすることになっている。 | It is arranged that we do exercises every morning [standing rule]. |
|---|---|
| | *Context: School routine, company policy, or personal rule. The arrangement is already in place and continues.* |

| ここでは<ruby>禁煙<rt>きんえん</rt></ruby>することになっている。 | Smoking is prohibited here [rule in effect]. |
|---|---|
| | *Context: A posted rule. The grammar frames the prohibition as an existing arrangement, not a new decision.* |

| <ruby>会議<rt>かいぎ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>金曜日<rt>きんようび</rt></ruby>に<ruby>開<rt>ひら</rt></ruby>くことになっている。 | The meeting is scheduled for Friday [scheduled arrangement]. |
|---|---|
| | *Context: The calendar says so. The arrangement predates the current moment.* |

!!! note "〜ことになっている vs 〜ことになった"
    〜ことになった is a **punctual** event — the decision was made. 〜ことになっている is a **durative** state — the arrangement is currently in force. A meeting is <ruby>開<rt>ひら</rt></ruby>くことになった when the boss scheduled it last week; it is <ruby>開<rt>ひら</rt></ruby>くことになっている when you check the calendar today.

## 〜なくなる — The Negative Transition {#naku-naru}

This N4 pattern attaches to the **negative plain form** of verbs and the **negative adverbial form** of adjectives. It means "cease to be / lose the ability to / become no longer."

| Verb Negative | + なる | Meaning |
|---------------|--------|---------|
| <ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>かない | <ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>かなくなる | stop going / cease to go |
| <ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べられない | <ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べられなくなる | become unable to eat |
| <ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>えない | <ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>えなくなる | become unable to see |
| <ruby>分<rt>わ</rt></ruby>からない | <ruby>分<rt>わ</rt></ruby>からなくなる | become unable to understand |
| <ruby>聞<rt>き</rt></ruby>こえない | <ruby>聞<rt>き</rt></ruby>こえなくなる | become unable to hear |

| I-Adj Negative | + なる | Meaning |
|----------------|--------|---------|
| <ruby>大<rt>おお</rt></ruby>きくない | <ruby>大<rt>おお</rt></ruby>きくなくなる | become not big |
| <ruby>寒<rt>さむ</rt></ruby>くない | <ruby>寒<rt>さむ</rt></ruby>くなくなる | become not cold |
| <ruby>難<rt>むずか</rt></ruby>しくない | <ruby>難<rt>むずか</rt></ruby>しくなくなる | become not difficult |

| Noun/Na-Adj Negative | + なる | Meaning |
|----------------------|--------|---------|
| <ruby>元気<rt>げんき</rt></ruby>ではない | <ruby>元気<rt>げんき</rt></ruby>ではなくなる | become not healthy (formal) |
| <ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>かではない | <ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>かではなくなる | become not quiet (formal) |
| <ruby>元気<rt>げんき</rt></ruby>じゃない | <ruby>元気<rt>げんき</rt></ruby>じゃなくなる | become not healthy (casual) |

!!! note "〜なくなる as loss"
    This pattern is the grammar of **decline, forgetting, and fading**. <ruby>忘<rt>わす</rt></ruby>れなくなる means "come to not forget / stop forgetting" — it is the transition into a state where forgetting ceases. Do not confuse it with <ruby>覚<rt>おぼ</rt></ruby>えられなくなる (become unable to remember), which is the true loss pattern. The change is often involuntary.

| <ruby>年<rt>とし</rt></ruby>を<ruby>取<rt>と</rt></ruby>ると、<ruby>小<rt>ちい</rt></ruby>さい<ruby>字<rt>じ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>えなくなる。 | As you age, you become unable to see small text [natural decline]. |
|---|---|
| | *Context: The grammar encodes a universal physical transition. No agent; just time.* |

| <ruby>忙<rt>いそが</rt></ruby>しくて、<ruby>映画<rt>えいが</rt></ruby>を<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>に<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>かなくなった。 | I got busy and stopped going to see movies [habit loss]. |
|---|---|
| | *Context: The negative verb <ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>かない is embedded in なくなる. The habit faded due to circumstances.* |

## 〜にする vs 〜になる: The Transitivity Mirror {#ni-suru-vs-ni-naru}

This is the most consequential N4 distinction. Every なる sentence has a する counterpart, and choosing the wrong one reverses the social geometry.

| | 〜にする (Transitive) | 〜になる (Intransitive) |
|---|---|---|
| **Core meaning** | I make it X / I choose X | It becomes X / It turns out X |
| **Subject** | The agent (I) | The thing changing |
| **Object** | The thing being changed (を) | None — the subject changes itself |
| **Power dynamic** | Active control | Passive reception |
| **Example** | <ruby>部屋<rt>へや</rt></ruby>を<ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>かにする。 | <ruby>部屋<rt>へや</rt></ruby>が<ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>かになる。 |
| | "I make the room quiet." | "The room becomes quiet." |

| <ruby>部屋<rt>へや</rt></ruby>を<ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>かにする。 | I make the room quiet [transitive]. |
|---|---|
| | *Context: The speaker takes action — closes windows, shushes people. The を marks the room as the object of the speaker's will.* |

| <ruby>部屋<rt>へや</rt></ruby>が<ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>かになる。 | The room becomes quiet [intransitive]. |
|---|---|
| | *Context: People leave, or time passes. The room quiets without the speaker being the named agent. The が marks the room as the subject of its own change.* |

| <ruby>日本語<rt>にほんご</rt></ruby>の<ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>にする。 | I will make (someone) a Japanese teacher. |
|---|---|
| | *Context: する with a person means appointment or selection of another person. For your own career, use なる instead. The transitive force is explicit.* |

| <ruby>日本語<rt>にほんご</rt></ruby>の<ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>になる。 | I become a Japanese teacher. |
|---|---|
| | *Context: The speaker enters the profession. No external appointer is named, even if one exists. The grammar frames the change as self-occurring.* |

!!! note "The cultural preference for なる"
    Japanese often prefers なる even when the speaker caused the change, because it sounds less ego-driven. よくなりました ("It improved") is humbler than よくしました ("I improved it"). The intransitive frame deflects personal agency.

## In Real Life {#in-real-life}

!!! example "In Real Life — Acquisition"
    **Situation:** ミサ has been studying Japanese for two years. She is at a yakitori restaurant with a friend who only speaks English. The server asks a question about the order, and Misa answers without hesitation. Her friend stares.

    > <ruby>日本語<rt>にほんご</rt></ruby>が<ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>せるようになったんだ。

    *"I've come to be able to speak Japanese."*

    **Why 〜ようになる fits:** Misa is not saying "I speak Japanese" (<ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>せる) or "I spoke Japanese" (<ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>した). She is marking the milestone — the point where ability crystallized. The んだ adds explanatory weight: "This is why I could answer."

    **What <ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>せるようになった implies:** It took time. There was a before and an after. The grammar celebrates the journey, not just the result.

!!! example "In Real Life — External Decision"
    **Situation:** ケン is packing a suitcase. His partner asks why. He shrugs with mild resignation.

    > <ruby>来週<rt>らいしゅう</rt></ruby>、<ruby>大阪<rt>おおさか</rt></ruby>に<ruby>出張<rt>しゅっちょう</rt></ruby>することになった。

    *"It was decided that I'm going on a business trip to Osaka next week."*

    **Why 〜ことになる fits:** Ken did not choose this. The company decided, and he is reporting the arrangement. The grammar lets him state the fact without naming the decider or sounding like he is complaining.

    **What <ruby>出張<rt>しゅっちょう</rt></ruby>することにした would change:** That would mean Ken decided his own business trip — perhaps he is self-employed. The shift from なる to する shifts agency entirely.

    **What <ruby>出張<rt>しゅっちょう</rt></ruby>する would change:** That is a simple future plan. It lacks the "arranged / settled" nuance that 〜ことになる carries.

!!! example "In Real Life — Standing Arrangement"
    **Situation:** A new employee asks a senior colleague about lunch breaks.

    > ここでは12<ruby>時<rt>じ</rt></ruby>から1<ruby>時<rt>じ</rt></ruby>まで<ruby>休憩<rt>きゅうけい</rt></ruby>することになっているよ。

    *"Here, it's arranged that we take breaks from 12 to 1."*

    **Why 〜ことになっている fits:** This is not a decision made today. It is an existing rule. The grammar frames it as a durable structure of the workplace.

    **What 〜ことになった would change:** That would imply the rule was just created — perhaps a new policy announcement. 〜ことになっている is the grammar of "how things are."

!!! example "In Real Life — Negative Transition"
    **Situation:** ユイ is visiting her childhood home. She picks up an old video game she used to play daily. After five minutes, she puts it down, bored.

    > <ruby>子供<rt>こども</rt></ruby>の<ruby>頃<rt>ころ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>毎日<rt>まいにち</rt></ruby>やってたけど、<ruby>今<rt>いま</rt></ruby>は<ruby>全然<rt>ぜんぜん</rt></ruby><ruby>面白<rt>おもしろ</rt></ruby>くなくなった。

    *"I played it every day as a kid, but now it's completely stopped being interesting."*

    **Why 〜なくなる fits:** <ruby>面白<rt>おもしろ</rt></ruby>くない is the negative state. <ruby>面白<rt>おもしろ</rt></ruby>くなくなる is the transition into that state. The grammar encodes the fading of childhood magic.

    **What <ruby>面白<rt>おもしろ</rt></ruby>くない would change:** That is a static judgment: "It's not interesting." <ruby>面白<rt>おもしろ</rt></ruby>くなくなった is a narrative of loss: "It used to be interesting, and now that quality has left."

## Grammar Analysis {#grammar-analysis}

### Function in Context

- <ruby>日本語<rt>にほんご</rt></ruby>が<ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>せるようになった: <ruby>日本語<rt>にほんご</rt></ruby>が<ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>せる is the potential clause ("can speak Japanese"). 〜ようになる wraps it in a developmental frame. The subject (I) is implied.
- <ruby>出張<rt>しゅっちょう</rt></ruby>することになった: <ruby>出張<rt>しゅっちょう</rt></ruby>する is the plain verb. 〜ことになる nominalizes and arranges it. The subject of なる is the abstract "thing" (こと) of the business trip.
- <ruby>毎朝<rt>まいあさ</rt></ruby><ruby>体操<rt>たいそう</rt></ruby>をすることになっている: <ruby>体操<rt>たいそう</rt></ruby>をする is the plain verb. 〜ことになっている places it in a standing-rule frame. The 〜ている adds durative aspect to the arrangement.
- <ruby>小<rt>ちい</rt></ruby>さい<ruby>字<rt>じ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>えなくなる: <ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>えない is the negative spontaneous (cannot see). なくなる transitions into that negative state. The grammar frames aging as a process of progressive loss.

### Construction {#construction}

#### Noun / Na-Adj + になる

| Preceding Form | Pattern | Example |
|----------------|---------|---------|
| Verb negative | negative + なる | <ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>かなくなる |
| I-adj negative | Adverbial form + なくなる | <ruby>寒<rt>さむ</rt></ruby>くなくなる |
| Na-adj negative | stem + ではなくなる | <ruby>元気<rt>げんき</rt></ruby>ではなくなる |

    !!! failure "Transitivity error"
        <ruby>部屋<rt>へや</rt></ruby>を<ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>かになる。
    !!! success "Correct"
        <ruby>部屋<rt>へや</rt></ruby>が<ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>かになる。

    ~なる is intransitive. The changing entity is the grammatical subject (が), never the object (を).~

    !!! failure "Incorrect"
        <ruby>元気<rt>げんき</rt></ruby>だになる。
    !!! success "Correct"
        <ruby>元気<rt>げんき</rt></ruby>になる。

    ~だ is the predicative copula. It cannot stack before なる. The particle に alone connects the noun/na-adj to the change verb.~

## Japanese Mindset {#japanese-mindset}

The なる family reveals a cultural preference for **framing change as process rather than agency**.

- **Intransitive humility.** Using なる instead of する deflects personal agency. よくなりました ("It improved") is humbler than よくしました ("I improved it"). In Japanese workplace communication, なる is the default for reporting improvements unless the speaker wants explicit credit.
- **Externalization of decision.** 〜ことになる allows the speaker to report being assigned something without sounding resentful or prideful. The decision is simply "what became." This aligns with cultural norms of accepting group-determined outcomes gracefully.
- **Development as narrative.** 〜ようになる is the grammar of maturation. Japanese education and self-help discourse rely heavily on this pattern because it frames growth as a natural unfolding rather than a forced achievement.
- **Standing rules as social fabric.** 〜ことになっている treats customs and rules as part of the environment — not imposed, just "how it is." This reflects a cultural tendency to naturalize social structures.
- **Loss without blame.** 〜なくなる encodes decline (forgetting, aging, fading interest) without assigning fault. The grammar is compassionate: things simply enter negative states.

## Nuance Comparison {#nuance-comparison}

| Grammar Point | Core Meaning | Agency | Aspect | Register | JLPT |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 〜になる / 〜くなる | Become [state] | None (intransitive) | Change | Neutral | N4 |
| 〜にする | Make [state] / choose [state] | Speaker as agent | Action | Neutral | N4 |
| 〜ようになる | Come to [do/be able to] | None (developmental) | Acquisition | Neutral | N3 |
| 〜ことになる | It is decided / it turns out | External / convergent | Arrangement | Neutral | N3 |
| 〜ことにする | I decide that | Speaker as decider | Decision | Neutral | N4 |
| 〜ことになっている | It is arranged / customary | Institutional / habitual | Durative | Neutral | N3 |
| 〜なくなる | Cease to be / lose ability | None (often involuntary) | Negative transition | Neutral | N4 |
| 〜てしまう | End up doing (regret) | Speaker involved | Completion | Casual | N4 |
| 〜<ruby>始<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>める | Begin to do | Speaker or natural | Inceptive | Neutral | N4 |
| 〜<ruby>続<rt>つづ</rt></ruby>ける | Continue to do | Speaker or natural | Continuative | Neutral | N4 |

**〜になる vs 〜にする:** The transitivity mirror. If the subject is causing the change, する. If the subject is undergoing the change, なる. This distinction is stricter in Japanese than in English, where "become" and "make" are clearly different but "get" blurs the line (I got the room quiet = I made it quiet).

**〜ようになる vs 〜ことになる:** Both attach to plain verbs. 〜ようになる is about **internal development** (ability, habit). 〜ことになる is about **external settlement** (schedule, decision, result). <ruby>泳<rt>およ</rt></ruby>げるようになる = my body learned. <ruby>泳<rt>およ</rt></ruby>ぐことになる = my calendar says so.

**〜ことになる vs 〜ことにする:** 〜ことにする is active decision. 〜ことになる is reported arrangement. A student says <ruby>留学<rt>りゅうがく</rt></ruby>することにした (I decided to study abroad). Their parents tell relatives <ruby>留学<rt>りゅうがく</rt></ruby>することになった (It has been decided that they will study abroad). Same event, different grammatical framing of agency.

## Register and Formality {#register-and-formality}

=== ":material-chat-outline: Casual"
    <ruby>日本語<rt>にほんご</rt></ruby><ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>せるようになった。

    *"I've come to be able to speak Japanese."*

    **Shift:** Subject omitted. <ruby>日本語<rt>にほんご</rt></ruby> is the topic. <ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>せる is potential. ようになった is the milestone marker. The rhythm is: topic-ability-acquisition.

=== ":material-chat-outline: Casual (negative transition)"
    <ruby>最近<rt>さいきん</rt></ruby>、コーヒー<ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>まなくなった。

    *"Lately, I've stopped drinking coffee."*

    **Shift:** <ruby>最近<rt>さいきん</rt></ruby> sets the time frame. コーヒー is the topic. <ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>まなくなった is negative verb + なる. Simple, direct.

=== ":material-tie: Basic Polite"
    <ruby>来月<rt>らいげつ</rt></ruby>、<ruby>出張<rt>しゅっちょう</rt></ruby>することになりました。

    *"It has been decided that I will go on a business trip next month."*

    **Shift:** Standard business report. 〜ことになりました is the polite form of 〜ことになった. The subject is omitted because the focus is on the arrangement.

=== ":material-tie: Basic Polite (standing rule)"
    こちらでは、<ruby>毎朝<rt>まいあさ</rt></ruby><ruby>体操<rt>たいそう</rt></ruby>をすることになっています。

    *"Here, it is arranged that we do exercises every morning."*

    **Shift:** こちらで (formal "here"). 〜ことになっています adds politeness and durative aspect. The grammar frames the rule as institutional.

=== ":material-briefcase-outline: Extra Polite"
    <ruby>本年度<rt>ほんねんど</rt></ruby>より、<ruby>新<rt>しん</rt></ruby>システムを<ruby>導入<rt>どうにゅう</rt></ruby>することとなっております。

    *"From this fiscal year, it has been arranged that we will introduce the new system."*

    **Shift:** 〜こととなっております is the humble/extra-polite form of 〜ことになっている. より marks the starting point formally. This is standard corporate announcement grammar.

=== ":material-briefcase-outline: Extra Polite (developmental)"
    お<ruby>陰様<rt>かげさま</rt></ruby>で、<ruby>少<rt>すこ</rt></ruby>しずつ<ruby>日本語<rt>にほんご</rt></ruby>がお<ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>しできるようになってまいりました。

    *"Thanks to you, I have gradually come to be able to speak Japanese."*

    **Shift:** お<ruby>陰様<rt>かげさま</rt></ruby>で (humble gratitude). お<ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>しできる (humble potential). まいりました (humble past of <ruby>来<rt>く</rt></ruby>る). The entire sentence is wrapped in humble grammar, with 〜ようになって as the core developmental frame.

## Common Mistakes {#common-mistakes}

### 1. Using を with なる

!!! failure "Transitivity error"
    <ruby>部屋<rt>へや</rt></ruby>を<ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>かになる。

    ~なる is intransitive. The changing entity must be marked by が, not を. を is the object marker for transitive verbs like する.~

!!! success "Correct"
    <ruby>部屋<rt>へや</rt></ruby>が<ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>かになる。

### 2. Confusing 〜ようになる and 〜ことになる

!!! failure "Pattern error"
    <ruby>泳<rt>およ</rt></ruby>ぐようになった (when meaning "it was decided that I will swim")

    ~<ruby>泳<rt>およ</rt></ruby>ぐようになった means "I came to swim / I developed the habit of swimming." It is about acquisition, not arrangement. For a scheduled swim, use <ruby>泳<rt>およ</rt></ruby>ぐことになった.~

!!! success "Correct (acquisition)"
    <ruby>泳<rt>およ</rt></ruby>げるようになった。 (I came to be able to swim.)

!!! success "Correct (arrangement)"
    <ruby>泳<rt>およ</rt></ruby>ぐことになった。 (It was decided that I will swim.)

### 3. Using い instead of く before なる

!!! failure "Conjugation error"
    <ruby>大<rt>おお</rt></ruby>きいなる。

    ~い-adjectives cannot attach directly to verbs. The adverbial form く is required.~

!!! success "Correct"
    <ruby>大<rt>おお</rt></ruby>きくなる。

### 4. Using だ/だった with nouns/na-adj before なる

!!! failure "Copula stacking error"
    <ruby>元気<rt>げんき</rt></ruby>だになる。

    ~だ is the predicative copula. It cannot precede なる. Use に alone.~

!!! success "Correct"
    <ruby>元気<rt>げんき</rt></ruby>になる。

### 5. Using 〜ことになる for self-decisions

!!! failure "Agency error"
    <ruby>留学<rt>りゅうがく</rt></ruby>することになった (said when the speaker decided entirely by themselves)

    ~If the speaker made the decision alone, <ruby>留学<rt>りゅうがく</rt></ruby>することにした is more accurate. 〜ことになる implies external decision, group arrangement, or convergent result. Using it for a purely self-directed choice sounds like false modesty or evasion.~

!!! success "Correct"
    <ruby>留学<rt>りゅうがく</rt></ruby>することにした。 (I decided to study abroad.)

### 6. Using 〜なくなる when 〜ないようにする is intended

!!! failure "Pattern error"
    <ruby>忘<rt>わす</rt></ruby>れなくなる (when meaning "I will try not to forget")

    ~<ruby>忘<rt>わす</rt></ruby>れなくなる means "I will come to not forget / I will stop forgetting" — it describes a natural transition into a state of reliable memory. If you want to express deliberate effort to avoid forgetting, use <ruby>忘<rt>わす</rt></ruby>れないようにする (I will make an effort not to forget). The difference is agency: なくなる is involuntary change, ようにする is conscious effort.~

!!! success "Correct (intention)"
    <ruby>忘<rt>わす</rt></ruby>れないようにする。

!!! success "Correct (natural result)"
    <ruby>忘<rt>わす</rt></ruby>れなくなる。 (I will stop forgetting. / I will come to not forget.)

## Hidden Points {#hidden-points}

- **よくなる has two lives.** <ruby>体<rt>からだ</rt></ruby>がよくなる = "health improves." <ruby>状況<rt>じょうきょう</rt></ruby>がよくなる = "the situation improves." But よくなる can also mean "to heal" in medical contexts: <ruby>体調<rt>たいちょう</rt></ruby>がよくなった = "my condition improved." The ambiguity is resolved by subject.
- **なる with weather is standard; する with weather is godlike.** <ruby>雨<rt>あめ</rt></ruby>になる = "it will rain." <ruby>雨<rt>あめ</rt></ruby>にする = "I will make it rain" (only wizards and meteorological engineers). The transitivity mirror is absolute here.
- **〜ようになる can attach to negative verbs for "quitting."** <ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>かないようになる = "come to not go / stop going." This is the grammar of drifting away from people, places, and habits without conscious rejection.
- **〜ことになる can express fatalism.** <ruby>遅刻<rt>ちこく</rt></ruby>することになった = "I ended up being late." The speaker did not plan it; circumstances converged. The grammar absolves the speaker by removing agency.
- **The て-form chain 〜なってきた signals incipient change.** <ruby>寒<rt>さむ</rt></ruby>くなってきた = "it has started to get cold." The 〜てくる adds the sense of change approaching the speaker over time. This is N2 but appears naturally in N3 contexts.
- **〜なくなる is the grammar of aging and entropy.** <ruby>聞<rt>き</rt></ruby>こえなくなる, <ruby>覚<rt>おぼ</rt></ruby>えられなくなる, <ruby>好<rt>す</rt></ruby>きじゃなくなる. These are not decisions — they are states that arrive. The pattern is deeply embedded in Japanese expressions of mortality and loss.
- **Some nouns resist なる because they imply instantaneity.** <ruby>結婚<rt>けっこん</rt></ruby>になる is ungrammatical/unnatural; you say <ruby>結婚<rt>けっこん</rt></ruby>する (to marry) because marriage is an action, not a gradual state change. <ruby>結婚生活<rt>けっこんせいかつ</rt></ruby>になる ("married life begins") is acceptable because it describes a state.
- **なる with food/ingredient names means "turn into."** カレーになる = "it becomes curry" (transformation of ingredients). This is literal, not metaphorical.
- **The past negative of なる is ならなかった.** <ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>にならなかった = "did not become a teacher." Do not confuse this with ならない in the construction 〜てはならない ("must not"). On its own, ならない simply means "does not become."
- **〜ことになっている can describe biological instinct.** <ruby>人間<rt>にんげん</rt></ruby>は<ruby>死<rt>し</rt></ruby>ぬことになっている = "It is ordained that humans die." The grammar frames mortality as a standing arrangement — perhaps the ultimate example of naturalizing a rule.

## Learner Stage {#learner-stage}

| Stage | What It Looks Like |
|-------|-------------------|
| **Now — N4** | Produce 〜になる and 〜くなる correctly with nouns, na-adj, and i-adj; distinguish 〜にする from 〜になる; use 〜なくなる for loss of state; avoid を and だ stacking errors |
| **Developing — N4→N3** | Deploy 〜ようになる for acquired abilities and habits; use 〜ことになる for reporting arrangements and results; use 〜ことになっている for standing rules; understand the agency distinction between 〜ことにする and 〜ことになる |
| **Automatic — N3→N2** | なる arrives as a change-of-state marker before conscious analysis. You hear <ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>せるようになった and immediately register "developmental milestone." You choose 〜ことになる for external decisions without deliberation. |
| **Mastery** | You control the full なる spectrum: basic change (<ruby>大<rt>おお</rt></ruby>きくなる), acquisition (<ruby>泳<rt>およ</rt></ruby>げるようになる), arrangement (<ruby>出張<rt>しゅっちょう</rt></ruby>することになる), standing rules (<ruby>毎朝<rt>まいあさ</rt></ruby><ruby>体操<rt>たいそう</rt></ruby>をすることになっている), negative transition (<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>えなくなる), and the transitivity mirror (<ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>かにする vs <ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>かになる). You never use を with なる, and you know when self-deprecating なる is more socially graceful than agency-claiming する. |

## See Also {#see-also}

### Same Family

- 〜にする — Transitive counterpart (make it X / choose X)
- 〜なくなる — Negative transition (cease to be / lose ability)
- 〜ようになる — Acquisition frame (come to be able to / come to do)
- 〜ことになる — Decision/result frame (it is decided that…)
- 〜ことになっている — Standing arrangement frame

### Commonly Confused With

- 〜てしまう — End up doing (regretful completion, not change of state)
- 〜<ruby>始<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>める — Begin to do (inceptive, not developmental)
- 〜<ruby>続<rt>つづ</rt></ruby>ける — Continue to do (continuative, not arrangement)
- 〜ことにする — I decide that (active decider, not external arrangement)
- 〜ようにする — I make an effort to (active effort, not natural acquisition)
- 〜がる — Show signs of (third-person emotion, not change of state)

### Builds From

- Particle に (N5) — Target state marking
- Particle が (N5) — Subject marking for intransitive change
- I-adjective adverbial form く (N5) — Required for verb adjunction
- Na-adjective stem + に (N5) — Required for state targeting
- Potential form (N4) — Embedded in 〜ようになる
- Plain verb forms (N5) — Required for 〜ようになる and 〜ことになる
- 〜ている (N4) — Required for 〜ことになっている

### Unlocks Next

- 〜てくる / 〜ていく (N2) — Directional aspect with change (<ruby>寒<rt>さむ</rt></ruby>くなってきた)
- 〜がちになる (N2) — Tendency development
- 〜<ruby>気味<rt>ぎみ</rt></ruby>になる (N2) — Slight tendency development
- <ruby>使役<rt>しえき</rt></ruby> (〜させる) (N4) — Making someone do (causative transitivity)
- <ruby>使役受身<rt>しえきうけみ</rt></ruby> (〜させられる) (N3) — Forced action (causative + passive)
