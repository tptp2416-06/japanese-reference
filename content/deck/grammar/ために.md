One of the two big "in order to" patterns. ために states a deliberate aim
outright; its counterpart ように covers outcomes you can't simply will into
happening (understanding, remembering, becoming able to).

@@ purpose

Attach it to a dictionary-form verb, or to a noun with の:

- 日本語を勉強するために、アプリを入れた。 — I installed an app to study Japanese.
- 家族のために働いてる。 — I work for my family's sake.

Two things to keep straight:

1. **The same subject controls both halves.** ために wants a deliberate action
   aimed at a goal the actor can pursue directly. If the goal isn't something
   you can just decide to do — not forgetting, being able to — Japanese
   reaches for ように instead: 忘れないようにメモした。
2. **With a noun it shades into "for the benefit of".** 家族のために is
   benefit, not plan; context decides, and the two readings sit close enough
   that this rarely causes trouble.

In casual speech the に often drops: 何のため？ — what for?
