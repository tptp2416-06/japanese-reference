Think of a question word as opening a list of possible answers.

何 asks: which thing?
誰 asks: which person?
どこ asks: which place?

With common forms like these, add も and make the predicate negative, and every
possible answer is ruled out:

```eg
何[なに]も食[た]べない | do not eat anything, eat nothing
```

The sentence does not choose one particular thing and negate it. It says that
**none of the possible things qualify**.

The technical way to describe this is as a set of possible answers. The useful
learner intuition is simpler:

**question word + も + negative → none of the possible answers**

**Deciding which particle to add is the real choice** — か for one
unspecified answer, も + negative for none, でも for any answer at all. Pick
the particle for what the possible answers should do, then build the
predicate to match.

@@ tldr

With common forms such as 何, 誰, どこ and どれ,
`疑問詞 + も + negative predicate` gives a nothing / nobody / nowhere / none
type of meaning:

何も食べない — eat nothing, do not eat anything
誰も来ない — nobody comes
どこも開いていない — nowhere is open

Use +か when **one unspecified answer** is meant, and +でも when **any answer
is acceptable or possible**.

@@ main

**Form:** `疑問詞 + も + negative predicate` · `疑問詞 + case particle + も + negative predicate`

- **The negative predicate rules out every possible answer** — 誰も来なかった
  means nobody came: no person in the relevant group satisfies 来た — came.
- **も does not by itself mean nobody or nothing here** — the negative
  predicate completes that reading. 誰も identifies the range being
  considered; 来なかった rules everyone in that range out.
- **For this none reading, the predicate stays negative** — 何も食べる does
  not mean eat something. The choice between か, も〜ない and でも changes
  how the possible answers are treated.

A useful way to keep the three systems apart is:

| shape                | what happens to the possible answers | example |
| --------------------- | ------------------------------------ | ------- |
| `疑問詞 + か`            | one unspecified member               | 何か食べたい — I want to eat something |
| `疑問詞 + も + negative` | no member                            | 何も食べたくない — I do not want to eat anything |
| `疑問詞 + でも`           | any member is acceptable or possible | 何でも食べられる — I can eat anything |

With 何, the contrast is especially easy to see:

```eg
何[なに]か | something
何[なに]も〜ない | nothing, not anything
何[なに]でも | anything, whatever
```

- **A common mistake is to mix these patterns** — 何も食べたい does not mean
  I want to eat something; that needs 何か食べたい. If the desire itself is
  negative, 何も食べたくない means I do not want to eat anything.
```ex
朝[あさ]から何[なに]も食[た]べていない。
en: I haven't eaten anything since this morning.
bd: 朝から|since this morning / 何も*|anything / 食べていない*|have not eaten
pat: 何 + も, negative predicate
```
```dialog
note: what licenses 何も食べてない — B's answer negates the very item A's か-question left open.
A: 何[なに]か食[た]べた？ | Did you eat something?
B: ううん、何[なに]も食[た]べてない。 | No, I haven't eaten anything.
```

- **In quick replies, the phrase alone can stand for the whole sentence** —
  何も。／誰も。／どこも。 answer a question directly, the negative
  predicate understood from context.
- **Politeness generally attaches to the predicate, not to も itself** —
  誰もいらっしゃいませんでした is a formal, written-register way to say
  誰も来なかった — nobody came; the range-marking も does not change.

### Which particles も replaces

- **が and を normally give way to も** — 誰も来なかった — nobody came — and
  何も買わなかった — I bought nothing — do not need an additional が or を. も
  takes the place where が or を would otherwise appear.
- **Other case particles can remain before も** — 誰にも会わなかった, I did
  not meet anyone, 誰とも話さなかった, I did not speak with anyone, and
  どこにも行かなかった, I did not go anywhere, keep に or と because those
  particles still express a relationship the verb requires.
- **So do not apply "replace the particle with も" as a blanket rule** —
  whether another particle remains depends on what that particle is doing in
  the sentence.
```dialog
note: what licenses 誰も来られない — B answers A's who-question by ruling out every member of the group A asked about.
A: 明日[あした]のパーティー、誰[だれ]が来[く]る？ | Who's coming to tomorrow's party?
B: それが、誰[だれ]も来[こ]られないんだって。 | Actually, apparently no one can come.
```

### 疑問詞＋も can be affirmative too

Yes. The "none" pattern on this page is important, but it is not a rule that
every question word followed by も must appear with a negative predicate.

??? note "Affirmative 疑問詞＋も"
    Some forms instead give an **all / every** reading with an affirmative
    predicate:

    ```eg
    どれもおいしい | all of them are delicious
    どこも混んでいる | everywhere is crowded
    ```

    With people, a common affirmative form is:
    ```eg
    誰もが知っている | everyone knows
    ```

    So the practical rule is deliberately narrower:

    **When forms such as 何も, 誰も, どこも and どれも carry the not-one,
    none reading, that reading is completed by a negative predicate.**

    Do not extend the formula mechanically to every question word plus も.
    Some combinations have their own established behavior. For example,
    いつも normally means always.

    The goal is to learn the productive pattern without turning it into a rule that
    predicts every expression containing 疑問詞 + も.

Two related patterns extend this system further: 一度も〜ない narrows the
same negative scope to counting, and 疑問詞＋でも develops the separate
"any answer is fine" reading introduced above.
