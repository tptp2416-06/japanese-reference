から marks where a span begins, and まで marks the endpoint it runs through.
Together they let you measure a stretch from end to end: hours on a clock, stops
on a route, or any other ordered range.

@@ tldr

`start + から + endpoint + まで` marks a whole span — from A through／to B. It
works for time, space and other ordered ranges, with まで including the endpoint.

@@ range — measuring a whole span, start to end

**Form:** `start + から + endpoint + まで + predicate`

- **Time becomes one stretch** — 9時から5時まで働く — work from nine to five
  — presents the working period from its starting point through its endpoint.
- **Space uses the same frame** — 東京から大阪まで行く — go from Tokyo to Osaka
  — traces the route between two places.
- **The endpoint belongs to the span** — 月曜日から金曜日まで — from Monday
  through Friday — includes Friday rather than stopping before it.
- **Other ordered ranges work too** — 1ページから10ページまで読む — read from
  page one through page ten — treats numbered pages as a continuous span.

```ex
月曜日[げつようび]から金曜日[きんようび]まで働[はたら]く。
en: I work from Monday through Friday.
note: Both endpoints are named, and Friday is included in the working span.
bd: 月曜日から*|from Monday / 金曜日まで*|through Friday / 働く|work
pat: start + から + endpoint + まで
```
```dialog
note: what licenses stating both ends — A asks for the whole schedule, so B answers with から〜まで.
A: 何時[なんじ]から何時[なんじ]まで開[あ]いてる？ | What hours are you open, start to finish?
B: 10時[じ]から7時[じ]まで開[あ]いてるよ。 | We're open from ten to seven.
```

### Either end can stand alone

Yes. 9時から働く — work from nine — names only the start; 5時まで働く — work
until five — names only the endpoint. Each particle has uses of its own, while
the paired frame is what explicitly measures the whole span end to end.

-> Prerequisite: [[kara#temporal-since]] — から alone, marking only where a span starts
-> Prerequisite: [[made#extent]] — まで alone, marking only the endpoint it reaches

Do not replace this durative まで with deadline までに. 5時まで働く — work until
five — continues through the interval; 5時までに終える — finish by five —
requires the result no later than that point and says nothing about filling the
interval.

-> Contrast: [[made-ni]] — a deadline that must be reached by, not a stretch filled up to
